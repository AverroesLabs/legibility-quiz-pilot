# Plan v4: calibrating an error-catching legibility audit

Written 2026-11-12. Approved by Jason the same day. This replaces plan v2 for new work. The v3 branch (`v3/openai-overnight-20261111`) is not used: no code, prompts or results from it count here (see `docs/decisions.md`).

## Goal of this phase

Find settings (reader model and budget, error templates, clarification prompt, review and grading prompts) under which a weak reader catches planted errors in a clarified copy of a paper and misses them in illegible copies of the same paper. Then freeze the settings and test them on fresh papers. This follows Jan Kirchner's suggestion: a legible paper is one where a reader would notice if the reasoning were wrong, not only one where it can answer questions.

The calibration grid, its key, the checks it names, the selection rule and the post-selection steps are in [`calibration-table.md`](calibration-table.md). That document is the design. This plan says what to run first.

## First experiments, in order

Each experiment gets its own script under `scripts/v4/`, writes to `data/v4/<experiment>/`, and is reported before the next one starts. Costs are estimates.

Look at the reviews: can each reader write a usable review, and does the strength order match our guess? Once errors exist, these reviews give the false-accusation baseline.
0b. **Contradiction inventory (about $3 to $5, added after experiment 0).** gpt-6.1-sol reads each original, `obf` and `compobf` of the three papers, judges every accusation the experiment 0 readers made against it (real or false), and lists any further real internal contradictions with exact quotes. Script `scripts/v4/inventory.py`, output `data/v4/inventory/`. Experiment 2 must plant errors away from the listed passages, and experiment 3 grades accusations of listed passages as neither catches nor false accusations. Clarified copies (step 1) get the same check. One copy is run first as a slice.

3. **Coarse grid (about $40).** Every surviving error in all four copies, five reader settings, 2 samples each. A gpt-5-mini grader decides whether each review caught the planted error. ## Experiment 5 preregistration: pointed check (committed 2026-11-12, pod pass 9, before any experiment 5 data)

From `docs/decisions.md` (pass 8, item 1): when a reader misses a planted error, is that because it did not find the passage (search) or because, looking straight at it, it does not judge it wrong (judging)? And does illegibility hurt finding, judging, or both?

- **Script:** [`scripts/v4/exp5_pointed.py`](../scripts/v4/exp5_pointed.py), output `data/v4/exp5-pointed/`.
- **Settings:** `luna-low` (gpt-5.6-luna low, the largest gap so far) and `mini-high` (gpt-5-mini high, the only setting at clarified 0.70). 1 sample, max 32,000 output tokens.
- **Errors and copies:** the 17 errors of experiment 4, planted in clarified, repaired `obf` and repaired `compobf` (the original is left out to save cost).
- **Pointed prompt:** the same system prompt as the review (`REVIEW_SYSTEM`), and a user prompt that gives the whole copy and then quotes one passage: "Check whether this passage is consistent with the rest of the paper". The quoted passage is the error's planted text in that copy (the `new` strings of its edits, joined by " ... "). Reply JSON: `{"consistent": true|false, "evidence": ..., "explanation": ...}`.
- **Control:** the same prompt on the unplanted clarified copy, quoting the same passage before planting (the `old` strings). It measures how often the reader calls a correct passage inconsistent when pointed at it.
- **Grading:** a reply with `consistent: false` is graded as a one-item review with experiment 3's grader and prompt (gpt-5-mini low, `GRADE_USER`); caught means the grader says it names the planted error for the right reason. `consistent: true`, unparsed or failed replies are not caught (failed and unparsed are counted and excluded from rates). Control: the share of replies saying `consistent: false` (any reason), and the share graded as the planted reason.
- **Measures, per setting:** pointed catch rate on clarified, on illegible (`obf` + `compobf` pooled), and the clarified-minus-illegible pointed gap (paired bootstrap over errors, 2000 resamples, seed 0). Main comparison: the pointed rate on (error, copy) pairs the reader **missed** in its unpointed reviews (experiment 3/3r for luna-low, experiment 4 for mini-high; an error-copy counts as missed if the reader missed it in a majority of its samples, ties counted as missed), against the rate on pairs it caught. Grader check: gpt-6.1-sol low regrades all `consistent: false` replies of the control and 20 random planted replies (seed 0); disagreements are reported.
- **Reading:** if pointed catch rates on clarified are high (at least 0.85) with control accusations low (at most 0.15), clarified misses are a search problem and a larger budget or a search-focused prompt could lift clarified rates. If pointed rates on missed errors stay low, those errors are judged consistent even when seen: they are too subtle or ambiguous for this reader, and the error set needs review. If the pointed gap between clarified and illegible is near 0 while the unpointed gap is not (luna-low: 0.21), illegibility acts on finding, not judging, which favours harder illegible copies that hide where evidence is (decisions pass 8, item 2). A pointed gap as large as the unpointed one means illegibility hurts judging.
- **Cost estimate:** about $13: 102 planted and 34 control calls at the experiment 4 per-review cost (luna about $0.08 to $0.11, mini-high about $0.08), plus grading under $0.5. One error first (`--slice`).

## Experiment 8 preregistration: a reader with a limited view (committed 2026-11-12, pod pass 13, before any experiment 8 data)

From Jason's note in `INBOX.md` (2026-11-12 ~22:30 UTC) and the report's diagnosis: whole-paper readers search for free, so planted errors measured search effort more than legibility. This reader cannot search: it reads once, in order, with a small memory.

- **Script:** [`scripts/v4/exp8_limited.py`](../scripts/v4/exp8_limited.py), output `data/v4/exp8-limited/`. `--chunks` writes the length table with no API calls.
- **The reader.** The copy is split at level-1 and level-2 headings (deeper headings stay in their section); a section over the variant's chunk cap is split at paragraph breaks, greedily, into chunks of at most the cap; a chunk under 60 words (title lines, a bare heading) joins the next one; a references section would be dropped (none of these copies has one). At each step the reader sees only the system prompt, "part k of n", its notes and the current chunk (`STEP_SYSTEM`, `STEP_USER`). It reports errors it can show now (a claim in the chunk that conflicts with its notes or the chunk, or a noted claim the chunk contradicts), quoting the claim and the conflicting content, and rewrites its notes. Notes over 1.1 times the cap are cut to that length (counted and reported). A step that fails or does not parse is retried once; if it fails again the notes carry over unchanged and the step is counted as failed. The findings of all steps form the review, in experiment 3's format, graded by experiment 3's grader (gpt-5-mini low, `exp3.grade`, unchanged). Max 16,000 output tokens per step.
- **Variants (at most 3, fixed now; no tuning of chunking or the notes prompt after any catch rate is seen):**
  - **A:** section chunks, cap 800 words; notes at most 300 words.
  - **B:** section chunks, cap 800 words; notes at most 150 words.
  - **C:** paragraph chunks: every section packed at paragraph breaks into chunks of at most 250 words; notes at most 300 words.
  A variant that fails for a technical reason is recorded in `run-log.md` and fixed as a new, named variant (A2 and so on), counted against the 3.
- **Chunk counts (from `--chunks`, before any call):** variant A and B: clarified 13 to 14, original 12 to 14, repaired `obf` 12 to 14, repaired `compobf` 9 to 13, `scatter` 12 to 14 per paper. Variant C: 20 to 30 for the full-length copies, 12 to 15 for `compobf`.
- **Copies and errors:** the 17 errors of experiment 4 in clarified, original, repaired `obf` and repaired `compobf`; the 13 set-6 errors in `scatter`. Rows from `data/v4/exp6-scatter/errors.jsonl`. 2 samples per copy. Clean copies: all 5 copies of the 3 papers, 2 samples per reader, each graded against every error of that paper (for `scatter`, the set-6 errors) for false accusations. gpt-6.1-sol low regrades 20 random planted reviews per variant (seed 0).
- **Readers and order (fixed by cost, not by results):** 1. variant A with luna-low (gpt-5.6-luna low) and mini-high (gpt-5-mini high); 2. variant B with mini-high; 3. variant C with mini-high; 4. variant A with mini-med (gpt-5-mini medium). Luna is charged at the Sol fallback rate in the ledger, so luna runs only on A. If the slice shows a step costs more than 1.5 times the estimate below, steps 2 to 4 are re-estimated and cut from the end of this list, not chosen by result.
- **Measures, per variant and reader:** catch rate on clarified, original, illegible (repaired `obf` + `compobf` pooled) and `scatter`; gaps clarified minus illegible and clarified minus `scatter` (paired bootstrap over errors, 2000 resamples, seed 0); false accusations (clean reviews, per copy and pooled). **Primary error set: the 12 errors of the three types other than "left out of the summary" (`omitted`).** The 5 `omitted` errors are reported separately, and all 17 together as secondary. Length: words, chunks, notes rewrites, notes cut, failed steps and cost per pass, per copy; reported as a finding, not tuned away.
- **Reading (the calibration grid's key and selection rule):** a variant **meets the bars** if, on the primary set, clarified at least 0.70, illegible at most 0.35 and false accusations at most 0.10 in every copy. It **comes close** if clarified at least 0.60, the clarified-minus-illegible gap at least 0.20 with its interval excluding 0, and pooled false accusations at most 0.15. The same reading with `scatter` in place of the illegible pair is reported as a second, separate test. Among variants that meet or come close, the largest gap is chosen (near-ties to the cheaper). Only then: the held-out `imp2` copies, once, at that variant and reader only, under a separate preregistration written before planting errors into `imp2`. If none meets or comes close, the result is reported as negative for this reader design and nothing further is run without Jason.
- **Cost estimate:** a step is about 1,500 input tokens and 700 output tokens for luna-low (about $0.03 at the fallback rate) or about 3,500 output tokens for mini-high (about $0.015). Variant A: about 2,500 steps per reader, so about $72 (luna-low) and $37 (mini-high); B about $33; C about $60; A with mini-med about $20; grading about $3. Total about $225 of the $377 left. Slice first: one `other_section` error in all 5 copies, both readers, 1 sample (about $3).

### Clean versions
