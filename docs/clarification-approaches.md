## What follows for our clarifier (inference, not measured)

1. **Repair inference gaps, not words** (4, 5, 7). The best-supported intervention is to make implicit links explicit: state the connecting step, repeat the referent instead of a pronoun or code, and put each claim next to its evidence. This is exactly what `obf-max` removes, so it is the natural decoder. Elaboration (7) keeps the original content and vocabulary and adds redundancy, which fits our fidelity rule better than simplification, which drops material.
2. **Expert readers may not gain, and may lose** (5, 6). Our readers are LLMs with strong background knowledge, and the audit's intended human reader is a researcher. Extra cohesion may add length without helping, and McNamara et al. found the benefit on recall and text-based questions differed from that on inference questions. Our planted errors are caught by checking claims against evidence, which is closer to text-base checking; so cohesion should help catching on `obf-max`, but on `orig` it may only lengthen the text. That is the result the 2×2 is built to show (`clar-orig` ≈ `orig`). A cohesion prompt that lengthens a lot is also a risk for a limited-view reader, which pays per step.
3. **Reading-grade targets are weak goals** (1, 2, 3). Formulas measure sentence and word length only. Writing to a grade makes sentences short, which can break a claim from its qualifier. We measure two grades as reported outcomes, and one prompt sets a target to test whether a grade target helps, but no prompt is judged by its grade (plan rule).
4. **Navigation aids work for both people and models** (1, 8, 9, 10): a summary up front, a glossary, definitions at the point of use, headings that say what a section shows, and pointers from questions or claims to the passage that answers them. For a limited-view reader, definitions at the point of use matter more than a glossary it may never page back to.
5. **Danger specific to the audit.** Any prompt that "spells out" reasoning can silently resolve, smooth over or expose an inconsistency, which would change catch rates for reasons other than clarity. Every prompt therefore keeps `base`'s fidelity rules verbatim, including "if the original is inconsistent, keep the inconsistency as it is". Clarifications are made from clean versions (errors come later), so this risk is checked properly only in the error stage.

## Prompt set to write next (step 4, second half)

Five prompts that differ in approach. Each keeps `base`'s fidelity rules block unchanged (same sections and order, every number, table, caveat, nothing added, inconsistencies kept, about 1.6× length cap except where stated) and changes only the "Goals" block. Texts are committed in the clarification script before any is run, and never edited after output is seen (plan rule).

| Name | Approach | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | Experiment 1's conservative prompt, unchanged (define terms at first use, state assumptions, evidence next to claims, short sentences). | control |
| `plain` | Plain language at a target Flesch-Kincaid grade of 12: active voice, strong verbs, one idea per sentence, everyday words for non-technical content, technical terms kept but defined. Grade is measured; the target is a goal, not a pass rule. | 1, 2, 7 (simplified) |
| `cohesion` | Repair every inference call: state each connecting step, repeat referents instead of pronouns or codes, put each claim next to its evidence with the numbers restated, add connectives (because, so, however). Length cap raised to 2×. | 4, 5, 7 (elaborated) |
| `explainer` | Structured explainer: a short summary at the top, a glossary, then the paper with a one-line "what this section shows" under each heading, a claim-to-evidence map at the end of each results section (claim → table/row/number), definitions repeated at point of use. Added material may only restate the paper. | 1, 8, 9, 10 |
| `twostage` | Two calls: first extract the argument (terms with definitions, claims, each claim's evidence and numbers, assumptions, caveats) as a structured list; then write the clarified paper from the original plus that list. Aimed at decoding `obf-max`, where codes and scattered evidence must be resolved before rewriting. | 4, 8 |
