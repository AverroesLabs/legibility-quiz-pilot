# Automated legibility tests and human-like readers: what exists and how well it was validated

Evidence-quality legend: [HUMAN-COMP] = validated against measured human comprehension (quiz, recall, verification accuracy); [HUMAN-RATING] = validated against human *ratings* of readability/clarity (perception, not comprehension); [AI-ONLY] = validated only against AI judges or not validated; [SNIPPET] = number seen only in a search snippet / secondary summary, not confirmed in the full text.

Overall headline: I found **no validated, working system that automatically certifies a technical/scientific paper as legible to humans**. The closest pieces are (a) LLM readability judges that correlate moderately with human *ratings* (r ~0.5-0.76) but not shown to track comprehension; (b) QA-based metrics (QAEval) and a calibrated LLM reader simulator (KnowledgeGain/LLMSim) that are the only methods shown to relate to measured human comprehension, and even these are weak at the per-document level (e.g. simulator-vs-human KnowledgeGain corr ~0.26; LLMSim "does not reliably predict topic-level KGain"); (c) prover-verifier "legibility" training at OpenAI, where small-model checkability tracked time-limited human checking on grade-school math only.

## 1. LLM-based readability / clarity judges for scientific & technical text (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
LLM judges beat classic formulas against human readability *ratings* (Pearson ~0.45-0.56 on 60 scientific plain-language summaries; r ~0.74-0.76 on the general-English CLEAR corpus; Kendall tau ~0.43-0.73 across science-flavoured datasets), but every one of these validations is against perceived difficulty, not comprehension. Where comprehension was measured directly, LLM-judge clarity scores were nearly uncorrelated with what readers learned (Spearman 0.089 in KnowledgeGain) and human subjective ratings failed to separate texts that produced different comprehension (Guo et al. 2025). This matches the pilot's finding that LLM legibility ratings and quiz scores diverge.

### Cited Findings
- **Cachola, Khashabi, Dredze, "Evaluating the Evaluators: Are readability metrics good measures of readability?" (EMNLP 2025).** Data: 60 summaries of 10 scientific papers (2 expert-written + 4 GPT-3 per paper, from August et al. 2024), 593 MTurk annotators rating *reading ease* on 1-5 (ratings, not comprehension). [HUMAN-RATING] — [arXiv 2508.19221](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19221v1)
  - Formulas vs. human ratings (Pearson / Kendall): DCRS 0.37/0.24, CLI 0.36/0.20, FRE 0.29/0.15, GFI 0.21/0.11, FKGL 0.16/0.08, Spache 0.13/0.04, ARI 0.10/0.02, Linsear Write -0.06/-0.03; 6 of 8 below 0.3 Pearson. — [arXiv 2508.19221](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19221v1)
  - LLM judges ("Own Reasoning" prompt, best of three prompts: Simple, ASCB guidelines, Own Reasoning): Mistral-7B 0.52/0.44, Mixtral 0.54/0.41, Gemma-7B 0.54/0.43, Llama-3.1-8B 0.45/0.34, Llama-3.3-70B 0.56/0.35. All beat every formula; even the weakest prompt beat FKGL. Authors say LMs better capture "required background knowledge". — [arXiv 2508.19221](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19221v1)
  - Recommendations: stop using FKGL for PLS evaluation; use DCRS/CLI plus LM judges as "additional metrics"; limitations: small dataset (60 summaries), LM bias, low interpretability. — [arXiv 2508.19221](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19221v1)
- **Belem, Glenn, Samuel, Kumar, Liu, "Readability Reconsidered: A Cross-Dataset Analysis of Reference-Free Metrics" (TSAR @ EMNLP 2025).** 5 datasets: Scientific Papers (August et al.; 180 query-focused summaries of 10 papers, Low/Med/High), CLEAR (1,000-excerpt subsample), ELI-Why GPT-4 (897) and Human (~117-123) explanations, ScienceQA (2,295 K-12 items). [HUMAN-RATING] — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1)
  - LLM judge = Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, temperature 0: 0-shot 3-way label (using the human annotator instructions), 5-shot, and a continuous 1-100 "teacher" prompt adapted from Trott & Riviere. Kendall tau (abs. values; sign flips are scale direction): 0-shot 0.57 SciPapers / 0.50 CLEAR / 0.49 ELI-GPT4 / 0.73 ELI-Human / 0.60 ScienceQA (avg rank 2.4); continuous 0.56/0.59/0.53/0.68/0.52 (avg rank 2.4). — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1)
  - Best traditional metric: "# Difficult Words" (0.26/0.40/0.45/0.46/0.48, avg rank 8.6); ARI best "psycholinguistic" (rank 11.0). Continuous judge saturates on ScienceQA (81.3% of scores on three values). — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1)
  - Annotator rationale analysis (90 ELI-Why items): "Wording/Terminology" most common justification, i.e. human readability *perceptions* themselves lean on surface vocabulary. — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1)
  - Internal inconsistencies flagged: abstract says 15 metrics vs appendix "20+"; abstract says four model-based metrics are top-four but Table 2 ranks ReadMe++ 6th; human-judgment count differs across versions (897 vs 1.2k). — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1); [TSAR listing](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.tsar-1.4)
- **Trott & Riviere, "Measuring and Modifying the Readability of English Texts with GPT-4" (TSAR 2024).** Zero-shot GPT-4 Turbo and GPT-4o-mini readability ratings vs CLEAR human estimates on 4,724 excerpts: r = 0.76 and 0.74; split-half reliability of CLEAR itself only r = 0.63. Pre-registered study (N = 59) found GPT-4-modified easier/harder versions of 100 excerpts were *judged* in the intended direction. Authors' own blog flags reliance on proxies for readability (judgments, not comprehension). General English, not technical. [HUMAN-RATING] — [arXiv 2410.14028](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14028v1); [Trott blog](https://seantrott.substack.com/p/modifying-readability-pt-2-the-human)
- **KnowledgeGain (Soos, Jiang, Wu 2026)** directly compared LLM-judge dimensions with measured human learning: Spearman with human normalized KGain — judge clarity 0.089, judge completeness -0.016, judge relevance 0.033, judge "expected KGain" 0.168, judge accuracy 0.291 (ROUGE-2 0.296, BLEU 0.267, BERTScore 0.232). Overall pointwise LLM-judge scores correlated with human mean ratings at r = 0.19. Human-rated clarity was flat between systems (4.08 vs 4.06) while comprehension differed. [HUMAN-COMP] — [arXiv 2605.31099](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31099)
- **Guo, Sohn, Leroy, Cohen, "Are LLM-generated plain language summaries truly understandable?" (arXiv 2505.10409, 2025).** 150 MTurk participants, 1,346 annotated PLS-abstract pairs (rigorous subset N = 459). Subjective Likert ratings showed LLM PLSs ~indistinguishable from human-written ones, yet MCQ comprehension accuracy was significantly higher for human-written PLSs. PLSs prompted to prioritise simplicity got the *lowest* simplicity ratings. Human subjective ratings did weakly predict comprehension in mixed models (standardised beta 0.037-0.048, all p<.05). [HUMAN-COMP] — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1) (numbers from the full PDF)
- **Medical/patient-education literature**: I found no study comparing an LLM readability judge to patient comprehension tests on the same materials. A JMIR AI letter argues improved readability scores do not establish improved patient understanding; authors reply conclusions are limited to readability and endorse PEMAT + direct comprehension testing. A gynecology study found LLM rewrites more concise but with more complex vocabulary; no LLM reached 6th-grade level; clarity/comprehension judged by two OB-GYNs, not patients. — [JMIR AI 2026 e107277](https://ai.jmir.org/2026/1/e107277); [PubMed 40578780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40578780/)

### Inferences
- Human readability *ratings* are themselves surface-driven (terminology dominates rationales) and decouple from comprehension (Guo; KnowledgeGain). So an LLM judge validated against ratings (r ~0.5-0.76) inherits that surface bias; this plausibly explains the pilot's result that LLM legibility ratings track surface features and disagree with quiz scores.
- No LLM readability judge has been validated on dense research papers (all data: summaries, explanations, K-12 science, literary/informational excerpts).

### Gaps
- No per-document correlation between an LLM readability judge and human comprehension on full technical papers found.
- Medical LLM-judge-vs-patient-comprehension validation: none found.

## 2. Comprehension-based (QA-based) measures, simulated readers, recall, cloze

### Takeaway
QA-based measures are the only automated family with evidence of tracking human comprehension, and the effect is small. In Guo et al., QAEval was the only one of 10 automatic metrics significantly associated with human MCQ comprehension on the rigorous subset (beta 0.039 per SD). KnowledgeGain's calibrated LLM reader simulator matches *aggregate* human Correct/Incorrect/IDK distributions well (KL 0.007-0.12) but is only weakly correlated with human results per topic (~0.26), and the authors explicitly call it a development proxy, not a predictor of per-article comprehension. No one has shown that an unconstrained LLM taking a quiz reproduces human per-document comprehension differences; the working simulator deliberately *handicaps* the LLM (memory bottleneck, personas, explicit "I don't know").

### Cited Findings
- **KnowledgeGain: Evaluating and Optimizing Science News Generation for Reader Learning (Soos, Jiang, Wu; arXiv 2605.31099, May 2026).** [HUMAN-COMP] — [arXiv 2605.31099](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31099)
  - Metric: pre/post quiz; 6 MCQs per source abstract, each with "I do not know" option; Bloom tiers (Q1-2 extractive T/F, Q3-4 extractive MC with distractors, Q5-6 inferential MC); questions must be answerable from source without outside knowledge; flawed items replaced; 180 human-study questions manually verified.
  - Human study 1: 30 STEM undergrads, 30 topics x 3 artifacts (abstract, ScienceAlert news, tweet), crossover design. Mean KGain (0-100): news 58.6 (+/-8.27, 168 s reading), abstract 54.0 (+/-8.04, 92 s), tweet 26.7 (+/-7.28, 36 s). News vs abstract not significantly different; both beat tweet; medium x phase interaction chi2(2) = 33.33, p = 5.8e-8.
  - LLMSim (simulated reader): 5 empirical personas (Heavy Abstainer 26.7%, Cautious Gist Reader 26.7%, Average Gist Reader 23.3%, Overconfident Misreader 13.3%, Confident Guesser 10%); **memory bottleneck** (writes a short memory trace and answers from it, not from the full text); explicit IDK; verbalised sampling over options; parameters tuned to minimise KL to human answer distributions. Alignment: KL pre 0.090 / news 0.021 / abstract 0.007 / tweet 0.119; correct-rate MAE 0.059-0.151.
  - Per-topic validity: authors state simulated and human normalized gains are "only weakly correlated"; Table 3 "KGain corr." = 0.263 for the full model (definition not fully clear); limitations: "LLMSim does not reliably predict topic-level KGain".
  - Human study 2 (20 topics, 640 participant-article observations): generator fine-tuned on KGain-filtered data vs strongest agentic baseline; human normalized gain 0.777 vs 0.719 (Wilcoxon p = .048; mixed model beta 0.060, p = .015); post-reading accuracy OR 1.30 (p = .013); raw gain n.s. (p = .183). Simulator g 0.378 vs 0.330 (same direction, but much lower absolute level than humans). Human pairwise preference 87.0% for proposed system.
  - Limitations: STEM undergrads only; KGain depends on the question set; risk of overfitting to generated questions/simulator; optimising for answerability may sacrifice nuance.
- **Guo et al. 2025 (PLS crowdsourced study).** Comprehension = 3 LLaMA-3-generated MCQs per summary + free-text recall after summary removed; between-subject (each participant saw one version). Mixed models with random intercepts for participant and abstract; automated metric as fixed effect predicting normalized MCQ accuracy. [HUMAN-COMP] — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1)
  - Rigorous subset (N = 459): QAEval beta 0.039 (p = .035, BH-corrected) = only significant metric; Coherence-bag 0.033 and Coherence-chn 0.032 (p = .053); GPT-PPL 0.014, BERTScore 0.011, BLEU 0.007, LENS 0.006, ROUGE -0.001, METEOR -0.010, SARI -0.016 (all n.s.). — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1)
  - Full set (N = 1,346): QAEval 0.040 (p<.001), Coherence-chn 0.034, BERTScore 0.028, Coherence-bag 0.024, ROUGE 0.017 significant; BLEU, LENS, GPT-PPL, SARI n.s. No readability formula or LLM-judge readability score was in the metric set. — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1)
  - QAEval here = generate QA pairs from the reference text and test whether a QA model extracts correct answers from the summary (reported as LERC scores). Note: this measures information *preservation* answerable by a model, closely analogous to the pilot's quiz. — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1)
  - Observation: participants with moderate topic familiarity outperformed self-identified experts; more time on task -> higher accuracy (reading effort is a confound for any human gold standard). — [arXiv 2505.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10409v1)
- **Related QA-based tools (not validated against human comprehension per document):** PlainQAFact (QA-based factuality for biomedical PLS; validated vs factuality labels); INFOLOSSQA (QA pairs surfacing what simplification omits; >400 pairs expert-judged). [HUMAN-RATING / expert labels] — [search result summary; CL4Health 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix-sig-urls/2025.cl4health-1.22.pdf); [arXiv 2401.16475](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16475)
- **Zero-shot lay summarisation with QA stage (arXiv 2501.05224):** lay participants answered fixed questions (problem, method, findings) from the lay summary only, on 20 articles; QA-stage summaries got fewer "disagree" votes. Small-scale. [HUMAN-COMP, small] — [arXiv 2501.05224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.05224)
- **LLM-simulated test takers for item difficulty (adjacent field):** Benedetto et al. (EMNLP Findings 2024) found LLM role-played "virtual pretesting" difficulty correlated with human pretest difficulty, but the prompt did not transfer across LLMs (strength not captured). Acquaye et al. (2026) on NAEP math: LLMs are poor *direct judges* of difficulty, while *simulation*-based approaches (simulated classrooms + IRT) are promising. A feature-based reading-comprehension difficulty model reached r = 0.77, vs prior 0.3-0.7. "Act Like a 5th Grader" (arXiv 2608.30033) targets bounding LLM knowledge in user simulators. [SNIPPET for most numbers] — [Benedetto et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.663.pdf); [arXiv 2601.09953](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09953); [arXiv 2608.30033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30033)

### Inferences
- The pilot's null result (weak models lose <=9 points on obfuscated text) is consistent with the literature: LLMs read from the full text with near-perfect working memory and no fatigue, so obfuscated prose barely hurts them. The only simulator that got close to humans (LLMSim) imposed a **memory bottleneck** (answer from a short gist trace), persona-based abstention, and calibration to human data; even then it matched aggregates, not per-document rankings. Directly actionable ideas: closed-book after reading, gist/summary-then-answer, token/time budgets, and calibrating against a small human panel.
- Direct "LLM-as-judge" of difficulty underperforms "LLM-as-simulated-test-taker" in the item-difficulty literature too (Acquaye et al.), echoing the pilot's split between ratings and quiz scores.
- Effect sizes for the best metric (QAEval beta ~0.04 per SD of normalized accuracy) are small; a per-paper pass/fail gate would have low reliability on this evidence.

### Gaps
- No validation found of cloze tests (human or LLM-administered) for scientific papers in 2023-2026; I did not find sources and could not confirm any.
- No study of QA-based legibility on full research papers (all evidence is on abstracts, news, PLSs).
- No study tested obfuscated vs clear versions of the same content with both humans and LLM readers (the exact pilot design).

## 3. Clarity / presentation scores in automated peer review

### Takeaway
Evidence that LLM reviewers' clarity/presentation scores agree with human reviewers is thin and mostly weak-to-moderate: ICC ~0.45-0.60 on abstract clarity (snippet), Pearson ~0.58-0.61 for *overall* scores on 64 ICLR papers, and one agent paper claiming "high" presentation correlation on 1,000 ICLR 2023 papers with numbers only in a figure. Worse for gating: presentation-only rewrites raise AI-reviewer scores in 75% of attempts (+1.21/10) without changing substance, so LLM "clarity" scores are an optimisation target, not a robust legibility measure. And human reviewers' presentation scores are not comprehension measures either.

### Cited Findings
- **Generative Agent Reviewers (GAR), Bougie & Watanabe (Woven by Toyota), arXiv 2412.10415 (preprint, Dec 2024).** 1,000 ICLR 2023 papers; human and LLM scores on soundness, presentation, contribution (1-4); Pearson correlations reported as "high", strongest on soundness and presentation, lower on contribution. Exact values appear only in Figure 4 (not in text). GAR's human-likeness was judged by GPT-4o. [HUMAN-RATING, numbers unverified; partly AI-ONLY] — [arXiv 2412.10415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10415)
- **"No Hidden Prompts Needed! You Can Game AI Peer Review with Presentation-Only Revisions" (Yang et al., arXiv 2606.13044, June 2026).** Revising only abstract/framing/related work/discussion with AI-reviewer feedback (methods, results, figures unchanged): 75.1% attack success, +1.21 mean score on 10-point scale across three mainstream AI reviewers; not explained by ordinary polishing; reviewers mistake the appearance of addressing a limitation for resolving it. Same paper (per snippet): AI reviewer scores vs historical human means on 64 ICLR 2024-25 papers Pearson r = .58-.61, Spearman .56-.58. [HUMAN-RATING; correlation SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2606.13044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13044)
- **Evaluating LLMs for abstract evaluation (arXiv 2601.19925, Jan 2026):** ChatGPT and Claude reached moderate agreement with human conference reviewers, ICC ~.45-.60 for composite, impression, clarity, objective and results; LLMs agree with each other more (ICC 0.59-0.87) than with humans. [HUMAN-RATING; SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2601.19925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19925v1)
- **Fine-grained ICLR/NeurIPS review study (arXiv 2604.19578):** clarity-related mentions in reviews show no significant relationship with overall score; LLM-assisted reviews devote less focus to clarity. [SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2604.19578](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19578)
- LLM review scores cluster near their mean, blurring accepted vs rejected papers (human scores more dispersed). [SNIPPET] — [CEUR-WS Vol-4130 paper110](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4130/paper110.pdf)
- 300 ICLR 2026 submissions, GPT-5.4 / Gemini 3.1 Pro / Claude Opus 4.6: broad decision alignment does not imply agreement on finer human judgments. [SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2608.03659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03659)
- "Verifying the Verifier" (NeurIPS 2026): agreement with human reviewers does not measure validity of LLM reviews; a multi-agent reviewer agreed less with humans but found more confirmed defects. [SNIPPET] — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/156456)
- **Agents4Science 2025** (AI-authored papers, AI reviewers): 315 submissions, 62 desk-rejected, three LLM reviewers scored 253 on soundness, significance, **clarity**, originality; top 79 also got human expert assessment. A secondary blog claims ~51% AI-human agreement (unverified). [design confirmed via search summary of official paper; agreement SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2511.15534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15534); [secondary analysis](https://sanand0.github.io/datastories/ai-agents-for-science)

### Inferences
- Agreement with human reviewers' presentation scores is at best moderate and is itself agreement with a perception-based score; given gameability (+1.21/10 from framing only), LLM clarity scores are unsuitable as a legibility gate for papers written by optimising AI researchers.

### Gaps
- No study found that reports a clean clarity-score-specific LLM-vs-human correlation on full papers with numbers in text; GAR's figures unreadable from text.
- No study links reviewer clarity scores (human or AI) to reader comprehension.

## 4. Systems or proposals that gate/audit publications on legibility automatically

### Takeaway
I found no deployed system that gates publication on validated human legibility. The nearest are (a) Agents4Science's LLM-reviewer clarity score used in acceptance decisions (not validated against comprehension), and (b) OpenAI's prover-verifier "checkability training", which operationalises legibility as "small verifier can check it" and showed transfer to time-limited human checkers, but only on grade-school math with ground-truth answers.

### Cited Findings
- **Kirchner, Chen et al. (OpenAI), "Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility of LLM outputs" (arXiv 2407.13692, 2024).** Helpful prover RL-trained on correctness + acceptance by a smaller verifier; sneaky prover trained to fool it. Human evaluation: contractors with a strict 45 s limit per solution judged validity. Optimising for correctness alone reduced human accuracy and speed; over checkability rounds human accuracy on helpful-prover solutions rose and time fell, while accuracy on sneaky solutions fell (flaws got subtler). "Legibility tax": legible prover less accurate than correctness-only prover. Authors found legibility to small verifiers tracked legibility to humans; flag the human study as a pilot with design flaws; domain limited to GSM-style math. [HUMAN-COMP (verification accuracy), narrow domain] — [arXiv 2407.13692](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13692v2)
- Agents4Science 2025 used three LLM reviewers' scores including clarity in the acceptance pipeline, with humans assessing only the top 79. — [arXiv 2511.15534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15534)
- Presentation-only revisions can game AI review (75.1% success), a direct threat to any LLM-judge-based gate. — [arXiv 2606.13044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13044)

### Inferences
- The prover-verifier result suggests a design principle relevant to the pilot: the weak "reader" must be weak enough that legibility actually matters to it (a time/compute-limited verifier), and the measure is task performance (checking/answering) rather than a rating. The pilot's weak readers apparently were not weak in the right way (full text, no budget), so obfuscation did not bind.
- An adversarially robust gate would likely need a sneaky-author red team (as in prover-verifier games) and periodic human calibration.

### Gaps
- No proposal found for auditing AI safety papers for human legibility specifically.

## 5. Machine-learned readability models beyond Flesch-Kincaid and transfer to technical text

### Takeaway
Supervised readability models (BERT on WeeBit/Newsela/OneStopEnglish/CLEAR, ReadMe++) work in-domain but degrade across domains and use incompatible grade scales; on science-flavoured data in 2025 they were matched or beaten by zero-shot LLM judges. None has been validated against comprehension of research papers.

### Cited Findings
- Martinc et al. (Computational Linguistics 2021): BERT raised SOTA on WeeBit by ~4% classification accuracy; also used Newsela and OneStopEnglish. — [CL 2021](https://preview.aclanthology.org/author-url/2021.cl-1.6)
- ReadMe++ (Naous et al.): states LMs struggle with out-of-domain data; multi-domain sentence-level dataset; models fine-tuned on it generalise better to unseen domains. — [arXiv 2305.14463](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14463v4)
- Cross-domain degradation: hybrid neural+linguistic models degrade less than pure neural ones cross-domain (Chinese data); benchmarks Newsela, OneStopEnglish, WeeBit use different grade scales, hampering comparison. — [ALTA 2022](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2022.alta-1.8.pdf)
- In Readability Reconsidered, ReadMe++ (readabert-en) Kendall tau 0.40 SciPapers / 0.45 CLEAR / 0.50 / 0.50 / 0.44 ScienceQA (avg rank 6.2); Meta Rater professionalism (ModernBERT) avg rank 4.2; Meta Rater readability nearly useless (avg rank 21.0; tau -0.17 to 0.14), all below the Llama-3.3-70B judges. — [arXiv 2510.15345](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15345v1)
- CLEAR's own human split-half reliability is r = 0.63, capping what any model can show against it. — [arXiv 2410.14028](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14028v1)

### Inferences
- Trained readability models measure perceived difficulty of mostly general/educational text; nothing suggests they would detect deliberate obfuscation of expert-level technical content, which is mainly a problem of structure, missing definitions and compressed reasoning rather than vocabulary.

### Gaps
- No found study training or testing ML readability models on research papers against comprehension outcomes.
- No CommonLit-trained model evaluated on scientific papers was found.
