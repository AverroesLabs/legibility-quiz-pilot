# Some Robustness Properties of Label Cleaning

Chen Cheng

John Duchi

###### Abstract

Aggregated labels combine information from noisy responses. They permit robustness—retention of prediction guarantees under noise or mis-specification—that is unavailable without cleaning. Minimizing a surrogate loss, an easier replacement for the task loss, gives stronger task-risk consistency guarantees than fitting raw labels. Here task risk means expected task loss, and consistency means convergence to an optimal task predictor. The surrogate is typically convex: its value at a mixture of scores does not exceed the corresponding mixture of loss values.

Finite-dimensional classifiers, which use finitely many parameters, remain consistent under loss mis-specification that defeats standard approaches to modeling uncertainty. Perfectly specified statistical models favor incorporating all information for efficiency, or statistical precision from the available data. Yet even slight loss mis-specification destroys standard consistency. Aggregation retains convergence to optimal classifiers. Thus collection, fitting, and prediction jointly admit robustness through refinement of noisy signals. The ranking separation in Propositions 1–2, the comparison in Theorem 1, and the model results in Section 4 establish these claims.

## 1 Introduction

Aggregation permits consistent optimal prediction using a surrogate rather than the task loss when unaggregated estimation cannot. This holds even for finite-dimensional statistical problems. Constructions using linear estimators—estimators whose scores depend linearly on their inputs—separate two kinds of procedures: those restricted to individual input–label pairs and those using aggregation. The examples cover ranking, which orders items; binary classification, which predicts one of two labels; and multiclass classification, which predicts among several labels. Propositions 1–2 give the ranking separation. Proposition 4 and Corollary 4.1 give a binary separation. Proposition 5 and Theorem 3 address multiclass models.

The nonparametric results, which do not restrict scores to a fixed finite-dimensional family, combine aggregation count with noise conditions. They give approximately linear comparison inequalities under weak surrogate conditions; Theorem 1 states the quantitative result. Theorem 2 extends consistency beyond unrestricted classes of measurable functions.

The conventional supervised pipeline collects \((X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n\), where \(X_i\) is input \(i\), \(Y_i\) is its label, and \(n\) is the number of observations. It then fits a model [17]. This description suppresses the cleaning, filtering, repeated crowdsourced labeling, and denoising required by modern datasets [9, 22, 39]. Crowdsourcing obtains labels from multiple contributors; cleaning and denoising refine those responses before fitting. Work on crowdsourced “gold standard,” or reference, labels [8, 48, 47, 46, 37] studies this machinery. The present concern is its fundamental, necessary improvement of fitted-model consistency.

Important contributions to surrogate-risk consistency theory date to the 2000s [52, 24, 40]. Bartlett, Jordan and McAuliffe [4] characterize convex-surrogate consistency for binary zero-one classification, where the task loss records whether a prediction is wrong. Later settings include multi-label classification, which predicts several labels for one input [50, 43, 16, 49, 1]; ranking [13, 14, 36]; structured prediction, whose outputs have internal structure [34, 6, 32]; ordinal regression, whose labels have an order [35]; restricted hypothesis classes, or restricted sets of candidate predictors [2, 3, 28, 29]; and general theory [40].

Pair-based analyses admit empirical-process theory, which controls differences between sample averages and population expectations [45, 5, 21, 4]. They do not include the aggregation machinery used to create contemporary datasets. The questions are therefore whether aggregation is needed, whether pair-based surrogate consistency suffices, and how aggregation improves consistency.

## 2 Preliminaries

We retain two regimes for the hypothesis class \(\mathcal F\), the set of candidate score functions. The first allows all Borel measurable functions: functions measurable for the sets generated by open sets. Its classical consistency notion is **Fisher consistency**, coded **Q7D**. It requires surrogate optimization to identify task-optimal predictions; Section 3 gives the precise conditions. The second regime uses a parametric class, indexed by model parameters, that may be mis-specified: the assumed model need not describe the data correctly. Aggregation improves consistency in both regimes.

Let
\[
(X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n\sim P
\]
be independent and identically distributed, abbreviated **i.i.d.**, on the input and output spaces \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y\). Thus each pair has the same joint distribution \(P\), and the pairs are independent. Write \((X,Y)\) for a population pair. The conditional distribution \(P(\cdot\mid X=x)\) describes labels at input \(x\).

A score function is \(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d\), where \(\mathbb R^d\) is the space of \(d\)-dimensional real vectors and \(d\geq1\). A decoder \(\mathsf d:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal Y\) converts scores to a prediction:
\[
\widehat y=\mathsf d\circ f(x).
\]
The symbol \(\circ\) denotes function composition, so this expression means \(\mathsf d(f(x))\).

We use \(\mathbb E_P\) for expectation under \(P\), \(\mathbb P\) for probability, and \(\mathbb R_+\) for nonnegative real numbers. The indicator \(1\{B\}\) equals one when statement \(B\) holds and zero otherwise. An infimum, written \(\inf\), is the greatest lower bound; \(\operatorname{argmin}\) and \(\operatorname{argmax}\) denote minimizing and maximizing arguments. Where a prediction must be a single label, a tied argument is selected as specified.

The distribution-dependent task criterion is
\[
R(f)\coloneqq
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\ell(\mathsf d\circ f(X),P(\cdot\mid X))\right].
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\coloneqq\) defines the expression on its left, and \(\ell\) is the task loss. Its minimizers frequently coincide with the original problem (2). Allowing the loss to depend on the conditional distribution permits greater sophistication.

For example, let \(\mathcal Y=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), where \(k\) is the number of labels. We also write \([k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}\). With
\[
\ell(\widehat y,P)=\sum_y P(Y=y)1\{\widehat y\neq y\},
\]
the criterion is the standard zero-one error rate.

The instantaneous formulation uses a loss
\(\ell:\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Y\to\mathbb R_+\) on a prediction and an observed label. It minimizes, over \(f\in\mathcal F\),
\[
R(f):=\mathbb E_P[\ell(\mathsf d\circ f(X),Y)].
\tag{1}
\]
For binary classification, \(d=1\), the decoder is the sign function
\(\mathsf d(s)=\mathsf{sgn}(s)\), and
\[
\ell(y,y')=1\{yy'\leq0\},\qquad
R(f)=\mathbb P(Yf(X)\leq0).
\]

Task losses may be nonsmooth, meaning that derivatives need not exist, or nonconvex. They may also give little information for optimization. The zero-one loss is flat almost everywhere. This precludes even practical first-order optimization, which uses gradients or related slope information.

An easier surrogate loss
\(\varphi:\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal Y\to\mathbb R\) replaces the task loss:
\[
R_\varphi(f):=\mathbb E_P[\varphi(f(X),Y)].
\]
This replacement requires consistency with the task criterion.

### 2.1 Label aggregation

An aggregation procedure replaces an ordinary label by information obtained from a response \(Z\in\mathcal Z\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be the space of aggregated outputs, let \(A:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal A\) be the aggregation map, and let
\(\varphi:\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal A\to\mathbb R_+\) be a nonnegative surrogate. The **aggregated surrogate risk**, coded **P9S**, is
\[
R_{\varphi,A}(f):=\mathbb E[\varphi(f(X),A(Z))].
\tag{3}
\]
We assess its minimization against the actual task risk, rather than against a task defined by the replacement response.

Repeated labeling uses
\[
Z=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)\in\mathcal Y^m,\qquad
Y_i\mid X=x\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}P_{Y\mid X=x}.
\]
Thus \(m\) is the number of labels collected independently at the same input, conditional on that input. Empirical task minimization gives
\[
A(Z)=A(\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\})
=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_{l=1}^m\ell(y,Y_l).
\]
For \(\ell(\widehat y,y)=1\{y\neq\widehat y\}\), this is **majority vote**, coded **T4K**. General \(\ell\) permits more abstract aggregation procedures. The notation \(A_m(Z)\) records the repetition count. Mao et al. [28] use a similar multilabeled structure for multilabel losses, rather than for this aggregation pipeline.

A rough \(K\)-nearest-neighbor construction instead combines a label with labels at nearby inputs. It supplies \(X\) with \(Y\) and an additional independent sample
\((X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^m\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}P\), so
\(Z=(Y,(X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^m)\). A metric
\(\operatorname{dist}:\mathcal X\times\mathcal X\to\mathbb R_+\) measures input distance. Order the sampled inputs by
\[
\operatorname{dist}(X,X_{(1)})\leq\cdots\leq
\operatorname{dist}(X,X_{(m)}),\qquad X_{(0)}=X.
\]
The label \(Y_{(l)}\) accompanies \(X_{(l)}\), including the original pair at index \(0\). For \(K\geq0\), take
\[
A_{m,K}(Z)\coloneqq
\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_{l=0}^K\ell(y,Y_{(l)}).
\]
The population-level treatment using repeated samples also extends to aggregation from one sample \((X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n\); see Appendix C.

## 3 Surrogate consistency

The standard framework [40] takes \(\mathcal F\) to contain all Borel measurable \(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d\). For a score vector \(s\in\mathbb R^d\), define the conditional risks, their excesses above their best values, and the optimal population risks:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R(s\mid x)&\coloneqq
\ell(\mathsf d\circ s,P(Y\in\cdot\mid X=x)),\\
R_\varphi(s\mid x)&:=\mathbb E[\varphi(s,Y)\mid X=x],\\
\delta_\ell(s,x)&\coloneqq
R(s\mid x)-\inf_{s'\in\mathbb R^d}R(s'\mid x),\\
\delta_\varphi(s,x)&\coloneqq
R_\varphi(s\mid x)-\inf_{s'\in\mathbb R^d}R_\varphi(s'\mid x),\\
R^\star&:=\inf_{f\in\mathcal F}R(f),\qquad
R_\varphi^\star:=\inf_{f\in\mathcal F}R_\varphi(f).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\mathsf d\circ s\) means decoding the score \(s\). The quantities \(\delta_\ell\) and \(\delta_\varphi\) are conditional excess risks. Likewise, \(R(f)-R^\star\) and \(R_\varphi(f)-R_\varphi^\star\) are population excess risks, also called regrets.

For all distributions \(P\), the framework considers two requirements [40, 4, 52]:

(i) Every sequence \(f_n\in\mathcal F\) with
\(R_\varphi(f_n)\to R_\varphi^\star\) satisfies
\(R(f_n)\to R^\star\).

(ii) A nondecreasing function \(\psi:\mathbb R_+\to\mathbb R_+\), positive at every \(\epsilon>0\), satisfies
\[
\psi(R(f)-R^\star)\leq R_\varphi(f)-R_\varphi^\star
\quad\text{for every }f\in\mathcal F.
\]
Nondecreasing means that increasing the argument cannot decrease the function.

Requirement (ii), the **uniform comparison inequality**, coded **R2V**, is stronger than (i) and need not hold. The two coincide for convex margin-based binary surrogates [4]. A margin-based binary surrogate depends on the product of the label and its scalar score.

The canonical **calibration function**, coded **H8N**, measures the least surrogate excess compatible with a given task excess. Its pointwise and uniform forms are
\[
\psi(\epsilon,x)\coloneqq
\inf_{s\in\mathbb R^d}
\{\delta_\varphi(s,x)\mid\delta_\ell(s,x)\geq\epsilon\},
\qquad
\overline\psi(\epsilon)\coloneqq
\inf_{x\in\mathcal X}\psi(\epsilon,x).
\]
Thus \(\epsilon>0\) is a required task-excess level. For a function \(h\), its Fenchel biconjugate \(h^{**}\) is its closed convex lower envelope, the greatest lower-semicontinuous convex function below it. Lower semicontinuity excludes downward jumps at a limit point.

###### Corollary 3.1.

Positivity of \(\psi(\epsilon,x)\) for all \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(\epsilon>0\) is equivalent to \(\varphi\) being Q7D for \(\ell\). If \(\psi\) is the Fenchel biconjugate of \(\overline\psi\), then
\[
\overline\psi(\epsilon)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\psi(\epsilon)>0,
\qquad
\psi(R(f)-R^\star)\leq R_\varphi(f)-R_\varphi^\star
\]
for every measurable \(f\).

The equivalence and inequality follow from Zhang [51, Prop. 25] and Steinwart [40, Thm. 2.8 and Lemma 2.9].

A Q7D surrogate for the general task would make single pairs sufficient for consistent estimation. It would simplify collection and admit the full empirical-process toolkit. Such pair-only results are generally impossible, as the ranking example below shows.

### 3.1 Q7D failure without aggregation: ranking

Pairwise-comparison ranking [20, 15, 14, 30] uses
\[
\mathcal Y=\{(i,j):i\neq j,\ 1\leq i,j\leq k\},
\]
where \(k\) is the number of items and \((i,j)\) says that item \(i\) outranks item \(j\). For each input \(x\), population aggregation transforms the conditional comparison probabilities
\(p_{ij}=P(Y=(i,j)\mid x)\) into an ordering.

A simple comparison scheme [20, cf.] defines
\[
(C_x)_{ii}=0,\qquad
(C_x)_{ij}=\frac{p_{ij}}{\sum_{l\neq j}p_{lj}}
\quad(i\neq j),\qquad
0/0=1/(k-1).
\]
The matrix \(C_x\in\mathbb R_+^{k\times k}\) is stochastic: its entries are nonnegative and its columns sum to one. In symbols,
\(C_x^T\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), where \(T\), or \(\top\), denotes transpose and \(\mathbf1\) is the all-ones vector. The vector \(C_x\mathbf1\) measures preference frequency for each item. Higher matrix powers \(C_x^p\mathbf1\), Perron vectors associated with the dominant eigenvalue [20], and more sophisticated schemes admit similar negative results.

Absorb decoding into the loss. Let \(e_i\) be the coordinate vector whose \(i\)th entry is one and whose other entries are zero. Define
\[
\ell(s,C)\coloneqq
\max_{i<j}1\!\left\{
(s_i-s_j)(e_i-e_j)^TC\mathbf1\leq0,\ 
(e_i-e_j)^TC\mathbf1\neq0
\right\}.
\]
This loss penalizes disagreement between the score ordering and the matrix ordering. The resulting risk is
\[
R(f)\coloneqq
\mathbb P\!\left(f(X)\text{ and }C_X\mathbf1
\text{ order differently}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Restricting scores to \(s^T\mathbf1=0\), meaning that their entries sum to zero, is minor. This restriction is familiar in multiclass classification [51, 43] and natural when only ordering is decoded.

###### Proposition 1.

For this ranking task with \(k\geq3\) outcomes, every
\(\varphi:\mathbb R^k\times\mathcal Y\to\mathbb R\) convex in its first argument fails Q7D.

The proof is in Appendix A.1.

For the positive result, collect independent comparisons
\(Z=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)\) conditional on \(X\). Define
\[
m_{ij}=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y^m}1\{y=(i,j)\},
\qquad m_j=\sum_{i=1}^k m_{ij}.
\]
Let \(\star\) be a special aggregation output used when some \(m_j\) is zero. The aggregation rule is
\[
A(Z)=
\begin{cases}
\star,&m_j=0\text{ for some }j\in[k],\\[2mm]
\left(
\frac{m_{i1}}{m_1}+\frac{m_{i2}}{m_2}+\cdots+
\frac{m_{ik}}{m_k}
\right)_{i\in[k]},
&m_j>0\text{ for all }j\in[k].
\end{cases}
\]
Its non-\(\star\) output is a vector in \(\mathbb R^k\). Regression fits these vectors directly.

###### Proposition 2.

For score and target vectors \(s,q\in\mathbb R^k\), set
\[
\varphi(s,q)=\|s-q\|_2^2,\qquad \varphi(s,\star)=0.
\]
Here \(\|u\|_2\) is the Euclidean norm, the square root of the sum of squared coordinates of \(u\). With the aggregation specified above and \(m\geq k\), \(\varphi\) is Q7D for the ranking risk.

The proof is in Appendix A.2. The response space expands from \(\mathcal Y\) to \(\mathbb R^k\). That expansion is unavailable in the convex single-comparison formulation and separates Propositions 1 and 2. Regression on ordering frequencies gives the positive result.

### 3.2 Aggregation and stronger surrogate consistency

Aggregation extends ordinary surrogate-risk theory and upgrades nominally inconsistent losses. The extensions below first give a general calibration result. They then use an **identifying surrogate**, coded **W3C**, whose parameters distinguish the correct label; Definition 3.1 states that condition precisely.

#### 3.2.1 Basic extensions

For an arbitrary aggregation map \(A:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal A\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\varphi,A}(s\mid x)&\coloneqq
\mathbb E[\varphi(s,A(Z))\mid X=x],\\
\delta_{\varphi,A}(s,x)&:=
R_{\varphi,A}(s\mid x)-
\inf_{s\in\mathbb R^d}R_{\varphi,A}(s\mid x).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the aggregated conditional surrogate risk and its excess. Write
\(R_{\varphi,A}^\star=\inf_{f\in\mathcal F}R_{\varphi,A}(f)\) for the optimal aggregated surrogate risk. The corresponding calibration quantities are
\[
\overline\psi_A(\epsilon,x)\coloneqq
\inf_{s\in\mathbb R^d}
\{\delta_{\varphi,A}(s,x)\mid\delta_\ell(s,x)\geq\epsilon\},
\qquad
\overline\psi_A(\epsilon)\coloneqq
\inf_{s\in\mathbb R^d}\overline\psi_A(\epsilon,x).
\tag{5}
\]

###### Proposition 3.

Suppose a nonnegative function \(b:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R_+\) is integrable and bounds the conditional task excess:
\[
\int b(x)\,dP(x)<\infty,\qquad
\delta_\ell(f(x),x)\leq b(x).
\]
The integral is its expectation under the input distribution. Then \(\varphi\) is Q7D for the task risk exactly when
\(\overline\psi_A(\epsilon,x)>0\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(\epsilon>0\). With the biconjugate
\(\psi_A=(\overline\psi_A)^{**}\),
\[
\psi_A(R(f)-R^\star)
\leq R_{\varphi,A}(f)-R_{\varphi,A}^\star.
\]

Appropriate measurability conditions are tacit and noncentral. This result is a near-corollary of Steinwart [40, Thm. 2.8]; Appendix B.1 supplies a proof for completeness. It preserves classical guarantees. By itself, it does not identify a particular improvement from aggregation.

#### 3.2.2 W3C and consistency

The regime here has a finite label space:
\(\operatorname{card}(\mathcal Y)=k<\infty\), where
\(\operatorname{card}\), also written \(|\mathcal Y|\), denotes the number of elements. The instantaneous task risk is
\(R(f)=\mathbb E[\ell(f(X),Y)]\).

The ranking construction belongs to this regime. There, \(k\) was ranking length and
\(\operatorname{card}(\mathcal Y)=k(k-1)\). Henceforth \(k\) denotes label-space cardinality, with this slight abuse of notation.

For a parameter \(a_y\in\mathcal A\) associated with each label \(y\), define **generalized majority vote**, coded **J9P**, by empirical task minimization followed by parameter substitution:
\[
A_m(y_1,\ldots,y_m)\coloneqq a_{\hat y},
\qquad
\hat y=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\sum_{i=1}^m\ell(y,y_i).
\tag{7}
\]
Tie-breaking is arbitrary.

Let
\[
y^\star(x)\coloneqq
\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\mathbb E[\ell(y,Y)\mid X=x]
\]
be the set of population-optimal labels at \(x\). For i.i.d. \(Y_i\) and finite \(\mathcal Y\), a random finite count \(M<\infty\) necessarily exists such that every \(m\geq M\) satisfies
\[
\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^m\ell(y,Y_i)\right\}
\subset
y^\star(x).
\]
Thus empirical task minimization eventually selects only population-optimal labels. The strategy supplies asymptotic consistency under minimal identifiability, meaning that surrogate parameters can distinguish the labels, for any such task loss.

###### Definition 3.1.

A surrogate \(\varphi:\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal A\to\mathbb R\) is an **Identifying surrogate**, coded **W3C**, with constants
\(0<\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}\leq\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}<\infty\), when there exist parameters
\(\{a_y\}_{y\in\mathcal Y}\subset\mathcal A\) and score vectors
\(\{s_y\}_{y\in\mathcal Y}\subset\mathbb R^d\) such that
\(\mathsf d(s_y)=y\) and, for all \(y\neq y'\),
\[
\varphi(s_y,a_y)+\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}
\leq\inf_{\mathsf d\circ s\neq y}\varphi(s,a_y),
\tag{6a}
\]
\[
\varphi(s_y,a_{y'})-\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}
\leq\inf_{s\in\mathbb R^d}\varphi(s,a_{y'}).
\tag{6b}
\]

Inequality (6a) separates the score \(s_y\) from all scores that decode incorrectly at parameter \(a_y\). It therefore makes a minimizer at \(a_y\) identify \(y\). A finite lower bound on \(\varphi(\cdot,a)\) supplies a finite \(\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}\) for (6b).

The condition imposes neither convexity nor unaggregated consistency with \(\mathcal A=\mathcal Y\), \(Z=Y\). It also imposes no decoder requirement such as choosing the largest score coordinate by pointwise \(\operatorname{argmax}\).

###### Corollary 3.2.

For \(m=m(n)\to\infty\) and W3C \(\varphi\),
\[
R_{\varphi,A_m}(f_n)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(f_n)-R^\star\to0.
\]
Thus vanishing aggregated surrogate excess gives vanishing task excess when the aggregation count grows.

For example, binary hinge loss is
\(\varphi(s,a)=\max\{1-sa,0\}\) on
\(\mathcal A=\mathcal Y=\{\pm1\}\). Use \(a_y=s_y=y\). Then
\[
\varphi(s_1,a_1)=\varphi(s_{-1},a_{-1})=0,\qquad
\inf_{sa\leq0}\varphi(s,a)=1,
\]
\[
\varphi(s_1,a_{-1})=\varphi(s_{-1},a_1)=2.
\]
These values give W3C constants \((1,2)\).

#### 3.2.3 W3C and consistency amplification

Low noise permits a comparison written as
\[
\psi(R(f)-R^\star)\leq R_\varphi(f)-R^\star,
\]
with \(\psi\) linear over some range of positive \(\epsilon\). Linear growth is the strongest possible [34, 32]. Strict comparison requirements may exclude practically convenient inconsistent surrogates [23, 34, 32]. Following Osokin et al. [34], we use a relaxation that requires linear growth only above a threshold.

###### Definition 3.2.

The surrogate and aggregator have **level-\((\xi,\zeta)\) consistency**, coded **U6A**, when some \(\psi\) satisfies
\[
\psi(\epsilon)\geq\zeta\epsilon\quad(\epsilon\geq\xi),
\qquad
\psi(R(f)-R^\star)\leq
R_{\varphi,A}(f)-R_{\varphi,A}^\star.
\]
Here \(\xi\) is the task-excess threshold and \(\zeta\) is the required linear slope.

Only risks above the threshold require linear growth. This retains a “good enough” criterion. Minimal assumptions permit levels \((o_m(1),\zeta)\) even for a non-Q7D surrogate; \(o_m(1)\) denotes a quantity tending to zero as \(m\to\infty\).

We next define the noise quantities used in the theorem. The **minimal excess conditional risk**, coded **F8Q**, is the incorrect-prediction gap
\[
\Delta(x)\coloneqq
\min_{\mathsf d(s)\notin y^\star(x)}
\delta_\ell(s,x).
\tag{8}
\]
It equals \(|2P(Y=1\mid X=x)-1|\) in binary classification. Estimation is expected to be harder when this gap is nearer zero.

Adapting classical assumptions [25], also Bartlett et al. [4], use the **Mammen-Tsybakov noise conditions**, coded **N6Y**:
\[
\mathbb P(\mathsf d\circ f\neq\mathsf d\circ f^\star)
\leq
\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}(R(f)-R^\star)^\alpha
\quad\text{for all measurable }f,
\tag{Nα}
\]
\[
\mathbb P(\Delta(X)\leq\epsilon)
\leq(\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}\epsilon)^\beta
\quad(\epsilon>0).
\tag{Mβ}
\]
Here \(f^\star\) denotes a task-optimal predictor. The first probability measures disagreement between decoded predictions. The positive constant \(\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}\) may change between conditions. The noise exponents satisfy
\(\alpha\in[0,1]\) and \(\beta\in[0,\infty]\). Both conditions hold trivially at \(\alpha=\beta=0\). As in the binary result [4, Thm. 3], they are equivalent under
\(\beta=\alpha/(1-\alpha)\); see Appendix B.2.

Mao et al. [29] use exactly the same noise statistic for multiclass \(H\)-consistency. Here \(H\) denotes a restricted hypothesis class, and \(H\)-consistency compares task and surrogate excess risks within that class. Their result imposes surrogate and hypothesis-class structure to obtain a global convex lower bound.

With minimal structure, we instead require the **noise condition number**, coded **B2Z**:
\[
\kappa(x)\coloneqq
\frac{\max_{\mathsf d(s)\neq y^\star(x)}\delta_\ell(s,x)}
{\min_{\mathsf d(s)\neq y^\star(x)}\delta_\ell(s,x)}.
\tag{9}
\]
This ratio compares the largest and smallest conditional excesses among incorrect predictions. For \(\mathsf d(s)\neq y^\star(x)\),
\[
\Delta(x)\geq\delta_\ell(s,x)/\kappa(x).
\]
Binary classification has \(\kappa(x)=1\) provided
\(\mathbb P(\Delta(X)>0)=1\).

The auxiliary **error function**, coded **V5R**, is
\[
e_m(t)\coloneqq
t\sqrt{\frac2m
\log\!\left(
\frac{4k(\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}+\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})}
{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\right)}.
\tag{10}
\]
The logarithm \(\log\) is natural. With \(t=\kappa(x)\), this quantity expresses likely correctness of \(A_m\) when it is much smaller than one. In the theorem, \(M>0\) is a cutoff for the random condition number \(\kappa(X)\), distinct from the random eventual count \(M\) in Section 3.2.2.

###### Theorem 1.

Let \(\varphi\) be
\((\mathsf C_{\varphi,1},\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})\)-W3C with parameters
\(\{a_y\}_{y\in\mathcal Y}\), and let \(A_m\) be the J9P rule. Assume \(0\leq\ell\leq1\) and condition (Nα), with \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). For any \(M>0\) and \(f\in\mathcal F\) satisfying
\[
R(f)-R^\star\geq
2\mathbb P(\kappa(X)>M)+
(4\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}e_m(M))^{\frac1{1-\alpha}},
\]
one has
\[
R(f)-R^\star\leq
\frac{16}{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\bigl(R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star\bigr).
\]

The proof is in Appendix B.3. The theorem gives level-\((\xi,\zeta)\) consistency with
\[
\xi=2\mathbb P(\kappa(X)>M)+
(4\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}e_m(M))^{\frac1{1-\alpha}},
\qquad
\zeta=\frac{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}{16}.
\]
Define the optimized threshold
\[
\xi_m\coloneqq
\inf_M\left\{
2\mathbb P(\kappa(X)>M)+
(4\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}e_m(M))^{\frac1{1-\alpha}}
\right\}.
\]
Then \(\xi_m\to0\). The connecting steps are that
\(\mathbb P(\kappa(X)>M)\to0\) as \(M\uparrow\infty\), while
\(e_m(M)\to0\) with \(m\) for each fixed \(M\). Taking \(\alpha=0\) yields asymptotic consistency.

The crude bound \(\kappa(x)\leq1/\Delta(x)\), choosing \(M=1\) for \(\operatorname{card}(\mathcal Y)=k=2\) and optimizing \(M\) otherwise, lower bounds the promised \(\xi_{m,k}\) by setting
\[
\xi_{m,k}\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
\left(
\frac{32\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^2}{m}
\log\!\left(
\frac{8(\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}+\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})}
{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\right)
\right)^{\frac1{2(1-\alpha)}},
&k=2,\\[3mm]
4\cdot\left(
\frac{32\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^4}{m}
\log\!\left(
\frac{4k(\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}+\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})}
{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\right)
\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2(1-\alpha^2)}},
&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Appendix B.4 gives the algebraic substitutions and manipulations.

###### Corollary 3.3.

Under the theorem’s conditions, every \(f\) with
\(R(f)-R^\star\geq\xi_{m,k}\) satisfies
\[
R(f)-R^\star\leq
\frac{16}{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\bigl(R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star\bigr).
\]

For fixed \(f\), the comparison requires fewer aggregated samples as \(\alpha\) approaches \(1\). At \(\alpha=1\),
\[
m\geq
32\max\{\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^2,\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^4\}
\log\!\left(
\frac{4k(\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}+\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})}
{\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}}
\right)
=O(\log k)
\]
gives \(\xi_{m,k}=0\) and linear R2V. The notation \(O(\log k)\) means growth bounded by a constant times \(\log k\). Thus lower-noise problems require less aggregation for stronger consistency.

Theorem 1's bounds are coarse because the identifiability assumptions are weak. Tighter relationships between \(\ell\) and \(\varphi\), making J9P correct or sufficiently correct for \(\varphi\), should strengthen the bounds. Near-consistency without aggregation could also connect smaller initial inconsistency to better dependence on \(m\). These refinements are not pursued.

### 3.3 Surrogate consistency with T4K

The following majority-vote examples assume noise exponent \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). This loses no generality because \(\alpha=0\) always satisfies (Nα). Appendix B.5 proves each result.

#### 3.3.1 Binary classification, nonsmooth surrogate

Consider the binary margin surrogate
\[
\varphi(f(x),y)=\phi(yf(x)).
\]
Assume \(\phi\) is convex,
\(\partial\phi(0)\subset(-\infty,0)\), and
\(\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)=0\). Here \(\partial\phi(0)\) is the set of subgradients at zero: slopes of supporting lines below the convex function.

###### Lemma 3.1.

For every \(\delta>0\), \(\varphi\) is
\((\mathsf C_{\varphi,1},\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})\)-feasible, with the stated feasibility constants
\[
\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}=\phi(0)-\phi(\delta)>0,
\qquad
\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}=\phi(-\delta).
\]
Consequently, for \(k=2\) and \(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\),
\[
R(f)-R^\star\geq
\left(
\frac{32\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^2}{m}
\log\!\left(
\frac{8(\phi(-\delta)+\phi(0)-\phi(\delta))}
{\phi(0)-\phi(\delta)}
\right)
\right)^{\frac1{2(1-\alpha)}}
\]
implies
\[
R(f)-R^\star\leq
\frac{16}{\phi(0)-\phi(\delta)}
\bigl(R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star\bigr).
\]

For convex binary margin surrogates, Q7D and R2V over all measurable functions hold exactly when \(\phi'(0)<0\), where the prime denotes the derivative [4]. Aggregation can make an otherwise inconsistent \(\phi\) consistent. This is a somewhat trivial thought experiment, not a surrogate one would use.

#### 3.3.2 Bipartite matching

For a structured output space, an embedding
\(v:\mathcal Y\to\mathbb R^d\) represents each label's structural information as a vector [33]. Use the decoder
\[
\mathsf d(s)=\operatorname{argmax}_{y\in\mathcal Y}\langle s,v(y)\rangle,
\]
where \(\langle u,v\rangle\) is the ordinary inner product, the sum of coordinatewise products. For a task loss
\(\ell:\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Y\to\mathbb R_+\) satisfying \(\ell(y,y)=0\), the **maximum-margin surrogate**, coded **A3J**, is
\[
\varphi(s,y)=
\max_{\hat y\in\mathcal Y}
\left(\ell(\hat y,y)+
\langle v(\hat y)-v(y),s\rangle\right).
\tag{11}
\]
This is also called generalized hinge loss or structured hinge loss. It originates in [42, 44, 19]. Without aggregation it is generally inconsistent, except in certain low-noise cases [34, 32].

For the matching instance, let \(G=(V,E)\) be a graph with vertex set \(V\) and edge set \(E\). Its vertices split into
\[
V=V_1\cup V_2,\qquad
V_1=\{1,\ldots,N\},\qquad
V_2=\{N+1,\ldots,2N\}.
\]
Each of the \(N\) edges joins exactly one unique node on each side. Such a bipartite matching pairs the two sets of vertices. All matchings form \(\mathcal Y\), so
\(k=\operatorname{card}(\mathcal Y)=N!\), where \(N!\) is the factorial of \(N\).

The edge-index embedding and mistaken-edge loss are
\[
v(G)\coloneqq
(1\{(u,v)\in E\})_{u\in V_1,v\in V_2}
\in\mathbb R^{N^2},\qquad d=N^2,
\]
\[
\ell(y_1,y_2)\coloneqq
\frac1{2N}\|v(y_1)-v(y_2)\|_1
=\frac1{2N}\|v(y_1)-v(y_2)\|_2^2.
\]
Here \(\|u\|_1\) is the sum of the absolute values of the coordinates of \(u\).

###### Lemma 3.2.

On \(2N\) vertices, the matching surrogate is W3C with
\[
\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}=\frac1N,\qquad
\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}=2.
\]
Despite factorial label cardinality, aggregation yields consistency once its count exceeds \(O(N\log N)\). For \(k\geq3\), measurable
\(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d\) obeying
\[
R(f)-R^\star\geq
4\cdot\left(
\frac{32\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^4}{m}
\log(4k(2N+1))
\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2(1-\alpha^2)}}
\]
satisfy
\[
R(f)-R^\star\leq
16N\bigl(R_{\varphi,A_n}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_n}^\star\bigr).
\]

The label count \(k=N!\), the constants above, and the displayed comparison give the stated matching result; its proof is in Appendix B.5.

#### 3.3.3 Structured prediction

Assume the general structured decoder can select every class strictly. That is, for each \(y\), the score set
\[
\mathcal S(y)\coloneqq
\{s:\langle v(y),s\rangle>
\langle v(\hat y),s\rangle
\text{ for }\hat y\neq y\}
\]
is nonempty. Define its identifiability-gap quantity
\[
\tau(y)\coloneqq
\inf_{s\in\mathcal S(y)}
\max_{y_+,y_-\neq y}
\frac{\ell(y_+,y)}
{\langle v(y)-v(y_+),s\rangle}
\cdot
\frac{\langle v(y)-v(y_-),s\rangle}
{\ell(y_-,y)}.
\]
Here \(y_+\) and \(y_-\) range over labels different from \(y\).

###### Lemma 3.3.

A3J is
\((\mathsf C_{\varphi,1},\mathsf C_{\varphi,2})\)-W3C with
\[
\mathsf C_{\varphi,1}=
\min_{\hat y\neq y}\ell(\hat y,y),
\qquad
\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}=
\max_{y\in\mathcal Y}\tau(y)+1.
\]
In particular, if the embedding has only zero and one coordinates and the loss is
\[
v(y)\in\{0,1\}^d,\qquad
\ell(\hat y,y)=\frac1{2d}\|v(\hat y)-v(y)\|_1,
\]
then \(\tau(y)=1\) for every \(y\) and
\(\mathsf C_{\varphi,2}=2\).

Nontrivial consistency holds once
\(m\geq\log\operatorname{card}(\mathcal Y)\), as in the binary matching case. With \(0\leq\ell(\cdot,\cdot)\leq1\), every measurable
\(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfying
\[
R(f)-R^\star\geq
\left(
\frac{32\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}^4}{m}
\log\!\left(
4|\mathcal Y|
\left(1+
\frac{\max_{\hat y\in\mathcal Y}\tau(y)+1}
{\min_{\hat y\neq y}\ell(\hat y,y)}
\right)
\right)
\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2(1-\alpha^2)}}
\]
also satisfies
\[
R(f)-R^\star\leq
\frac8{\min_{\hat y\neq y}\ell(\hat y,y)}
\bigl(R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star\bigr).
\]
Appendix B.5 proves the lemma and comparison.

## 4 Robustness and consistency for models

We now replace unrestricted measurable functions by a restricted, often parametric class \(\mathcal F\). Well-specification means that the assumed statistical model correctly describes the data. It makes consistency straightforward, but is brittle and unrealistic. Surrogate consistency on subsets of measurable functions is a long-standing topic [12, 31].

Recent \(H\)-consistency bounds connect excess target risk and surrogate regret within a regret hypothesis class \(H\) in diverse settings [2, 3, 26, 28, 29]. Those results are primarily surrogate-centric: they focus on the surrogate's properties. The aggregation contribution is orthogonal to them and less dependent on a particular class and surrogate.

### 4.1 Binary consistency failure in finite dimensions

The binary setup uses
\[
\mathcal Y=\{\pm1\},\qquad
\ell(\mathsf d(s),y)=1\{ys\leq0\},\qquad
\varphi(s,y)=\phi(sy),
\]
where \(\phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R_+\) is convex. Let \(\mathcal X=\mathbb R^d\) and use linear scores
\[
\mathcal F=
\{f_\theta:f_\theta(x)=\langle\theta,x\rangle\}_{\theta\in\mathbb R^d}.
\]
Here \(\theta\) is the parameter vector.

The condition **optimal predictability**, coded **K7M**, requires that \(\mathcal F\) contain an optimal sign classifier: some \(f\in\mathcal F\) satisfies
\[
\mathsf{sgn}(f(x))=
\mathsf{sgn}(P(Y=1\mid X=x)-1/2).
\tag{12}
\]
For a vector \(\theta^\star\) realizing this condition,
\[
\mathsf{sgn}(\langle\theta^\star,x\rangle)
=
\mathsf{sgn}(P(Y=1\mid x)-\tfrac12).
\]
Then \(f_{\theta^\star}\) minimizes \(R\) over every measurable function. We say that \(P\) has K7M along \(\theta^\star\).

Even including such an optimal classifier does not preserve classical consistency. Proposition 4 gives both a strict task-risk failure and an arbitrarily small absolute cosine with an optimal direction.

###### Proposition 4.

For every \(\epsilon>0\) and nonzero
\(\theta^\star\in\mathbb R^d\), some distribution \(P\) on \((X,Y)\) satisfies K7M along \(\theta^\star\), yet every
\[
\theta_\varphi\in
\mathop{\rm argmin}_\theta R_\varphi(f_\theta)
=
\mathop{\rm argmin}_\theta
\mathbb E[\phi(f_\theta(X),Y)]
\]
has
\[
R(f_{\theta_\varphi})>R(f_{\theta^\star}),
\qquad
|\cos\angle(\theta_\varphi,\theta^\star)|
=
\frac{|\langle\theta_\varphi,\theta^\star\rangle|}
{\|\theta_\varphi\|_2\|\theta^\star\|_2}
\leq\epsilon.
\]
Here \(\angle(u,v)\) is the angle between vectors \(u\) and \(v\); a small absolute cosine means a direction close to perpendicular. This essential orthogonality occurs for any nonnegative loss \(\phi\).

The proof is in Appendix D.1.

To state aggregation recovery, define an approximate minimizer. For a function \(g\) and tolerance \(\epsilon\),
\[
\operatorname{\epsilon\textup{-argmin}}g
=
\operatorname{\epsilon\textup{-argmin}}_\theta g(\theta)
\coloneqq
\{\theta:g(\theta)\leq\inf_\theta g(\theta)+\epsilon\}.
\]
Thus its objective value is within \(\epsilon\) of the infimum.

With independent repeated labels \(Z=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)\) conditional on the input and T4K \(A_m\), consider
\[
\theta_m\in
\operatorname{\epsilon_m\textup{-argmin}}_\theta
R_{\varphi,A_m}(f_\theta)
=
\operatorname{\epsilon_m\textup{-argmin}}_\theta
\mathbb E[
\phi(A_m(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)f_\theta(X))].
\]

###### Corollary 4.1.

As a corollary to Theorem 2, if \(P\) satisfies K7M along
\(\theta^\star\) and \(\epsilon_m\to0\) as \(m\to\infty\), these aggregated approximate minimizers satisfy
\[
R(f_{\theta_m})\to R(f_{\theta^\star}),
\qquad
\cos\angle(\theta_m,\theta^\star)\to1.
\]

Thus a linear restriction can make unaggregated surrogate minimization arbitrarily incorrect, as Proposition 4 states. Aggregation retains asymptotic consistency and recovers the direction, as Corollary 4.1 states.

### 4.2 Aggregation and restricted classes

Use multiclass labels \(\mathcal Y=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), zero-one loss
\(\ell(y,y')=1\{y\neq y'\}\), and decoder
\[
\mathsf d(s)=\operatorname{argmax}_{y\in[k]}s_y.
\]
The class \(\mathcal F\) has zero-sum scores and is a cone, meaning that it is closed under multiplication by a positive scalar:
\[
f(x)^T\mathbf1=0,\qquad
f\in\mathcal F,\ t>0\ \Longrightarrow\ tf\in\mathcal F.
\]
The normalization does not affect decoding.

Multiclass K7M requires an \(f\in\mathcal F\),
\(f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^k\), such that
\[
\operatorname{argmax}_y f_y(x)
\in\operatorname{argmax}_yP(Y=y\mid X=x)
\quad\text{for every }x.
\]
The population maximizing label
\(y^\star(x)=\operatorname{argmax}_yP(Y=y\mid x)\) is unique.

Assume the surrogate
\(\varphi:\mathbb R^k\times[k]\to\mathbb R_+\) is nonnegative and Q7D, and that
\[
\varphi(s,y)\to0
\quad\text{if}\quad
s_y-s_j\to+\infty\text{ for every }j\neq y.
\tag{13}
\]
In words, its loss tends to zero when the correct class's score exceeds every competing score by an increasing amount. The response space is
\(\mathcal Z=\mathcal Y^m\), and aggregation is T4K.

###### Theorem 2.

Let a cone \(\mathcal F\) satisfy K7M for \(P\), and assume
\(P(\Delta(X)>0)=1\). For \(\epsilon_m\geq0\),
\(\epsilon_m\to0\), every sequence
\[
f_m\in
\operatorname{\epsilon_m\textup{-argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal F}
R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)
=
\operatorname{\epsilon_m\textup{-argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal F}
\mathbb E[
\varphi(f(X),A_m(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m))]
\]
satisfies \(R(f_m)\to R^\star\).

The proof is in Appendix D.2. This broad cone result requires aggregation. Without it, even binary subclasses containing an optimal predictor can have arbitrarily uninformative surrogates, as Proposition 4 shows.

Examples of Q7D losses satisfying (13) are multiclass logistic loss
\[
\varphi(s,y)=\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^k e^{s_j-s_y}\right)
\]
and
\[
\varphi(s,y)=\sum_{i\neq y}\phi(s_y-s_i)
\]
for convex, nonincreasing \(\phi\) with \(\phi'(0)<0\) and
\(\inf_t\phi(t)=0\). Nonincreasing means that larger arguments cannot increase \(\phi\). The notation \(e^u=\exp(u)\) denotes the exponential function. Zhang [51, Thm. 8] establishes consistency of these losses over
\[
\mathcal F=\{f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^k:\mathbf1^Tf=0\}.
\]

Binary margin predictors correspond to
\(f(x)=(g(x),-g(x))\) for measurable \(g\). For
\(\mathcal X=\mathbb R^d\), linear scores
\[
f(x)=(\langle\theta_1,x\rangle,\ldots,\langle\theta_k,x\rangle),
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i=0,
\]
form a convex cone: they are closed under positive scaling and convex combinations. Without aggregation, such examples typically require strong structure such as convexity. The minimal limiting condition (13) permits aggregated asymptotic consistency.

### 4.3 Finite-dimensional multiclass classification

We compare pair-based fitting with majority-vote fitting when the true conditional probabilities need not have a logistic link. A link converts score vectors into label probabilities. **Ordering consistency** will require its most probable classes to agree with its largest scores.

Let \(\mathcal X=\mathbb R^d\), \(\mathcal Y=[k]\), and
\[
\Theta=[\theta_1~\cdots~\theta_{k-1}]
\in\mathbb R^{d\times(k-1)}.
\]
The columns \(\theta_i\) are class parameters; class \(k\) is the reference class. Write
\(Y\mid X=x\sim\mathsf{Cat}(p_\Theta(x))\), where
\(\mathsf{Cat}\) is the categorical distribution that assigns each label its stated probability. The probability vector satisfies
\(p_\Theta(x)\in\mathbb R_+^k\), \(\mathbf1^\top p_\Theta(x)=1\), and
\[
p_\Theta(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sigma(\langle\theta_1,x\rangle,\cdots,\langle\theta_{k-1},x\rangle)\\
1-\mathbf1^\top
\sigma(\langle\theta_1,x\rangle,\cdots,\langle\theta_{k-1},x\rangle)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The link \(\sigma:\mathbb R^{k-1}\to\mathbb R_+^{k-1}\) has
\(\mathbf1^\top\sigma\leq1\). For a score vector \(t\), put
\(t_k=0\) and \(\sigma_k(t)=1-\mathbf1^\top\sigma(t)\). Impose ordering consistency:
\[
t_y=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}t_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma_y(t)=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\sigma_i(t).
\tag{14}
\]

Multiclass logistic regression uses the link
\[
\sigma_i^{\rm lr}(t)=
\frac{e^{t_i}}{1+e^{t_1}+\cdots+e^{t_{k-1}}},
\]
where the superscript \({\rm lr}\) identifies logistic regression. This probability transformation is also called softmax, with the reference-class score fixed at zero.

Let \(\Theta^\star\) denote the true parameter matrix. The multilabeled dataset is
\[
\{(X_i;(Y_{1i},\cdots,Y_{im}))\}_{i=1}^n,
\]
with
\[
Y_{ij}\mid X_i\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}
\mathsf{Cat}(p_{\Theta^\star}(X_i)).
\]
The arbitrarily tie-broken majority-vote label
\(Y_i^+=A_m(Y_{i1},\cdots,Y_{im})\) obeys
\[
Y_i^+\mid X_i\sim\mathsf{Cat}(p_{\Theta^\star,m}(X_i)).
\]
Let \(\rho_m(t)\) be the T4K distribution for \(m\) labels with initial probabilities \(\sigma(t)\in\mathbb R_+^{k-1}\). Then
\[
p_{\Theta,m}(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\rho_m(\langle\theta_1,x\rangle,\cdots,\langle\theta_{k-1},x\rangle)\\
1-\mathbf1^\top
\rho_m(\langle\theta_1,x\rangle,\cdots,\langle\theta_{k-1},x\rangle)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The link \(\rho_m\) satisfies ordering consistency.

We fit logistic loss even when \(\sigma\) is not logistic. With
\(\theta_k=\mathbf0\), the zero vector, the fitted loss is
\[
\varphi(\Theta^\top x,y)=
-\langle\theta_y,x\rangle+
\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}
\exp(\langle\theta_i,x\rangle)\right).
\tag{15}
\]
For the population loss
\(L_m(\Theta)=\mathbb E[\varphi(\Theta^\top X,Y_m^+)]\), differentiation gives
\[
\nabla_{\theta_i}\varphi(\Theta^\top x,y)=
-x(1\{y=i\}-\sigma_i^{\rm lr}(\Theta^\top x)).
\]
Here \(\nabla_{\theta_i}\) is the gradient, the vector of partial derivatives with respect to \(\theta_i\). Its minimizer \(\Theta_m\) therefore satisfies
\[
\nabla_\Theta L_m(\Theta_m)=
\mathbb E\!\left[
X\left(
\sigma^{\rm lr}(\Theta_m^\top X)
-\rho_m({\Theta^\star}^\top X)
\right)^\top
\right]=0.
\]

Well-specified unaggregated models have standard statistical consistency and efficiency. The binary case also has a robustness property absent from the multiclass result below. For \(m\geq1\), \(k=2\), and
\(X\sim\mathsf N(0,I_d)\), Cheng et al. [7, Prop. 3] obtain
\(\Theta_m=t_m\Theta^\star\) for some \(t_m>0\), even with a mis-specified link. The notation \(\mathsf N(0,I_d)\) means a normal distribution with mean zero and covariance equal to the \(d\)-dimensional identity matrix \(I_d\). Thus the direction
\(\Theta^\star/\|\Theta^\star\|_2\) suffices for consistent binary classification at every aggregation level.

To state the multiclass failure, fix
\(\mathcal T_\epsilon\subset\mathbb R^{k-1}\) of Lebesgue measure \(\epsilon\), where Lebesgue measure is ordinary Euclidean volume. Define the corrupted link
\[
\sigma^\epsilon(t)=
\sigma^{\rm lr}(t)1\{t\notin\mathcal T_\epsilon\}
+\frac1k\mathbf1\,1\{t\in\mathcal T_\epsilon\}.
\]
The conditional distribution on \(Y\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) samples
\(Y\sim\sigma^{\rm lr}(t)\) if \(t\in\mathcal T_\epsilon\) and uniformly otherwise. The link satisfies ordering consistency and K7M.

For \(\epsilon>0\), define
\[
L_{m,\epsilon}(\Theta)\coloneqq
\mathbb E[
\mathbb E_{\sigma^\epsilon}[
\varphi(\Theta^\top X,Y_m^+)\mid X]],
\qquad
\Theta_m(\epsilon)=
\operatorname{argmin}_\Theta L_{m,\epsilon}(\Theta).
\]
The inner expectation uses the corrupted link. This is the population logistic loss for \(m\)-label T4K when
\(Y\mid X=x\sim\sigma^\epsilon({\Theta^\star}^\top x)\).
An unsubscripted \(\|\cdot\|\) denotes a norm.

###### Proposition 5.

Let \(k\geq3\), \(\Sigma=I\), where \(I\) is the identity matrix. For
\(Z\sim\mathsf N(0,I_{k-1})\), assume invertibility of
\[
M\mapsto DM\coloneqq
\mathbb E\!\left[
Z\bigl(
\nabla\sigma^{\rm lr}({\Theta^\star}^TZ)MZ
\bigr)^T
\right].
\]
Here \(Z\) is a normal random vector, not the aggregation response used earlier. The matrix \(M\) is the argument of the linear map \(D\), and
\(\nabla\sigma^{\rm lr}\) is the Jacobian matrix of first derivatives of the link. Invertibility means that the map has an inverse.

Some \(\epsilon_0>0\) then has the property that every
\(\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0)\) admits \(\mathcal T_\epsilon\) of Lebesgue measure at most \(\epsilon\) with
\[
\Theta_1(\epsilon)/\|\Theta_1(\epsilon)\|
\neq
\Theta^\star/\|\Theta^\star\|.
\]

The proof is in Appendix E.1. Although \(\Theta_1(0)=\Theta^\star\), arbitrarily small positive corruption destroys unaggregated consistency.

For recovery under a general ordering-consistent link \(\sigma\), define
\[
L_{m,\sigma}(\Theta)=
\mathbb E[
\mathbb E_\sigma[
\varphi(\Theta^\top X,Y_m^+)\mid X]],
\qquad
\Theta_m=\operatorname{argmin}L_{m,\sigma}(\Theta).
\]

###### Theorem 3.

Suppose \(\Theta^\star=U^\star T^\star\), with orthogonal
\(U^\star\in\mathbb R^{d\times(k-1)}\) and nonsingular
\(T^\star\in\mathbb R^{(k-1)\times(k-1)}\). Orthogonality here means that the columns of \(U^\star\) are perpendicular unit vectors. Nonsingularity means that the square matrix \(T^\star\) is invertible.

For each \(m\) there exists
\(T_m\in\mathbb R^{(k-1)\times(k-1)}\) with
\(\Theta_m=U^\star T_m\), and
\[
\|T_m\|\to\infty,\qquad
T_m/\|T_m\|\to T^\star/\|T^\star\|
\quad(m\to\infty).
\]

The proof is in Appendix E.2.

Pair-based surrogate risks can therefore be inconsistent even with K7M and a linear model correct except on an \(\epsilon\)-fraction of data. Proposition 5 gives the normalized-parameter failure. T4K recovers the optimal linear predictor asymptotically, as Theorem 3 states. For \(k\geq3\), arbitrarily slight true-link mis-specification defeats risk consistency. Aggregation requires no true-link assumption beyond ordering consistency.

These results give robustness under mis-specification whenever some link describes the \(X\)–\(Y\) relationship. Without aggregation, multiclass methods are generally inconsistent.

## 5 Numerical illustrations

Two experiments corroborate the theory: nonparametric amplification and finite-dimensional multiclass asymptotic consistency under link mis-specification.

##### Surrogate consistency amplification for D4X.

The **truncated quadratic loss**, coded **D4X**, is the binary margin loss
\[
\varphi(f(x),y)=\phi(yf(x)),\qquad
\phi(\delta)=\max\{1-\delta,0\}^2.
\]
Its exact unaggregated H8N calibration function is
\(\psi(\epsilon)=\epsilon^2\) [4, Example 2].

Take \(\delta=1\) in the binary lemma. Impose no low-noise condition: use \(\alpha=0\) and
\(\mathsf C_{\mathsf{MT}}=1\). The resulting aggregated comparison curve is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\psi_m(\epsilon)
&=
\frac{\phi(0)-\phi(\delta)}{16}
\left(
\epsilon-
\sqrt{\frac{32}{m}
\log\!\left(
\frac{8(\phi(-\delta)+\phi(0)-\phi(\delta))}
{\phi(0)-\phi(\delta)}
\right)}
\right)_+\\
&=\frac1{16}
\left(\epsilon-\sqrt{\frac{32}{m}\log40}\right)_+.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \((u)_+=\max\{u,0\}\) is the positive part. The parallel comparisons are
\[
\psi(R(f)-R^\star)\leq R_\varphi(f)-R_\varphi^\star,
\qquad
\psi_m(R(f)-R^\star)\leq
R_{\varphi,A_m}(f)-R_{\varphi,A_m}^\star.
\]

The aggregated curve changes from quadratic toward linear as
\(m\to\infty\), upgrading consistency. Figure 1 compares the optimal unaggregated curve with the conservative curves supplied by the general theorem.

\widetilde\sigma(t)=

## 6 Discussion and future work

Cheng, Asi and Duchi [7] discuss the collection debate. Using all unaggregated labels appears statistically more efficient when models can represent all uncertainty. Otherwise, cleaning appears more robust. Dorner and Hardt [11] favor more noisy rather than cleaned labels for validation comparisons of binary classifiers—that is, comparisons using data to assess the classifiers. The present results establish fundamental limitations of uncleaned supervised-label information and robustness gains from cleaning.

##### Finite \(m\) and fundamental limits.

Many consistency results require \(m\to\infty\). Reliance on this limit remains unsatisfying. Ranking admits explicit finite-\(m\) guarantees: Proposition 2 requires \(m\geq k\). A theory depending jointly on the task loss \(\ell\), surrogate \(\varphi\), and available count \(m\) remains open.

Second- or third-order item comparisons, including powers \(C_x^p\) suggested by Keener [20], may require increasing counts. Precisely separating problems that require cleaning and aggregation from those that do not is a central challenge and a natural, important direction.

##### Fundamental limits of B2Z.

Beyond binary classification, the noise condition number \(\kappa(X)\) may remain large even with low Mammen-Tsybakov noise, \(\alpha\approx1\). Binary problems trivially have \(\kappa(X)=1\). The minimal surrogate assumptions produce this behavior.

Closer task–surrogate connections could better represent difficulty. Explicit failure constructions and sharper fundamental limits could improve understanding of predictive-model fitting.

##### Mis-specified models.

The mis-specified-model results require K7M: \(\mathcal F\) must contain a Bayes-optimal classifier, meaning a classifier with the least task risk among all measurable predictors. Classical surrogate consistency can fail even under this requirement, while aggregation succeeds.

Beyond this restriction, Nguyen et al. [31] for binary classification and Duchi et al. [12] for multiclass classification study joint inference of predictor \(f\) and a representation of input \(x\). They show that this joint inference forces a task-loss-dependent surrogate form. Their decision rules remain infinitely powerful rather than restricted to finite-dimensional classes. New approaches are needed.

##### Restricted-class comparison with aggregation.

The nonparametric comparison inequalities mitigate regret induced by the surrogate. The parametric results establish statistical consistency. Recent \(H\)-consistency work [2, 28, 27, 29] suggests extending aggregation comparisons to restricted classes.

Mao et al. [27] establish rate-optimal quadratic \(\psi\) growth for smooth convex loss, where smoothness provides differentiability and rate-optimality means that the quadratic growth rate cannot be improved in that setting. Aggregation-based upgrades in \(\mathcal H\)-consistency, with \(\mathcal H\) denoting the restricted hypothesis class, remain of interest. An extension could unify the parametric and nonparametric advantages and characterize how hypothesis-class “size” affects aggregation.
