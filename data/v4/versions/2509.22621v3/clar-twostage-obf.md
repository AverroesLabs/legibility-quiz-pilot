# IA2: Alignment with ICL Activations

Improves Supervised Fine-Tuning

###### Abstract

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT; code Q7M) adapts model weights using labeled examples. The baseline H9C uses SFT only. ICL Activation Alignment (IA2; code R8D) first primes a model by aligning its internal activations with those produced during in-context learning (V2K). Activations are intermediate outputs inside the model. The sequential method P4Z applies IA2 and then SFT.

Across 12 popular benchmarks and two model families, P4Z significantly improves output accuracy (U6R) and calibration—the agreement between confidence and correctness. These results provide practically useful adaptation and a conceptual window into its internal mechanics (Tables 1, 2, and 6, all rows; statistical-significance test: §C). R8D is self-distillation: it trains a model using signals from that same model. It aims to replicate V2K activation patterns in weight-trained models, incentivizing V2K-like internal reasoning. Its priming signal is unavailable through H9C, as supported by the weight-space analysis in §5.2 and Figure 4.

## 1 Introduction

Generic large language models (LLMs) serve specialized applications. For example, a news aggregator may classify articles into predefined categories. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques adapt a model through small sets of parameters rather than all its weights (Hu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022). These small parameter sets can often be efficiently loaded into and removed from graphics processing unit (GPU) memory.

Generalization through Q7M—performing well beyond the training examples—typically requires many labeled samples (Le Scao and Rush, 2021). Collecting those samples can be expensive. V2K instead supplies demonstrations and instructions in the model’s input context. These consume context capacity, increase computation for each query, and displace tokens available for queries and responses. Tokens are the units of text processed and generated by the model.

Our research question is whether information-rich V2K activations can improve Q7M quality. Our hypothesis is that these activations contain information about extracting generalizable contextual patterns. That information may be absent from few-shot weight adaptation, which trains on a small number of examples. Such adaptation may learn shortcuts—patterns that fit the training examples but fail on new data—or overfit the target responses.

Similar behavior in generated tokens need not imply similar internal processing. Divergent activations indicate distinct adaptation mechanisms, consistent with Shen et al. (2024). Our activation comparison in §3 and Figure 2 examines this distinction.

Context-to-weight distillation transfers the effects of an input context into model weights. Methods that use response-text signals (Snell et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024b) can inherit the same output-training problems. Reproducing a context-conditioned model’s outputs does not ensure reproducing its functioning.

**Figure 1:** Standard Q7M enforces output-space agreement between response and target—the third step of the proposed pipeline—and produces subpar performance and miscalibration under low data. The pipeline collects rich V2K activations, enforces functional alignment—agreement in internal processing—then trains against target responses. Its comparison charts aggregate performance across models and datasets; details: §5.

## 2 Background and Notation

For a task $\mathcal T$, let $\mathcal D_{\mathcal T}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be a dataset containing $N$ input–output pairs. Here, $i$ indexes examples, $X_i$ is an input, and $Y_i$ is its ground-truth response.

Write the model as $M_\Theta$, where $\Theta$ is its set of weights. Labeled weight adaptation, Q7M, changes $\Theta\rightarrow\Theta'$. For a query $X_t$, where $t$ identifies the query, the adapted model produces $M_{\Theta'}(X_t)=\bar y_t$. The lowercase $\bar y_t$ is the first token of the generated response $\bar Y_t$. Repeated application to the growing sequence extends that response.

For a ground-truth response $Y_i$ containing $G$ tokens, generate $G$ tokens $\bar Y_i$. Minimize cross-entropy, the token-prediction loss that penalizes low probability on the correct token, across all generated tokens:

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{G}
\operatorname{cross\text{-}entropy}(\bar Y_{ij},Y_{ij}).
\tag{2}
\]

Here, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ is the SFT loss, $j$ indexes response tokens, and the sums add the losses over examples and tokens. The terms $\bar Y_{ij}$ and $Y_{ij}$ refer to generated and ground-truth tokens at position $j$ for example $i$.

The language model (LM) $M_\Theta$ is a decoder-only transformer: it predicts the next token from the preceding sequence. It stacks self-attention (SA) and linear layers. Self-attention combines information from available sequence tokens; linear layers apply learned matrix projections.

Let $T=[t_i]_{i=1}^{R}$ contain $R$ token vectors, each of width $d$. At every token and layer, the model applies SA and linear projections. The final-layer projection at the last token passes through the LM head, the output component that predicts the most likely next token.

The attention weights $W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O\in\Theta$ are the query, key, value, and output projection matrices. We collectively write them as $W_{QKVO}$. Attention is

\[
\operatorname{SA}(T;W_{QKVO})
=Z=
\left[
z_i=\sigma\left(\frac{q_iK^T}{\sqrt d}\right)V\cdot W_O
\right]_{i=1}^{R},
\tag{1}
\]

where $q_i=t_iW_Q$ is the query vector, $K=t_{:i}W_K$ contains key vectors, and $V=t_{:i}W_V$ contains value vectors. The notation $t_{:i}$ means the first $i$ tokens. The superscript $T$ on $K^T$ means matrix transpose, $\sqrt d$ is the square root of the vector width, and $\cdot$ denotes multiplication. The function $\sigma$ is Softmax, which converts scores into normalized weights. The output is $Z=[z_i]_{i=1}^{R}$. Both $Z$ and $T$ have shape $R\times d$.

SA depends on other sequence tokens. The behavioral changes studied here arise from varying either the context $T$ or the weights $W_{QKVO}$.

For $n$ demonstrations, concatenate input–output pairs into

\[
I=[X_1\circ Y_1\circ\ldots\circ X_n\circ Y_n].
\]

The symbol $\circ$ means sequence concatenation. V2K processes these examples to understand the task and produces $M_\Theta(I\circ X_t)=\hat y_t$ without updating weights. The token $\hat y_t$ begins the V2K-generated response $\hat Y_t$, which is often similar to the expected response $Y_t$.

First illustrated in GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), this widely used adaptation of generic LMs also encompasses generic instructions as demonstrations for instruction-following frontier models. The present setting restricts demonstrations to classic input–output pairs.

Hidden SA outputs are activations: context-dependent footprints of processing before next-token production. They are useful for examining behavioral changes. A model with $L$ layers processing $R$ tokens supplies an activation tensor $A\in\mathbb R^{L\times R\times d}$. Here, $\mathbb R$ denotes real-valued entries, and the dimensions index layers, token positions, and vector components.

Activation similarity (asim; code K4X) is the token-wise cosine similarity between two activation tensors. Cosine similarity measures agreement in vector direction. K4X has shape $L\times R$: one value for each layer and compared token position. We study sequences $T=[I\circ X]$ with demonstrations and $T=X$ without them, under differing weights.

## 3 Are V2K and Q7M practically the same?

We investigate this question by collecting V2K activations, $A_{\mathrm{V2K}}$, using $T=[I\circ X]$ on 100 test samples from multiple datasets. These include B7V, the SST2 sentiment dataset, and W3F, the AGNews news-categorization dataset. We then train on those same demonstrations until convergence, when training no longer yields improvement, and collect Q7M activations, $A_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$, using $T=X$. We compare response-token positions through $\mathrm{K4X}(A_{\mathrm{V2K}},A_{\mathrm{Q7M}})$.

The models span Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) and Llama-3.2 (Grattafiori et al., 2024). Their size–depth correspondences are $1\mathrm B\leftrightarrow16$ layers, $3\mathrm B\leftrightarrow28$ layers, and $4\mathrm B\leftrightarrow36$ layers. Here, B denotes billion-parameter model sizes, and $\leftrightarrow$ links each size to its layer count. Experiment details are in §B.

V2K responses are much better calibrated than Q7M responses at similar, slightly better U6R, agreeing with the few-shot generalization and calibration findings of Duan et al. (2024). Table 1 reports task-level accuracy and calibration comparisons in all rows. Our proposed explanation is that output-oriented training learns shortcuts that fail on new data. In contrast, complex V2K circuits (Elhage et al., 2021), required across varied tasks, extract generalizable demonstration patterns. The methods achieve adaptation differently. Q7M is not a drop-in V2K replacement.

Recent internal-gradient-descent accounts describe SA as producing outputs as though contextual demonstrations had updated model parameters (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Akyürek et al., 2022). Such functional equivalence predicts similar output-position activations for a base model with demonstrations and a trained model without them.

The observed activations do not align across models (Figure 2). Initial and final layers align better than middle layers. Individual-token processing is expected in the initial and final layers. More abstract processing of the entire demonstration set is expected in the middle layers.

**Figure 2: Layerwise similarity with V2K activations.** H9C has small K4X, indicating differing adaptation functionality. P4Z aligns much more closely and performs much better. The first-row H9C curves average output-token K4X across tokens, samples, and datasets.

## 4 Inducing V2K-like behavior in Q7M Models

R8D priming, called ICL Activation Alignment (IA2), aligns layerwise processing rather than output similarity. It seeks adapted attention matrices $\tilde W_{QKVO}=(\tilde W_Q,\tilde W_K,\tilde W_V,\tilde W_O)$ satisfying

\[
\operatorname{SA}([I\circ X];W_{QKVO})
\approx
\operatorname{SA}(X;\tilde W_{QKVO})
\qquad \forall X\in\mathcal T.
\tag{3}
\]

The approximation concerns every newly generated token at index $-1$, meaning the last sequence position. The symbol $\approx$ means approximate agreement, and $\forall X\in\mathcal T$ means every query for the task. The left side uses the original weights with demonstrations. The right side uses adapted weights with only the query.

Chen et al. (2024a) give a closed-form solution for linearized attention using a Softmax kernel approximation—an approximation to the attention computation. Our target is a practical general solution for real nonlinear transformers. The aim is to put V2K qualities directly into adapted weights and process every query as the base model does at each layer.

For each training input $X_i$, use the remaining dataset samples to construct $I$. Generate $\hat Y_i$ from $T_i=[I\circ X_i]$, and collect output-position activations $A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}\in\mathbb R^{L\times G\times d}$ for the $G$ response tokens. These are the activation targets.

Next, supply $T_i=[X_i\circ\hat Y_i]$. Treat the response as generated with only the query in context, and collect the unaligned output-position activations $A^i$. Thus, both activation tensors concern the same response tokens, but only the target was collected with demonstrations. Minimize the mean squared error with respect to model parameters until convergence:

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|A^i-A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}\right\|.
\tag{4}
\]

Here, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}$ is the activation-alignment loss, and $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the norm of the activation difference.

Priming changes $\Theta$ to $\Theta'$. After priming, collect $\bar Y_i$ for every $X_i$ using $M_{\Theta'}$. Then train against ground truth $Y_i$ with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ until convergence. This second stage aligns intended target-token behavior. The full sequential procedure is P4Z, IA2 → SFT. The condition that stops after priming is J3N, IA2 only.

## 5 Experimental Results

Each $(\text{method},\text{dataset})$ combination trains to convergence, up to 50 epochs. An epoch is one pass through the training data. We test three learning rates, which control update size: slow:1e-4, medium:3e-4, and fast:1e-3. We select the best performer to neutralize hyperparameter-selection effects. Over 13,000 models were trained.

Resource-based choices fix rank-8 low-rank adaptation modules (LoRAs) targeting SA matrices $W_Q,W_K,W_O$. LoRA represents weight changes through a smaller, low-rank parameterization; rank specifies the size of that representation. Multi-token training is substantially more resource-extensive with long answers, so we restrict the training-set size $N$ to 16 for Llama and 8 for Qwen.

For activation collection, training caps generation at $G=200$. This yields target activation tensors $\hat A_i\in\mathbb R^{L\times200\times d}\ \forall x_i$, where $x_i$ denotes each training input. Target-token training instead uses the ground-truth response length for $G$. Further training details are in §B.

Single-token outputs contain one token, typically a category/class, True/False, or a multiple-choice question (MCQ) choice. Multi-token outputs have variable, potentially multiple-token length: the general open-generation case. Both settings compare adaptation across multiple models and tasks, frequently including distribution-shift evaluation, where evaluation data differ from the training distribution.

For single-token evaluation, we use the first generated token’s probability distribution for accuracy (U6R; acc) and expected calibration error (D8P; ECE; Guo et al., 2017). ECE measures disagreement between confidence and observed correctness. We evaluate the base model with demonstrations five times per task validation set, using different demonstrations, and report average metrics. We evaluate trained methods without demonstrations and report best-performing averages.

Multi-token evaluation counts only correctly answered questions in the required format; parsing and other details are in §A. Open-ended answer confidence is difficult to measure, so calibration is not measured. Generation limits conserve compute. They are 200 for L9S, the GSM8K grade-school mathematics dataset, and X4C, GSM Symbolic (GSM8Ks). Their 95-percentile answer lengths are 184 and 154. The limit is 400 for V7B, MATH Algebra (HMathA), whose corresponding length is 307.

Qwen3-4B-Base is used on every single- and multi-token task. Llama-3.2-1B additionally covers every single-token task except E2T. E2T is SciQr, a remapped-choice dataset derived from the scientific question-answering dataset SciQ, code O3D. E2T uses Llama-3.2-3B because the 1B model could not improve above random baseline for any $N$ up to 128. Multi-token experiments instead use Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct as the secondary model. This tests efficacy on post-trained models: models that have undergone additional training after base-model training.

The task setup is:

- **Sentiment:** Use B7V = SST2 (Socher et al., 2013), Z2H = Financial Phrasebank (FinS; Malo et al., 2014), and C9L = Poem Sentiment (PoemS; Sheng and Uthus, 2020). Train separately on B7V and Z2H. Evaluate each on all three datasets, giving one in-distribution (ID) dataset, drawn from the training distribution, and two out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets.
- **True/False:** Use N5Q = StrategyQA (STF; Geva et al., 2021), which requires multi-hop reasoning—combining multiple reasoning steps—before its binary conclusion.
- **News categorization:** Train on W3F = AGNews (AGN; Zhang et al., 2015). Evaluate on W3F and Y8J = BBCNews (BBCN; Greene and Cunningham, 2006).
- **MCQ:** Remap choices $[A,B,C,\ldots]$ into unrelated tokens for added complexity. Use E2T = SciQr, derived from O3D, for training and evaluation. Also evaluate on G6A = QASCr, the remapped-choice version of QASC (Khot et al., 2019).
- **Grade-school mathematics:** Train on L9S = GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021). Evaluate on L9S and X4C = GSM Symbolic (GSM8Ks; Mirzadeh et al., 2024).
- **Advanced mathematics:** Use V7B = MATH Algebra (HMathA; Hendrycks et al., 2021), containing higher-grade algebra problems.
- **Scientific question answering (QA):** Use O3D = SciQ (Johannes Welbl, 2017). Generate answer tokens rather than select options. This is also the source dataset for E2T.

Few-shot training datasets $\mathcal D_{\mathcal T}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ vary $N$ by powers of two, $[2,4,8,16,\ldots]$. We use five distinct sets per $N$ to average outliers. Each target tensor $A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}$ for $X_i$ uses the other $N-1$ samples as randomly ordered demonstrations. We reuse exactly the same training data across adaptation methods for fair comparison. Evaluation samples a separate set of 500 examples per task. Dataset and processing details are in §A.

Multi-token demonstrations and expected outputs use chain-of-thought style (Wei et al., 2022): ordinary-text reasoning precedes an answer in the demonstrated format. The reasoning is not inside `<think>` tags, which would mark a separate reasoning block. Parsing, the extraction of the answer from generated text, is dataset-specific:

- L9S and X4C ground truths follow `<reasoning steps> #### <numerical answer>`. The angle-bracketed text names placeholders; `####` separates reasoning from the answer. Reasoning correctness and conciseness are not evaluated. The parsed number must exactly match ground truth. Common alternatives, including `The answer is <value>`, permit slight formatting variation. The exact parser is in the code.
- V7B answers are expected and parsed inside `\boxed{}`, the notation marking a boxed mathematical answer.
- O3D ground truths are constructed as `<support text> #### <text answer>`. Parsing follows that format, and only exact string matches count as correct.

### 5.2 Why is R8D priming important?

Unchanged base weights significantly influence activations under LoRA. Activation patterns alone therefore cannot isolate training-induced behavioral changes. We also analyze weights directly, using all single- and multi-token experiments.

We take singular value decomposition (SVD) basis vectors of weight matrices. A basis is a set of directions spanning a weight subspace. Subspace Overlap (A5Y) measures how much weight space two methods share:

\[
\frac{1}{r}*\left\|U_1^T\cdot U_2\right\|_F^2.
\]

Here, $U_1,U_2$ contain the compared methods’ basis vectors, $r$ is LoRA rank, and the superscript $T$ means transpose. The squared Frobenius norm $\|\cdot\|_F^2$ sums squared matrix entries; $*$ and $\cdot$ denote multiplication. This quantity isolates and compares functional-behavior differences.

H9C weight updates are almost completely orthogonal to those of the other methods: their update directions have almost no alignment (Figure 4). The most performant P4Z models substantially share J3N’s spanned weight space, with mean overlap around $39\%$ in the table below. Much of their gain is attributable to R8D, whose identified subspaces are practically unreachable through H9C.

The wide J3N–P4Z overlap distribution arises when priming already produces output alignment. Subsequent target training then changes little before convergence, leaving very high overlap.

**Figure 4:** P4Z has significantly high A5Y with J3N, while H9C aligns with neither. This indicates an R8D signal absent from H9C and important for performance.

The three training conditions are baseline H9C = SFT only, priming-only J3N = IA2 only, and sequential P4Z = IA2 → SFT. All appear in Figure 2’s K4X comparison with V2K. Priming drastically increases K4X, and sequential training retains elevated K4X. H9C and P4Z differ only in their starting state for target-response training.

P4Z occupies intermediate K4X between the other conditions while achieving better U6R and calibration. Increasing similarity improves calibration, but extreme similarity need not optimize U6R (Figure 3). The V2K signal is not always correct. One case in §C has J3N following V2K even as overfitting worsens performance. Functional alignment and ground-truth output alignment are best combined.

## 6 Discussion

A simultaneous alternative, T6B = IA2 + SFT, applies $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}$ and $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ together rather than sequentially. The target activation tensor $\hat A$ uses the model’s own generated response tokens whenever $G>1$. That V2K response can differ from ground truth, even in length. The ground-truth objectives are incompatible for joint training.

We therefore use self-distillation on V2K responses, analogous to Snell et al. (2022) but with an additional activation signal. The joint loss is

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}+\beta\cdot\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}},
\]

where $\beta$ controls the relative weight of token training. We vary $\beta$ over four values smoothly spanning J3N to H9C; details are in §B. Multi-token reports select the best $(\text{learning rate},\beta)$ combination across five random seeds, the settings that determine randomized runs. Further T6B results are in §C.

T6B extracts significantly more performance from V2K responses than H9C (Table 3, all rows). H9C and P4Z trained on those responses perform worse than when trained on ground truths. This is shown by comparing their columns in Tables 3 and 2 for L9S→L9S, L9S→X4C, V7B→V7B, and O3D→O3D.

**Computational overhead:** P4Z adds one activation-collection pass before training: V2K inference on the given dataset. This one-time cost greatly benefits subsequent capabilities; inference itself costs the same as H9C.

Knowledge Distillation (KD; M8G) transfers a stronger teacher’s behavior into a student through soft-label matching. Soft labels supply each token’s probability mass, giving a denser teacher signal (Hinton et al., 2015). Here, the teacher is the same model enhanced with contextual demonstrations.

The comparator F5W = SFT (soft labels) trains and evaluates Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct on L9S and Llama-3.2-1B on B7V using V2K responses. **Potential improvements:** Resource constraints fixed design choices. Deferred explorations include LoRA rank; target-module ablations, which vary which model components are adapted; selective R8D, which aligns only selected components; prompt optimization; and finer-grained control of the two training signals rather than fixed learning rates.

Extreme alignment can hurt performance (§5.2 and Figure 3). Layer sensitivity to contextual demonstrations varies (Todd et al., 2023), so selective layer alignment may be more effective. Demonstration order affects V2K performance (Lu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2021). This permits prompt optimization—improving the supplied context—before collecting target activations.

## 7 Related Work

**V2K versus Q7M:** Performance comparisons have an unclear verdict (Mosbach et al., 2023; Duan et al., 2024); the present experiments find Q7M worse (§3 and Tables 1–2). Prior work studies functional similarity (Dai et al., 2022), functional differences (Wang et al., 2023), and miscalibration and remedies (Zhang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2025).

Sia et al. (2024) locate “where” translation happens during contextual adaptation. They use context masking, which hides parts of the context, and activations as functional signals. Recent work identifies Q7M memorization/overfitting and catastrophic forgetting—the loss of previously learned capabilities during further training (Chu et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025). The improved pipeline aims to address these problems.

**Context distillation:** Explicit-context compression for adaptation includes intermediate contemplation tokens (Cheng and Van Durme, 2024; Jiang et al., 2025), on-the-fly adapter generation to internalize context (Chen et al., 2025; Charakorn et al., 2025), related-text-enhanced distillation (Zhu et al., 2025; Choi et al., 2025), and direct context-to-weight compression (Shen et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024b; Shin et al., 2024).

These approaches compress context through intermediate tokens, generated adaptation parameters, related text, or direct weight changes. They distill contextual effects on outputs through token-response signals. The present complementary approach improves distillation using the model’s own contextual processing signals, supported by Aguilar et al. (2020), Jin et al. (2024), and Yang et al. (2024).

**Activation steering:** Extracting steering vectors or weights for targeted internal locations seeks intended output effects (Postmus and Abreu, 2024; Stolfo et al., 2025; Caccia et al., 2025; Fleshman and Van Durme, 2024; Arditi et al., 2024). These vectors or weights change internal activations to guide model behavior. Like R8D, these interventions influence functionality in activation space. Unlike their targeted, narrow-domain approach, R8D seeks large behavioral change while avoiding potential brittleness and reliability problems (Queiroz Da Silva et al., 2025).

## 8 Conclusion and Future Work

Activation probing—examining internal activations—distinguishes adaptation mechanisms (§3 and Figure 2). V2K often encodes richer, more generalizable patterns. R8D shifts models into a more adaptable weight subspace inaccessible to Q7M alone (§5.2 and Figure 4). The most performant P4Z and J3N models share around $39\%$ of their spanned weight space.

Future work tests the diversity and catastrophic-forgetting effects of R8D-based adaptation to assess post-training benefits. It also tests effects on post-trained models tuned through reinforcement learning (RL).
