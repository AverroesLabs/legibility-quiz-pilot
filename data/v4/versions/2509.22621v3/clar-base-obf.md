###### Abstract

In-context learning (ICL), called V2K here, adapts a model through examples in its input context without changing its weights. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT), called Q7M here, instead changes model weights using labeled examples. ICL Activation Alignment (IA2), called R8D here, primes a model by aligning its activations—internal processing outputs—with those produced during V2K. P4Z applies R8D and then Q7M. H9C is the baseline that applies Q7M alone.

Across 12 popular benchmarks and two model families, P4Z significantly improves output accuracy, called U6R, and calibration: the agreement between predicted confidence and observed correctness (§5; Tables 1–2; significance testing in §C). It provides practically useful adaptation and a way to examine the model’s internal mechanics. R8D is self-distillation: the model learns from its own context-conditioned processing. It aims to reproduce V2K activation patterns in weight-trained models and thereby encourage V2K-like internal reasoning. Its priming signal is unavailable through H9C.

## 1 Introduction

Generic large language models (LLMs) serve specialized applications. One example is classifying news articles into predefined categories for a news aggregator. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) changes a small set of model parameters rather than the full model. PEFT techniques (Hu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022) often make these small adapted parameter sets easy to load into, and remove from, graphics processing unit (GPU) memory.

Generalization through Q7M—performing well on examples not used for training—typically requires many labeled samples (Le Scao and Rush, 2021). Collecting those labels can be expensive. V2K avoids weight updates, but its demonstrations and instructions occupy the model’s limited input context. They also increase computation for each query and leave less room for query and response tokens. Tokens are the text units processed and generated by the model.

The research question is whether information-rich V2K activations can improve Q7M quality. The hypothesis is that these activations contain information about extracting generalizable patterns from context. That information may be absent from few-shot weight adaptation, which trains on a small number of examples and can learn shortcuts or overfit target responses. Overfitting means fitting the training examples in ways that do not generalize.

Similar behavior in output-token space need not imply similar internal processing. Different activations indicate different adaptation mechanisms, consistent with Shen et al. (2024).

Context-to-weight distillation trains model weights to reproduce behavior induced by an input context. Methods using response-text signals (Snell et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024b) can inherit the same output-training problems. Reproducing a context-conditioned model’s outputs does not ensure reproducing how it functions.

**Figure 1:** Standard Q7M enforces output-space agreement between a response and its target. This is the third step of the proposed pipeline. Under low data, it produces subpar performance and miscalibration. The pipeline first collects rich V2K activations, then enforces functional alignment, and finally trains against target responses. Its comparison charts aggregate performance across models and datasets. Details: §5.

## 2 Background and Notation

Let $\mathcal T$ denote a task. Its training dataset is
$\mathcal D_{\mathcal T}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $N$ is the number of examples, $i$ indexes examples, $X_i$ is an input, and $Y_i$ is its labeled output.

Let $M_\Theta$ be a model with parameters, or weights, $\Theta$. Labeled weight adaptation, Q7M = Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), changes $\Theta$ to $\Theta'$. For a test input $X_t$, the adapted model produces
$M_{\Theta'}(X_t)=\bar y_t$. Here, $t$ indexes the test example, $\bar y_t$ is the first generated token, and $\bar Y_t$ is the full generated response. Repeatedly applying the model to the growing input-and-response sequence extends that response.

For a ground-truth response $Y_i$ containing $G$ tokens, generate a response $\bar Y_i$ of $G$ tokens. Minimize cross-entropy, the loss comparing a predicted token with its target, across all generated positions:

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{G}
\operatorname{cross\text{-}entropy}(\bar Y_{ij},Y_{ij}).
\tag{2}
\]

Here, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ is the Q7M training loss, $j$ indexes response positions, and $\bar Y_{ij}$ and $Y_{ij}$ are the generated and target tokens at position $j$ of example $i$.

A decoder-only transformer language model (LM) predicts successive tokens using preceding tokens. It stacks self-attention (SA) and linear layers. Self-attention combines information from tokens in the sequence; linear layers apply learned linear transformations.

Let $T=[t_i]_{i=1}^{R}$ be a sequence of $R$ token vectors, where $t_i$ is the vector at position $i$. The model applies SA and linear projections at every token and layer. The final-layer projection at the last token passes through the LM head, the component that predicts the next token, to select the most likely next token.

SA depends on other tokens in the sequence. Let $W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O\in\Theta$ be the query, key, value, and output projection matrices. Write them collectively as $W_{QKVO}$. Then

\[
\operatorname{SA}(T;W_{QKVO})
=Z=
\left[
z_i=\sigma\left(\frac{q_iK^T}{\sqrt d}\right)V\cdot W_O
\right]_{i=1}^{R},
\tag{1}
\]

where:

- $Z=[z_i]_{i=1}^{R}$ is the sequence of SA output vectors.
- $d$ is the vector width; both $Z$ and $T$ have shape $R\times d$.
- $\sigma$ is Softmax, which converts scores into nonnegative weights that sum to one.
- $q_i=t_iW_Q$ is the query vector at position $i$.
- $t_{:i}$ denotes the first $i$ token vectors.
- $K=t_{:i}W_K$ and $V=t_{:i}W_V$ are their key and value matrices.
- The superscript $T$ on $K^T$ denotes matrix transpose.
- $\sqrt d$ scales the query–key scores, and $\cdot$ denotes matrix multiplication.

The behavioral changes studied here arise from changing either the context $T$ or the weights $W_{QKVO}$.

Let
$I=[X_1\circ Y_1\circ\ldots\circ X_n\circ Y_n]$
be a sequence of $n$ demonstrations. The symbol $\circ$ means concatenation: placing sequences one after another. Inference-time adaptation, V2K = In-Context Learning (ICL), processes these examples to understand the task without updating weights. It produces
$M_\Theta(I\circ X_t)=\hat y_t$,
where $\hat y_t$ is the first token of the V2K response $\hat Y_t$. The full response is often similar to the expected response $Y_t$.

First illustrated in GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), V2K is widely used to adapt generic LMs. It also includes using generic instructions as demonstrations for instruction-following frontier models. This paper restricts demonstrations to classic input–output pairs.

Hidden SA outputs are called activations. They are context-dependent records of processing before next-token production. They can therefore help examine behavioral changes.

A model with $L$ layers processing $R$ tokens supplies an activation tensor
$A\in\mathbb R^{L\times R\times d}$. A tensor is a multidimensional array; this one contains a $d$-dimensional activation vector for every layer and token. The symbol $\mathbb R$ denotes real-valued entries.

The comparison measure is K4X = activation similarity (asim). It computes cosine similarity—the agreement in vector direction—between corresponding token activations in two tensors. Its output has shape $L\times R$, with one similarity value per layer and token. The studied sequences are $T=[I\circ X]$ with demonstrations and $T=X$ without demonstrations, under different weights.

## 3 Are V2K and Q7M practically the same?

V2K responses are much better calibrated than Q7M responses while having similar, slightly better U6R. Table 1 reports the single-token accuracy and calibration comparisons. This agrees with findings on few-shot generalization and calibration from Duan et al. (2024).

The proposed explanation is that output-oriented training learns shortcuts that fail on new data. In contrast, complex V2K circuits—internal processing mechanisms (Elhage et al., 2021)—must work across varied tasks and extract generalizable patterns from demonstrations. The methods adapt differently. Q7M is not a drop-in replacement for V2K.

Recent accounts describe V2K as internal gradient descent: attention produces outputs as though contextual demonstrations had updated model parameters (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Akyürek et al., 2022). Gradient descent is the process of changing parameters to reduce a training loss. Such functional equivalence predicts similar activations at output-token positions for two models: a base model with demonstrations and a trained model without them.

The observed activations do not align across models (Figure 2). Initial and final layers align better than middle layers. Individual-token processing is expected in the initial and final layers; abstract processing of the entire demonstration set is expected in the middle layers.

**Figure 2: Layerwise similarity with V2K activations.** H9C has small K4X, indicating different adaptation functionality. P4Z aligns much more closely and performs much better. The first-row H9C curves average output-token K4X across tokens, samples, and datasets.

For this investigation, collect $A_{\mathrm{V2K}}$, the V2K activation tensor, using $T=[I\circ X]$ on 100 test samples from multiple datasets. These include B7V, the SST2 sentiment dataset, and W3F, the AGNews news-categorization dataset. Then train using those same demonstrations until convergence, meaning training has reached its stopping point, and collect $A_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ using $T=X$.

The models span Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) and Llama-3.2 (Grattafiori et al., 2024). Their size–depth correspondences are
$1\mathrm B\leftrightarrow16$ layers,
$3\mathrm B\leftrightarrow28$ layers, and
$4\mathrm B\leftrightarrow36$ layers.
Here, $\mathrm B$ denotes billion parameters, and $\leftrightarrow$ links each model size to its layer count.

The comparison at output-token positions is
$\mathrm{K4X}(A_{\mathrm{V2K}},A_{\mathrm{Q7M}})$.
Experiment details: §B.

## 4 Inducing V2K-like behavior in Q7M Models

The procedure has two stages: activation-alignment priming, followed by target-response training. Priming changes $\Theta$ to $\Theta'$. After priming, collect a generated response $\bar Y_i$ for every $X_i$ using $M_{\Theta'}$. Then train against ground truth $Y_i$ with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ until convergence. This second stage aligns the model with the intended target-token behavior.

The preceding priming operation, R8D = ICL Activation Alignment (IA2), aligns layerwise processing rather than output similarity. It seeks adapted attention weights
$\tilde W_{QKVO}=(\tilde W_Q,\tilde W_K,\tilde W_V,\tilde W_O)$
such that, at every newly generated token at index $-1$,

\[
\operatorname{SA}([I\circ X];W_{QKVO})
\approx
\operatorname{SA}(X;\tilde W_{QKVO})
\qquad \forall X\in\mathcal T.
\tag{3}
\]

The tilde marks adapted weights. The index $-1$ denotes the last, newly generated token position. The symbol $\approx$ means approximately equal, and $\forall X\in\mathcal T$ means for every input $X$ in the task.

Chen et al. (2024a) give a closed-form solution for linearized attention using a Softmax kernel approximation. That solution approximates attention to make it analytically tractable. The goal here is a practical, general solution for real nonlinear transformers. It places V2K qualities directly into adapted weights so that each query is processed as the base model processes it at each layer.

For each training input $X_i$:

1. Use the remaining dataset examples to construct demonstrations $I$.
2. Generate $\hat Y_i$ from $T_i=[I\circ X_i]$.
3. Collect V2K activations at output-token positions:
   $A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}\in\mathbb R^{L\times G\times d}$, where $G$ is the number of response tokens.
4. Supply $T_i=[X_i\circ\hat Y_i]$ without demonstrations. Treat the response as generated with only the query in context. Collect its unaligned output-position activations $A^i$.
5. Minimize the mean squared error with respect to model parameters until convergence:

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|A^i-A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}\right\|.
\tag{4}
\]

Here, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}$ is the priming loss, and the norm $\|\cdot\|$ measures the discrepancy between the two activation tensors.

## 5 Experimental Results

Each $(\text{method},\text{dataset})$ combination trains to convergence, for up to 50 epochs. An epoch is one pass through the training data. Training uses three learning rates, which control the update step size: slow:1e-4, medium:3e-4, and fast:1e-3. The best performer is selected to neutralize hyperparameter-selection effects. Hyperparameters are training choices set outside the learned weights. Over 13,000 models were trained.

Resource constraints fix rank-8 low-rank adaptation modules, or LoRAs. LoRA restricts learned weight changes to a low-rank parameterization; rank 8 sets its internal rank. These modules target the SA matrices $W_Q,W_K,W_O$.

Multi-token training is substantially more resource-demanding with long answers. It therefore restricts $N$ to 16 for Llama and 8 for Qwen. For activation collection, training caps generation at $G=200$, yielding
$\hat A_i\in\mathbb R^{L\times200\times d}\ \forall x_i$.
Here, $\hat A_i$ denotes the collected activation tensor, and $x_i$ denotes each input example. Target-token training instead uses the ground-truth response length for $G$. Further training details: §B.

Single-token evaluation uses the probability distribution of the first generated token for both measures:

- U6R = accuracy (acc), the correctness measure.
- D8P = expected calibration error (ECE), which measures the mismatch between predicted confidence and observed accuracy (Guo et al., 2017).

For each task validation set, the base model is evaluated five times with different demonstrations, and its metrics are averaged. Trained methods are then evaluated without demonstrations, and best-performing averages are reported.

Multi-token evaluation counts only questions answered correctly in the required format. Parsing—the extraction of the answer from generated text—and other details appear in §A. Confidence is difficult to measure for open-ended answers, so calibration is not measured.

Generation limits conserve compute. They are 200 for L9S, the GSM8K grade-school mathematics dataset, and X4C, the GSM Symbolic dataset, also called GSM8Ks. Their 95-percentile answer lengths are 184 and 154, respectively. The limit is 400 for V7B, the MATH Algebra dataset, also called HMathA, whose corresponding length is 307.

Qwen3-4B-Base is used on every single- and multi-token task. Llama-3.2-1B also covers every single-token task except E2T. E2T, called SciQr, is the remapped-choice version of the SciQ scientific question-answering dataset. It uses Llama-3.2-3B because the 1B model could not improve above the random baseline for any $N$ up to 128. Multi-token experiments instead use Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct as the secondary model. This tests efficacy on post-trained models: models already adapted after base training.

Single-token outputs contain one token, typically a category or class, True/False, or a multiple-choice question (MCQ) option. Multi-token outputs have variable length and may contain multiple tokens. They represent the general open-generation case. Both settings compare adaptation across multiple models and tasks, often including distribution shift: evaluation on data from a different distribution than the training data.

- **Sentiment:** B7V = SST2 (Socher et al., 2013) is used alongside Z2H = Financial Phrasebank [FinS] (Malo et al., 2014) and C9L = Poem Sentiment [PoemS] (Sheng and Uthus, 2020). FinS and PoemS are the corresponding dataset abbreviations. Training is performed separately on B7V and Z2H. Each trained model is evaluated on all three datasets. This gives one in-distribution (ID) dataset, matching the training source, and two out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets.
- **True/False:** N5Q = StrategyQA [STF] (Geva et al., 2021) requires multi-hop reasoning—combining multiple reasoning steps—before a binary conclusion. STF labels this True/False setting.
- **News categorization:** Training uses W3F = AGNews [AGN] (Zhang et al., 2015). Evaluation uses W3F and Y8J = BBCNews [BBCN] (Greene and Cunningham, 2006). AGN and BBCN are the corresponding dataset abbreviations.
- **MCQ:** Choices $[A,B,C,\ldots]$ are remapped into unrelated tokens for added complexity. E2T = SciQr, derived from O3D, is used for training and evaluation. G6A = QASCr, the remapped-choice version of QASC (Khot et al., 2019), is used for evaluation.
- **Grade-school mathematics:** Training uses L9S = GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021). Evaluation uses L9S and X4C = GSM Symbolic [GSM8Ks] (Mirzadeh et al., 2024).
- **Advanced mathematics:** V7B = MATH Algebra [HMathA] (Hendrycks et al., 2021) contains higher-grade algebra problems.
- **Scientific question answering (QA):** O3D = SciQ (Johannes Welbl, 2017) requires generating answer tokens rather than selecting options. It is also the source dataset for E2T.

Few-shot training datasets
$\mathcal D_{\mathcal T}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$
vary $N$ by powers of two: $[2,4,8,16,\ldots]$. Five distinct sets are used per $N$ to average outliers. Each target tensor $A^i_{\mathrm{V2K}}$, for every $X_i$, uses the other $N-1$ examples as randomly ordered demonstrations. All adaptation methods reuse exactly the same training data for fair comparison.

Evaluation uses a separate sample of 500 examples per task. Dataset and processing details: §A.

Multi-token demonstrations and expected outputs use chain-of-thought style (Wei et al., 2022): ordinary-text reasoning precedes an answer in the demonstrated format. The reasoning is not placed inside `<think>` tags.

Parsing is dataset-specific. L9S and X4C ground truths follow “$<$reasoning steps$>$ #### $<$numerical answer$>$”. Reasoning correctness and conciseness are not evaluated. The parsed number must exactly match ground truth. Common alternatives, including “The answer is $<$value$>$”, allow slight formatting variation. The exact parser is in the code.

V7B answers are expected and parsed inside `\boxed{}`. O3D ground truths are constructed as “$<$support text$>$ #### $<$text answer$>$”. They are parsed using that format, and only exact string matches count as correct.

### 5.2 Why is R8D priming important?

Under LoRA, unchanged base weights significantly influence activations. Activation patterns alone therefore cannot isolate behavioral changes caused by training.

Direct weight analysis instead uses all single- and multi-token experiments. It obtains basis vectors—vectors describing the directions spanned by a matrix—through singular value decomposition (SVD) of weight matrices. The comparison quantity is A5Y = Subspace Overlap. It measures shared weight space to isolate and compare functional-behavior differences:

\[
\frac{1}{r}*\left\|U_1^T\cdot U_2\right\|_F^2.
\]

Here, $U_1$ and $U_2$ contain the compared methods’ basis vectors, $r$ is the LoRA rank, and $*$ denotes multiplication. The subscript $F$ denotes the Frobenius norm. Its square sums the squared matrix entries. Thus, the expression sums the squared entries of $U_1^T\cdot U_2$ and divides by $r$.

H9C weight updates are almost completely orthogonal to those of the other methods: they occupy nearly nonoverlapping directions. The most performant P4Z models substantially share J3N’s spanned weight space (Figure 4; the table below reports around $39\%$ mean shared space). Much of their gain is attributable to R8D. The subspaces identified by R8D are practically unreachable through H9C.

The wide J3N–P4Z overlap distribution arises when priming already produces output alignment. Subsequent target training then changes little before convergence, leaving very high overlap.

**Figure 4:** P4Z has significantly high A5Y with J3N, while H9C aligns with neither. This indicates an R8D signal absent from H9C and important for performance.

|---|---|

The three training conditions are baseline H9C = SFT only, priming without target training J3N = IA2 only, and sequential adaptation P4Z = IA2 → SFT. All appear in Figure 2’s K4X comparison with V2K.

Priming drastically increases K4X. Sequential training retains elevated K4X. H9C and P4Z differ only in their starting state for SFT. P4Z has K4X between the other two conditions while achieving better U6R and calibration (Figures 2–3).

Increasing similarity improves calibration, but extreme similarity need not optimize U6R. The V2K signal is not always correct. In one case in §C, J3N follows V2K even as overfitting worsens performance. Functional alignment and ground-truth output alignment are best combined.

T6B applies $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}$ and $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}$ together rather than sequentially. However, whenever $G>1$, the target activation tensor $\hat A$ uses the model’s own generated response tokens. That V2K response can differ from ground truth, even in length. The ground-truth objectives are therefore incompatible for joint training.

Self-distillation on V2K responses avoids using different response sequences for the two objectives. It is analogous to Snell et al. (2022), with an additional activation signal, and uses

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{R8D}}+\beta\cdot\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Q7M}}.
\]

Here, $\beta$ weights the target-token loss relative to the activation loss. It varies over four values smoothly spanning J3N to H9C; details appear in §B. Multi-token reports select the best $(\text{learning rate},\beta)$ combination across five random seeds, which control randomized experimental choices. Further T6B results appear in §C.

**Computational overhead:** P4Z adds one activation-collection pass before training: V2K inference on the given dataset. This one-time cost greatly benefits subsequent capabilities. Inference itself costs the same as H9C.

**Potential improvements:** Resource constraints fixed the design choices. Deferred explorations include LoRA rank and target-module ablations—experiments that vary those choices—selective R8D, prompt optimization, and finer-grained control of the two training signals rather than fixed learning rates.

Extreme alignment can hurt performance. Layer sensitivity to contextual demonstrations also varies (Todd et al., 2023). Selective layer alignment may therefore be more effective. Demonstration order affects V2K performance (Lu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2021), allowing optimization of the demonstration prompt before collecting target activations.

## 7 Related Work

**V2K versus Q7M:** Performance comparisons have an unclear verdict (Mosbach et al., 2023; Duan et al., 2024). The present experiments find Q7M worse. Prior work studies functional similarity (Dai et al., 2022), functional differences (Wang et al., 2023), and miscalibration and its remedies (Zhang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2025).

Sia et al. (2024) locate “where” translation happens during contextual adaptation. They use context masking, which hides parts of the context, and activations as functional signals. Recent work identifies Q7M memorization/overfitting and catastrophic forgetting—the loss of previously learned behavior after further training (Chu et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025). The improved pipeline aims to address these problems.

**Context distillation:** Methods that compress explicit context for adaptation include intermediate contemplation tokens (Cheng and Van Durme, 2024; Jiang et al., 2025), on-the-fly generation of adapters to internalize context (Chen et al., 2025; Charakorn et al., 2025), distillation enhanced with related text (Zhu et al., 2025; Choi et al., 2025), and direct context-to-weight compression (Shen et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024b; Shin et al., 2024). Adapters are added trainable components used to adapt a model.

These approaches distill contextual effects on outputs through token-response signals. The present complementary approach improves distillation using the model’s own contextual processing signals. Aguilar et al. (2020), Jin et al. (2024), and Yang et al. (2024) support this approach.

**Activation steering:** Activation steering extracts vectors or weights for targeted internal locations to produce intended output effects (Postmus and Abreu, 2024; Stolfo et al., 2025; Caccia et al., 2025; Fleshman and Van Durme, 2024; Arditi et al., 2024). Like R8D, these interventions influence functionality in activation space. Unlike their targeted, narrow-domain approach, R8D seeks a large behavioral change while avoiding potential brittleness and reliability problems (Queiroz Da Silva et al., 2025).

## 8 Conclusion and Future Work

Activation probing—examining internal activations—distinguishes adaptation mechanisms. V2K often encodes richer, more generalizable patterns. R8D shifts models into a more adaptable weight subspace inaccessible to Q7M alone (Figures 2–4 and §5.2).

Future work will test the diversity and catastrophic-forgetting effects of R8D-based adaptation to assess its post-training benefits. It will also test effects on post-trained models tuned through reinforcement learning (RL).
