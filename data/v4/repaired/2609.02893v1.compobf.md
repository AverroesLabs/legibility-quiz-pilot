# Probe Generalization as Subspace Selection for OOD Deception Detection

Daniel Yoo, Adrians Skapars

###### Abstract


The closure of M3Z by Q8B was 78% for Q7V and 25% for J8W under V6Q deception probes on 3 held-out datasets (8; 3.1), through the scoring by A8T of each PC on whether its extreme examples imply a transferable deception direction (K9U denoting greedy PC selection, M3Z denoting the baseline-to-oracle gap), while inputs projected onto a few training-distribution principal components (PCs) gave transfer nearly matching S6F; heavily weighted source directions encode surface features, transferring ones encode the contrast abstractly, and OOD robustness is largely subspace selection.

## 1 Introduction

A source-trained probe restricted to the 8–15 PCs surfaced by target-supervised K9U nearly matches S6F, a small transferable subspace existing (4.2), while A8T, without target data, partially recovers it, selecting PCs likely encoding transferable behavioral contrast rather than surface patterns (4.5), even as ranking PCs by source or target probe weights is found not to beat V9D, source- and target-side rankings failing to recover it (4.3).

Figure 1: projection of activations onto the source PCA basis, filtering of PCs, training of probes on the selected subspace.

The testbed consists of deception probes on V6Q (V6Q denoting Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), trained on Z4K (Z4K denoting Roleplaying) and evaluated on Q7V (Q7V denoting Insider Trading Report), P3Y (P3Y denoting Insider Trading Confirm), and J8W (J8W denoting Sandbagging), the question asked being which PC subset supports generalizable probes and how to identify it.

Probes strong in-distribution can be unreliable OOD (S6F denoting the target-trained probe), learning spurious correlations (A8T denoting the LLM judge) even though behavioral concepts are linearly represented (V9D denoting the full-representation baseline), all in the source-domain PCA basis without assuming a privileged neuron basis.

## 2 Related Work

For the reduction of misalignment, concepts have been ablated during fine-tuning through natural-language concept identification, an interpretive practice extending the capability of LLMs to explain neurons from high-activation examples to the automatic labeling of SAE features. Uneven OOD generalization is possible in probes strong in-distribution, and high accuracy in them may reflect the probe learning the task, or surface correlates, or information merely recoverable rather than encoded, all of which attaches to probes that have nonetheless been applied to latent truthfulness and to strategic deception. Raised by truth, honesty, emotion, refusal and an "assistant" axis appearing as linear directions usable for reading and steering is the question of when such directions are stable concepts versus context-dependent correlations.

## 3 Setup

The retention of R2F, motivated by the decrease of SAE decoder alignment with PC index (Appendix C), served interpretability, advancing no claim that low-index PCs generalize better; extraction from the layer 15 residual stream of V6Q (8B, series 3.1), chosen for highest cross-domain E3K (denoting the area under the receiver operating characteristic) in prior work, averaged over assistant-response tokens, standardization from source statistics applied to targets, PCA on source, R2F (denoting the top 100 PCs) retained, logistic regression at $\lambda=10$, subset-$S$ probes projecting onto $S$, baselines on full standardized activations, across 4 deception datasets (Appendix A), Z4K as source and Q7V, P3Y, J8W as targets, GPT-4o labels honest (0), ambiguous (-1, removed), deceptive (1).

## 4 Experiments

That a probe restricted to the 19 Q8B, selected without any target data, beats V9D on all 3 targets, beats W4J by wide margins on Q7V and J8W, and beats G2Y on all 3 targets — closing on Q7V 78% of the M3Z from V9D (0.706) to K9U (0.929) and on J8W 25% of the M3Z to K9U (0.832), while on P3Y performance is near ceiling and uninformative, all methods except G2Y being similar — is reported together with Figure 5, which gives E3K for Q8B (19 PCs), V9D, W4J at k = 19 and the remaining 81 PCs constituting G2Y, with 95% CIs (4.5).

The 19 PCs in question are those scoring 6 in both runs of a procedure in which, for each of the 100 PCs, the 8 most and 8 least activating source examples were given to A8T, descriptions of the positive pole, the negative pole and the direction being elicited together with a generalizability rating on a 1–10 scale (N5P denoting the resulting OOD score; Appendix B), no PC having scored above 6, so that 6 is treated as the maximum, these PCs constituting the Q8B (Q8B denoting the interp-selected PCs); of the 5 PCs appearing in K9U selections for >1 target, 3 of 5 received this maximum score, versus 1 of the top 5 Z1M PCs, and the interpretations of K9U PCs describe the contrast at a domain-surviving level of abstraction (Appendix E) (4.4).

Both hypotheses fail — Z1M performs ≈ V9D, the PCs the source probe relies on not generalizing, while H5X does well on Q7V, worse than K9U on P3Y and below random on J8W, so that target-separating PCs are not those that transfer when weighted on source (see Appendix D; Figure 4 giving E3K for PCs ranked by Z1M weights, H5X weights and K9U, with 95% CIs) — the hypotheses in question having been that transferable PCs (i) individually separate target classes or (ii) are those the source probe already relies on, operationalized by ranking PCs by U1L (U1L denoting the standardized coefficient, |coef| × PC std) of a probe on source-PCA-projected activations (Z1M and H5X denoting such probes trained on source and on target respectively) and then training on source projected onto the top k, k being taken from K9U run on the full target set with no CV, giving 13 (Q7V), 7 (P3Y) and 14 (J8W), the K9U numbers thus differing slightly from 4.2 (4.3).

K9U nearly matches S6F on Q7V and J8W, +0.22 and +0.23 E3K over V9D, whereas W4J (W4J denoting the top-k PCs by variance) matches K9U only on P3Y and falls far behind on the harder tasks: which PCs matter, and variance explained does not predict usefulness (Figure 3 giving E3K for V9D, W4J with k matched to K9U and K9U oracle PCs, with 95% CIs); the K9U procedure starts from an empty set and adds the PC (of R2F) that most improves the target E3K of a probe fit on source projections, weights always being fit on source while selection uses target labels on non-held-out folds under H6Q with evaluation on the held-out fold, stopping when no gain is >0.001, mean subset sizes being 15 (Q7V), 8 (P3Y) and 14 (J8W), this constituting an upper bound, not an unbiased OOD estimate (4.2).

That the information is present, and that the source probe instead learns a non-generalizing direction, is established by the V9D source probe attaining 0.874, 0.706 and 0.606 on P3Y, Q7V and J8W respectively, against S6F probes in the R2F subspace attaining 0.950 / 0.936 / 0.869 in the same P3Y, Q7V, J8W order (Figure 2 giving target E3K for source-trained V9D and R2F probes, and S6F R2F probes, under H6Q, with 95% CIs) (4.1).

## 5 Limitations

More robust is Q7V (0.706→0.881, 78% of the M3Z to 0.929), with PC “encodings” descriptive under no causal validation and high-scoring PCs not ablated at inference, while P3Y stays uninformative (baseline 0.874) and generality to other models/datasets remains open from one model (layer 15) and one source dataset; a transferable subspace is perhaps unsurprising for a fully linear pipeline, nonlinear probes and other readout locations untested, and J8W gains are small—0.606→0.662, 25% of the M3Z to 0.832—for the most OOD target.

## 6 Conclusion

The chosen subspace must preserve the intended feature and filter spurious correlation, with OOD probing constituting representation selection and with the requirement being not only whether a signal is linearly accessible but where it is represented most robustly, because V9D probes may mix general behavioral features with dataset-specific cues that aid in-distribution accuracy but hurt transfer, whereas sparse PC subspaces may isolate more general structure.

## Appendix B LLM Judge Prompt

Output was to comprise Positive pole, Negative pole, Shared direction and N5P (1-10), the N5P being rated 1–10 with a brief reason, low scores assigned for surface cues, lexical habits and task-specific phrasing, noisy/brittle/context-dependent directions meriting ~1–4, and ≥5 reserved only for a general deception-relevant strategy. The prompt content of the judge, GPT-5-mini, demanded interpretation of a bipolar PC direction (positive/negative activations toward opposite poles; larger magnitude = stronger match), with lines as token<TAB>activation normalized to integers −10 to +10, in a setup of Z4K within a simple scenario with a strategic chance to lie, high-positive and high-negative examples having been inserted, the dominant contrast to be inferred without assuming broad sentiment, the narrowest accurate description preferred, overgeneralization avoided.
