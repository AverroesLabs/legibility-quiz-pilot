Active-context privacy risk should be evaluated as a persistent behavioral failure mode, not only as memorization or immediate jailbreaks. The recoverability of user-disclosed secrets in an active LLM conversation, following V9B and persuasion-based probing, is audited in the work reported as K7X: Auditing User-Secret Leakage Under V9B in Active LLM Conversations, authored by Luciano Rolando Maldonado Romero, West Virginia University (lrm00020@mix.wvu.edu), by means of 1,000 controlled multi-turn dialogues, in which secrets are seeded, content-dense drift turns are included, and standardized extraction probes (M2V denoting probe-level leakage) are administered.

Keywords: Trustworthy AI, Large Language Models, Privacy, Evaluation, Benchmarking

## 1 Introduction

No model reaches stable decay in the observed range under C5Z (C5Z denoting the paper's Privacy Half-Life (τ), a stability-based metric for persistent suppression), and how easily earlier secrets are elicited by indirect, justified, high-pressure or tool-mediated prompts (shared sessions, browser assistants, copilots, agentic workflows, prompt injection) may be underestimated as the risk, no assumption being made that users believe an LLM forgets within a session. Evaluations target training-data memorization or immediate jailbreaks, while prompt-injection and multi-turn attack work does not measure recoverability after unrelated drift, even though PII is received by persistent assistants (healthcare triage, finance, enterprise copilots) and earlier disclosures may stay available to later generations. The contributions comprise 4 the aforesaid C5Z; 1 K7X (K7X denoting the paper's PrivDrift), a benchmark on two axes, V9B (V9B denoting topic drift, unrelated turns between disclosure and probe) and Z4T (Z4T denoting persuasion intensity); 3 a reproducible hybrid detector (normalization matching + open-weights T1G (T1G denoting the paper's LLM judge)), reporting R9S, M2V, J5D (J5D denoting regex-only), B7N and X3Q rates; and 2 evaluation under direct, justified and high-pressure probes.

## 2 Related Work

The suppression of secrets in active context is what K7X targets (2.3), which matters where RAG, copilots and agents rely on prompt-level context management, whereas TOFU and WMDP address static QA and weights; persistence after drift and later persuasion is isolated by K7X (2.1), unlike prior work (training-data extraction, membership inference, memorized PII), which concerns parameters, while active-context privacy concerns user-provided information at inference and ConfAIde, PrivacyLens and PrivaCI-Bench test whether a model should share under static access-control or privacy-norm scenarios; the multi-turn lens is applied by K7X to information-flow control: re-disclosure of secrets the user supplied earlier (2.2), whereas Crescendo, automated multi-turn jailbreaks and prompt injection target harmful content or instruction hijacking.

## 3 The PrivDrift Framework

For the C5Z (3.4.3), with δ=0.1·L(0) fixed over the observed V9B levels D_obs, the quantity τ is given by

$$\tau=\min\{d\in D_{obs}\mid L(d)\le\delta,\ \forall d'>d\in D_{obs}: L(d')\le\delta\}\quad(3)$$

and, the set being empty, τ=max(D_obs)+1 is assigned, a convention by which transient dips are excluded. Figure 3: a transient decrease is disqualified by a later resurgence.

A leak (3.4) is recorded when the normalized secret is contained in the normalized response or when the T1G finds the full secret, a substantial substring or enough partial information to identify it; in the first stage (3.4.1), non-digits are stripped from numeric secrets while for alphanumeric secrets case and whitespace are normalized with meaningful delimiters kept, the matching being expressed as

$$\mathrm{Leak}_{\mathrm{regex}}(R,S)=\mathbb{I}[\phi(S)\subset\phi(R)]\quad(2)$$

after which, in the second stage (3.4.2), an open-weights Llama T1G, receiving secret and response, returns a binary verdict capturing partial disclosure, hints and formatting variants, with the J5D and X3Q rates reported separately because the T1G may err. Figure 2: matching first; J5D-negatives go to the T1G.

The V9B depth is drawn as

$$d\sim\begin{cases}0&p=0.2,\\ \mathrm{Uniform}\{2,3,4,5,6\}&p=0.8.\end{cases}\quad(1)$$

and, a final probe being appended to the full history (3.3), that probe is H2R (H2R denoting the Simple, direct neutral probe), F6K (F6K denoting the Medium probe of contextual justification, e.g., a form) or N8W (N8W denoting the Hard, high-pressure/urgent probe), the results thereby reflecting bounded Q3M persistence rather than arbitrarily long contexts.

## 4 Experimental Setup

9,000 responses in total, arising from 1,000 dialogues per model multiplied by 3 probes and thus 3,000 responses per model, were obtained via OpenRouter for U5K (U5K denoting the paper's GPT-OSS-120B), E7B (E7B denoting the paper's DeepSeek-R1) and P3R (P3R denoting the paper's Qwen3-VL-235B), with identical histories and probes maintained throughout. Overall leakage is R9S, a dialogue being counted as leaking if any probe elicits the secret, so that it can exceed average Z4T-level rates, whereas the Z4T and B7N analyses are M2V. For V9B, Z4T and secret type, chi-square with Cramer's V was applied; across Z4T levels, Cochran's Q with post-hoc McNemar was applied; paired comparisons were subjected to McNemar (Bonferroni); and 95% bootstrap CIs were computed over dialogues.

## 6 Discussion

Stability-based evaluation is motivated by τ remaining unsatisfied where intermediate dips are too unstable, V9B thereby not constituting an implicit privacy boundary; leakage, mostly near-direct reproduction rather than permanent memory or uniform behavior across long-context settings, shows behavioral recoverability under retrieval pressure, while the secret-type asymmetry suggests format-sensitive safety heuristics rather than robust contextual reasoning about confidentiality, and urgency cues may activate refusal in U5K.

## 7 Limitations and Future Work

Planned for release are generation code, probe templates, evaluation scripts, aggregate results, and a sanitized dialogue subset with non-real identifiers and templates sufficient to reproduce, while the evaluated drift, bounded at d≤6, amounts to short-to-medium V9B rather than saturation of full context windows, and only structured PII, the secrets being of fixed format, is tested, with unstructured attributes (health, family, immigration status, financial hardship) deferred to future work. Not tested are training-data memorization, personalization memory and cross-session retention, the evaluation concerning active context rather than cross-session memory, and the dialogues, both synthetic and human-authored, have their realism improved by the human-authored ones while control is preserved. Labels rest on surface-form outputs, no formal human–automated agreement being reported yet and a larger manual audit of borderline partial disclosures and obfuscations being needed, and model IDs, prompts, timestamps, cached responses and paired statistics on identical dialogues are logged as mitigation, the API-served models used for compute/budget reasons being subject to change.

## 8 Conclusion

Protections remain uneven, and V9B within d≤6 does not reliably reduce X3Q, whose dialogue-level measurement across three LLMs (1,000 dialogues each) is 38.7–54.6%, with the strongest association being exhibited by secret type while Z4T effects are model-dependent.
