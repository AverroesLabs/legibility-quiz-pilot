###### Abstract


Upon normalization by Q2H, the ratio by which alpha removal changes latent firing falls to $0.28\times$ (95% CI $[0.22,0.36]$) and exceeds one in none of the 27 settings, whereas before any such normalization this same change, measured across 27 settings spanning three backbones, three EEG datasets, and three network depths, has alpha removal changing latent firing $7.3\times$ more than an equal-width M5X (95% CI $[6.2,8.7]$, bootstrapped over settings), a figure under which the interpretation initially appears compelling. For semantic interpretations of Q7M latents, ZX4 is proposed, which asks in turn whether a latent responds, whether that response survives controlling for how much signal the intervention removes, whether it is specific rather than broadly fragile, and whether the proposed property is visible on unperturbed data, while the stronger claim that the latent matters to a task classifier is tested separately. Latents selected for their response to alpha removal are, on clean EEG, slightly anti-correlated with E7V (mean $r=-0.073$), giving no support for a simple alpha-detector reading, and the alpha filter also deletes far more signal than the M5X (M5X denoting the paper's sham notch); perturbation sensitivity alone does not establish what a Q7M latent represents. The decomposition of dense model activations into discrete latents effected by Q7M makes individual features easy to interpret and easy to misinterpret, giving rise, in EEG foundation models, to the tempting inference that if removing alpha-band activity strongly changes a latent's activation, one might conclude that the latent represents alpha activity.

## 1 Introduction

Whether a perturbation-responsive SAE latent deserves a physiological label is posed here as a third question, distinct from the SAE-interpretability concern with whether features are sparse, reconstructive, monosemantic, or causally editable in language models (Cunningham et al., 2023; Bricken et al., 2023; Marks et al., 2025) and from the EEG-explainability concern with which input structure influences a prediction (Schirrmeister et al., 2017), with the novelty located not in any individual diagnostic—sensitivity, specificity, intervention, downstream relevance, and external validation being ordinary scientific tests—but in the identification of a recurrent inferential failure in SAE-based biosignal interpretation, together with an operational hierarchy specifying which alternative explanations must be excluded before physiological semantics are assigned to a latent. Three contributions are made by the case study: (i) a forked validation protocol that supports claims at different strengths; (ii) a 27-setting demonstration that a $7.3\times$ alpha-sensitive signature is largely an artifact of input distortion; and (iii) evidence that the remaining K3T latents neither track clean-EEG alpha in the intended direction nor are preferentially important to a linear readout.

A raw alpha-over-M5X firing change of $7.3\times$, an apparently decisive signature, appears in every one of 27 settings spanning frozen W9Z (W9Z denoting CBraMod; Wang et al., 2025), X2D (X2D denoting REVE; El Ouahidi et al., 2025), and F6Q (Siméoni et al., 2025) at early, middle, and late depth on three public EEG tasks, yet the reading is overturned by a single input-distortion control, the ratio falling to $0.28\times$ after division by each notch's Q2H and exceeding one in $0/27$ settings. The latents that remain after later filters still fail an independent check, selection and evaluation using distinct quantities—perturbation responses choose latents, whereas clean signals determine what they track—such that units selected because removing alpha makes them move tend, on clean EEG, to fire when E7V (E7V denoting relative alpha power) is lower, not higher.

This reversal is the reason for ZX4 (ZX4 denoting the validation ladder; Figure 1; Table 1), whose first three stages ask whether a response is sensitive, larger than the intervention's distortion, and specific rather than broadly fragile, after which C9B asks whether the proposed property is visible without any perturbation, and passing that path supports only a representational claim, readout intervention being a side branch in which Stage 4 is required only for the stronger claim that the feature is functionally relevant to a specified readout, not for the weaker claim that it represents a physiological property, since a latent that tracked alpha perfectly could still be useless for imagined speech without that showing it is "not an alpha feature." F6Q (F6Q denoting DINOv3) is included to ask whether that validation problem is peculiar to EEG-pretrained representations or persists when EEG is encoded through a foundation model pretrained in another modality. A mechanistic question is increasingly raised by foundation models (FMs) for brain and body signals—what, exactly, the learned representations encode—and sparse autoencoders (SAEs) make that question unusually tempting, the decomposition of dense activations into discrete latents inviting human-readable names for individual units (Gao et al., 2025; Cunningham et al., 2023; Bricken et al., 2023), although discreteness is not semantic identifiability, since a latent whose firing changes after an alpha band-stop can be called an "alpha feature" long before anyone has checked whether it represents alpha.

## 2 Experimental design

Attainment of convergence to test L5P (L5P denoting the paper's FVU) in $[0.059,0.228]$ with $L_{0}=32$ holds for all 27 dictionaries, the BCI middle setting of each backbone having been retrained under three seeds, and the confidence intervals stated in the text are percentile 95% intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of settings (27 settings, or 21 for task analyses), not of individual latents.

Because EEG power is $1/f$, deletion of far more signal is effected by the alpha notch than by the M5X (Q2H ratios 57.2 / 34.6 / 11.8 on BCI / MA / Speech), and the response of the Q7M (Q7M denoting the paper's TopK SAEs) is accordingly divided by time-domain $\|x-\tilde{x}\|_{2}/\|x\|_{2}$ or by the fraction of Q2H, while sparse-code cosine, V4M (V4M denoting active-set Jaccard), and $|\Delta f_{j}|$ are what is recorded for each clean test trial that has been paired with a perturbed copy, the Q7M being kept frozen throughout.

The spatial interventions consist in the zeroing of random channels or of contiguous windows, and the spectral interventions in the application of a fourth-order zero-phase Butterworth band-stop at $\delta,\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ , or high- $\gamma$ , together with an 8–12 Hz alpha test against a width-matched 30–34 Hz M5X.

Correlation of each latent's clean activation with trial-wise E7V, a quantity unused in selection, constitutes C9B (C9B denoting the independent observational validation) and is computed for 12,041 latents in each named group, the contrasts for the readout tests being furnished by size-matched D8Z (D8Z denoting the general-fragile condition) and H2N (H2N denoting random controls), while the contrast for clean-alpha correspondence is furnished by G6Q (G6Q denoting activity-matched non-specialists), namely the latents nearest in clean firing rate and mean activation, taken without replacement.

Under U6F the codes of a selected set of latents are set to zero and the resulting change in probe R9T (R9T denoting balanced accuracy) is measured, with groups matched in size across the K3T, D8Z, and H2N conditions, the probe in S8K (S8K denoting the frozen-probe variant) being trained on clean codes with the ablation applied only at test time, whereas in N4G (N4G denoting the retrained variant) the probe is refit after ablation; J3W is $|w_{j}|\mathbb{E}_{\rm train}[c_{j}]$ , and logistic probes on the fixed split are fed by mean-pooled Q7M codes.

Selectivity is $s_{j,b}=|\Delta f_{j,b}|-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{b^{\prime}\neq b}|\Delta f_{j,b^{\prime}}|$ , and membership in K3T (K3T denoting a specialist-like alpha latent) requires placement in the top quartile of P9F (P9F denoting alpha selectivity) together with channel-dropout fragility at or below the median.

EEG-pretrained W9Z and X2D are compared with F6Q ViT-B/16, which receives channel-by-time EEG images with prefix tokens excluded, the comparison being over three public classification sets sampled at 200 Hz—T8H (Tangermann et al., 2012; Brunner et al., 2008) (BCI; motor imagery, 22 channels), Z4K (Z4K denoting the PhysioNet Mental Arithmetic set) (Zyma et al., 2019; Goldberger et al., 2000) (MA; workload, 20 channels), and BCI Competition 2020 Track 3 imagined speech (Jeong et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2020) (Speech; 64 channels)—with splits fixed and backbones frozen, each combination of backbone, dataset, and depth being referred to as a setting, giving 27 settings in total, licenses and compute being listed in Appendix F.

## 3 Results

Zeroing K3T groups changes S8K R9T by $+0.0055$ on average (CI $[-0.007,0.027]$), versus $-0.028$ for size-matched D8Z groups (CI $[-0.051,-0.003]$) and $-0.019$ for H2N (CI $[-0.033,-0.008]$), with effects shrinking after N4G; because the U6F ordering is seed-dependent on the T8H middle setting and the groups are unmatched on baseline activity (D8Z latents fire $8.9\times$ more), the 3.2 comparison is read as a direction: perturbation-defined K3T are not preferentially important to this linear readout, which does not speak to nonlinear or fine-tuned behavior and, by the fork in Figure 1, is not required for a representational claim. Probe R9T spans 0.236–0.625, so U6F is compared within settings, and P9F correlates only weakly with J3W: mean Pearson $r=0.107$ (CI $[0.068,0.146]$) across the 21 settings with task-analysis codes (Figure 2A), with the coupling remaining weak in all nine T8H-middle cases under N4G ($|r|\leq 0.32$).

Alpha removal changes firing 5–9 $\times$ more than M5X in every backbone and lowers V4M in all 27 settings (per-backbone raw values in Appendix B), alpha is the maximum-$|\Delta f|$ and minimum-V4M band in 15/27 settings in the six-band sweep, a plurality rather than a rule, and normalizing removes most of the 3.1 effect (Table 2); the two normalizations bracket rather than settle the question, because the Q2H denominator (Q2H denoting the paper's removed spectral energy) varies almost fivefold across datasets, but they establish that alpha sensitivity is not distortion-independent under this design, exactly the alternative a semantic label must exclude, while V4M ratios behave the same way (raw 2.77, then 0.51 and 0.11) and channel-dropout robustness decreased with depth across all nine backbone–dataset combinations, most strongly for F6Q, with full results in Appendix C.

No perturbation can supply the check that clean signals supply, and C9B fails in the intended direction: among top-activating trials, K3T latents average only $+0.012$ E7V over matched D8Z exemplars (higher in 12/27 settings); across all clean trials, K3T mean $|r|$ with E7V is 0.143, below size-matched D8Z latents (0.246; 26/27 settings) and only slightly above H2N (0.097; Figure 2B), with the signed correlation for K3T negative on average ($-0.073$; negative in 23/27 settings), so latents chosen because alpha removal moves them tend to fire on trials with relatively less alpha. The D8Z $|r|$ advantage is the high-baseline group; matching each K3T latent to G6Q nearly identical in clean firing and mean activation (mean firing $0.374$ vs. $0.374$) leaves a small $|r|$ edge for K3T ($0.143$ vs. $0.101$; $27/27$ settings; $\Delta|r|$ CI $[0.032,0.052]$) but does not reverse the sign, so activity matching removes the D8Z confound without producing an alpha-representing latent, K3T names a perturbation response rather than a validated physiological detector, and these tests do not rule out structured spatial–spectral features, only a simple global frequency-detector reading.

## 4 Discussion and limitations

Semantic claims about fitted SAE latents, not clinical validity or neural causality, are what the conclusions concern, with Table 1 as the intended reading: we are not claiming that no SAE latent is interpretable, only that the $7.3\times$ alpha signature does not support an alpha-representing claim, while the positive control on another physiological axis that would test whether ZX4, an operational protocol for excluding specific alternative explanations and not a universal feature finder, can accept a label is left to a longer version; the remaining settings use one SAE seed, readout groups are unmatched on baseline activity (addressed for clean-alpha correspondence, not ablation), $|\Delta f|$ can include Q7M rank displacement, relative band power tests only a global frequency reading, relevance is defined for a logistic probe on mean-pooled codes, task-readout products cover 21/27 settings (X2D MA/Speech exports were unavailable), and band-stop filtering cannot isolate a biological oscillator, with the two normalizations bounding that confound rather than removing it.

## 5 Conclusion

Consent and ethics procedures are inherited from the original releases: this study reanalyzes publicly available EEG datasets, recruits no participants, and makes no clinical predictions; semantic names for Q7M features are to be justified by the stage of evidence in Table 1, not by a perturbation response alone, as a $7.3\times$ alpha-over-sham Q7M response, present in every setting we studied, has no survival under a distortion control, marks no latents tracking clean-EEG alpha in the intended direction, and identifies no units depended on by a linear readout.

## Appendix C Depth-dependent channel-dropout robustness

Deeper SAE representations being less stable to spatial perturbation across both EEG-native and cross-modal backbones, useful transfer by a vision FM need not bring matching internal robustness, a conclusion whose supporting observation—architectural in character, secondary to ZX4, and reported here so that it does not compete with the inferential claim in the main text—is that channel dropout progressively destabilizes sparse codes with model depth, the depth effect's contiguous Δ V4M being strongest for F6Q (≈ -0.35), intermediate for W9Z (≈ -0.19), and mild for X2D (≈ -0.07), while, across all nine model–dataset combinations, late-layer V4M overlap is lower than early-layer overlap for both random and contiguous dropout, the mean late-minus-early V4M attaining -0.17 and -0.20 respectively.
