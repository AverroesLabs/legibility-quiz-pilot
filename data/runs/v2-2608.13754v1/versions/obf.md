# Explanation Multiplicity: Circuit-Level Interpretability

Evidence Does Not Survive Defensible Analytic Variation

Ajay Pravin Mahale

12 August 2026

###### Abstract


That the evidence fails ZK7 at every tolerance a conformity assessment body would plausibly accept is the conclusion warranted by the finding that the removal of circuit size from the claim in its entirety, with circuit size held fixed, leaves 27.1% (95% CI 0.255 to 0.286), a value still above the pre-registered threshold, and by the circuits underlying these claims being structurally near-disjoint, median pairwise Jaccard overlap 4%, and functionally uncorrelated at Cohen’s kappa 0.015, the instability thereby not being one mechanism described in different words; the derived statement flips across 73.2% of specification pairs (95% CI 0.725 to 0.738), the modal claim commanding 41.1% of the space, out of 15,840 pre-registered specifications on ML4 and the XB8 task, of which 7,561 produced a claim, while standardising the single most influential choice, the evaluation metric, leaves VX2 at 59.4%. ZK7 is given as a standalone protocol, one of the seven documented discovery objectives does not execute at all on the library’s own canonical task, and the study covering one model and one task leaves untested whether the conclusion holds at scale. The EU AI Act requires providers of high-risk systems to file technical documentation describing how the system reaches its decisions, mechanistic interpretability being the obvious source of such evidence and circuit discovery its most developed instrument, and whether that evidence survives the condition under which it would be relied upon, namely 2 competent analysts, the same system, the same tool, different defensible settings, is what is asked, a crossed grid of seven analytic axes having been pre-registered, every level taken from a published implementation, with each discovered circuit mapped through a deterministic QM3 to a structured Annex IV statement.

## 1 Introduction

Three of our pooled statistics invert, the inversion constituting a methodological result for GK5 designs generally, namely that when specifications select objects of different sizes pooled statistics can invert, and the instability thereby exhibited survives the most aggressive controls available — standardising any single axis, removing circuit size from the claim, and holding size fixed — so that the evidence, as currently produced, does not meet the standard that filing it presumes, which is neither a proposal of a better circuit discovery method nor an argument that interpretability is worthless. What is measured is how far a regulatory claim moves across the space of defensible analytic specifications, to which measurement a decision rule a standards body could use is attached in the form of ZK7 (ZK7 denoting the paper's filability criterion), $\pi^{*}\geq 1-\alpha$, a criterion a conformity procedure can apply, reported together with the measured value on a canonical task, the supporting construction being a pre-registered GK5 (GK5 denoting the paper's multiverse) over seven analytic axes of circuit discovery, with every level drawn from a published implementation, and a deterministic map from circuits to Annex IV statements. The standard in question arises from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, which requires providers of high-risk AI systems to file technical documentation, Annex IV point 2(b) asking for “the general logic of the AI system and of the algorithms”, point 2(e) asking for “an assessment of the technical measures needed to facilitate the interpretation of the outputs”, and Article 86(1) giving a person subject to certain automated decisions the right to “clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken”, these being quoted from the consolidated text as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, CELEX 02024R1689-20260727, read 11 August 2026. The technical content is offered to be supplied by mechanistic interpretability, circuit discovery — the part of the field with mature tooling — producing a subgraph said to explain a behaviour, and a provider filing a circuit-level account of why its system behaved as it did yields a filing worth something only if a second analyst, given the same system and the same tool, would file something compatible, an audit meaning that two auditors reach the same conclusion, not a definition borrowed from machine learning but what makes an audit worth commissioning.

## 4 Formalism

VX2 is not near one by construction, because $F\leq 1-1/k$ for $k$ claim classes and, with the nine classes observed here, the ceiling is 0.8889 while the observed 0.7316 is 82.3% of it, so a reported VX2 must be read against the number of classes $\varphi$ can produce; evidence satisfies ZK7 at tolerance $\alpha$ if and only if $\pi^{*}\geq 1-\alpha$, with VX2 (VX2 denoting the paper's flip rate) being the probability that two distinct specifications yield different statements and TN4 (TN4 denoting the paper's modal share) being $\pi^{*}=\max_{c}n_{c}/N$.

| | $F=1-\frac{\sum_{c}n_{c}(n_{c}-1)}{N(N-1)},$
| | (2)

In that expression $n_{c}$ counts specifications yielding claim class $c$ and $N$ is the number of specifications, and Equation 2 is the unbiased Gini–Simpson form, namely the probability that two draws without replacement differ, unit-tested against the brute-force pairwise definition over randomised cases.

| | $F_{\text{within}}(G)=1-\frac{\sum_{g}\sum_{c}n_{g,c}(n_{g,c}-1)}{\sum_{g}n_{g}(n_{g}-1)},$
| | (3)

For a set of conditioning axes $G$, this within-group form weights every pair equally rather than every group equally and reduces exactly to Equation 2 when $G$ is empty.

For two specifications, PD8 (PD8 denoting the paper's circuit instability) is given by

| | $D(s_{i},s_{j})=1-\frac{|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|}{|C_{i}\cup C_{j}|},$
| | (1)

and $\bar{J}$ is RW5 (RW5 denoting the paper's mean Jaccard similarity) over unordered pairs.

Circuit discovery returns $C(s)\subseteq E$, a subset of the model’s edge set, and for ML4 (ML4 denoting GPT-2 small) under the factorised graph with separate query, key and value inputs, $|E|=32{,}491$, confirmed from the instrument and independently from a closed-form count over the computation order in which block $b$ attention receives from $1+13b$ sources at three input slots for each of 12 heads, MLP $b$ receives from those sources plus its own block’s heads, and the output receives from all 157 non-terminal sources, while circuits touch components drawn from 144 attention heads and 12 MLPs, so 156 in total.

The $s$ appearing above is a tuple of analytic choices $s=(o,a,d,m,\tau,P,r,g)$, with $o$ the discovery objective, $a$ the ablation operator, $d$ the corruption distribution, $m$ the evaluation metric, $\tau$ the size threshold, $P$ the prompt variant, $r$ the seed and $g$ the granularity; every level of every axis is taken from a published implementation, and none was invented for this study, while $\varphi$ maps a circuit to a structured Annex IV statement.

## 8 The filability protocol

A filing that cannot state its own $\pi^{*}$ has not been audited but has merely been asserted, and the satisfaction of ZK7 at tolerance $\alpha$ by a filed interpretability claim $c$ derived from a circuit is constituted by the inequality $\pi^{*}\geq 1-\alpha$ (obligation 4), the quantity $\pi^{*}=\max_{c}n_{c}/N$ being computed over the specification space $\mathcal{S}$ and $F$ being obtained by Equation 2 (obligation 3), with $\pi^{*}$, $F$, the EX9 per axis level and the within-group decomposition of any pooled statistic all to be reported (obligation 5), these being determinations which presuppose the prior discharge of obligations 1 and 2, namely the declaration of $\mathcal{S}$—the analytic axes and the levels of each that a competent analyst could defend, each with a citation—and the declaration of QM3 $\varphi$ as deterministic, published code and not a language model; no adjudication by the regulator of which analytic choice is correct is required by the protocol, which is the property that makes it implementable, only that the filer declare the space and report how much of it agrees.

## 9 Limitations

The measured $F$ is therefore a lower bound with respect to the field's full set of defensible choices, the bootstrap is invalid for one reported family, and a second-model replication — pre-registered but not yet run, a 924-cell grid on GF7 identical on every axis except that the seed axis carries three levels rather than five — will be reported in a revision in whichever direction it falls. Fixing the circuit size drops $F$ from 0.7316 to 0.2746, a larger reduction than standardising any pre-registered axis, and no bias-corrected estimator was constructed after the naive intervals were seen to disagree, the selection of an estimator in response to a result being the behaviour this paper documents; the quantities whose conditioning groups hold only two to five specifications, where retained self-pairs dominate and the intervals are unusable, are reported as point estimates with the diagnostic attached. The EX9 is high and uneven, 52.3%, the surviving pool is metric imbalanced, and although the BJ9 was fixed in advance and no failing item was repaired, the composition, a property of the reported set, is stated rather than adjusted for. Chosen by the BJ9, the circuit size is an outcome, not an axis, and it has distorted three separate pooled quantities — $F$ itself, the direction of the QF4 comparison, and the sign of the functional gap — which we take to be a general hazard for GK5 designs whose specifications select objects of different sizes, every pooled statistic being reported with its within-size decomposition; the GK5 is as wide as the library, not the literature, optimal ablation [5] being a defensible operator the instrument does not implement, so that it never entered the space. The confinement of the analysis to one model and one task is the limitation most likely to change the number; larger models have more components, so that both the layer band and the size class have more room, and the direction of the effect on $F$ is not obvious; the analysis plan, the reduced grid, and the criteria for what would count as replication rather than refutation were committed and timestamped before any cell of the GF7 (GF7 denoting Pythia-160m, a model of a different architecture family that performs the same task) grid ran, with a second task remaining designed and not run.

## 10 Discussion

What a regulator is licensed to take from this is not that interpretability evidence is worthless, but that its evidentiary weight, rather than being an assumed property, is a measurable one, the measurement being cheap relative to the sweep by which the circuit was produced in the first place; and this evidentiary-weight conclusion is reached despite the fact that what the standardisation of one axis buys is less than it appears, the metric constituting the biggest lever and removing 0.1377 of a 0.7316 VX2, there being no single knob, such that a procedure standardising one axis and declaring the problem solved would be mistaken by a wide margin. Of seven documented, publicly exported discovery objectives, one does not run on the library's own canonical task, a fact which is reported here as a discard together with its EX9, and which is offered as an observation about the maturity of the tooling on which regulatory evidence is expected to rest, not as the paper's headline, and not as a criticism of a library whose authors are among those calling for the field to address exactly this [10].
