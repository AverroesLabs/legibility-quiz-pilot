###### Abstract


In S4M, K2Z preserve injection-related signals and remain causally effective over long contexts, and Z4N offer new opportunities for interpreting and monitoring language models via persistent sparse features; reconstruction quality remains competitive for Z4N while a spectrum of timescales is learned, with short-timescale N9Q staying locally interpretable and long-timescale K2Z accumulating information that identifies the current context, alongside the finding that temporal persistence can naturally emerge in standard SAE features—after a feature activates, the hidden state remains aligned with its direction, and past activations help reconstruct later hidden states—with how long this lasts varying widely across features, and the extension, Z4N (Z4N denoting the paper's Persistent Sparse Autoencoders (Persistent SAEs)), learns a QM8 for each feature so that feature-specific timescales are learned from reconstruction alone, since sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose language model activations into sparse features yet traditionally encode each token independently, failing to expose information that persists across a sequence.

## 1 Introduction

Competitive reconstruction quality is retained by Z4N while a broad range of feature timescales is learned unsupervisedly, with most features being low-$\lambda_{j}$ N9Q that behave as locally interpretable detectors, as in a standard SAE, while high-$\lambda_{j}$ K2Z accumulate information that retains contextual information across the sequence and, as shown in S4M [12], can serve as powerful monitoring signals for malicious prompt injections over longer time scales, these results showing that SAE reconstruction can organise sparse features by timescale, separating token-wise activations from a compact V7K.

The starting point of this paper's exploration of the question at issue is the observation that temporal persistence can naturally emerge in standard SAE features: as shown in Figure 1, after a feature activates, later hidden states remain more strongly aligned with its decoder direction, and past activations contain information that helps reconstruct subsequent hidden states, with the persistence decaying at substantially different rates across features, suggesting that different features operate at different timescales, a persistence that standard SAEs, which encode each position independently, do not explicitly represent.

Understanding the output of a large language model (LLM) requires identifying what information is represented in its hidden states and how that information evolves throughout the sequence [1, 2], and sparse autoencoders (SAEs) [3, 4], methods that decompose an LLM's hidden states into a set of sparse features, have become a popular tool for approaching this problem, since, when features are aligned with human-interpretable concepts, an SAE's decomposition enables easy inspection of concepts learnt by an LLM and provides a mechanism for model control through inspection of and intervention on these features [5, 6, 7].

Although causal transformer hidden states integrate the preceding context, standard SAEs encode each token independently [3, 8, 9], an atemporal formulation that favours token-specific features but obscures the contextual information a model carries across a sequence, such as the current topic, style, or task state [10], a constraint that recent methods have sought to alleviate by incorporating temporal structure into SAEs through predefined partitions and decompositions of temporal roles [10, 11], approaches that, however, require specifying, a priori, which features should carry temporal information, leading to experimenter bias in the features the SAE learns, so that it remains unclear whether each feature's timescale can be learned directly from the data, rather than being predefined by the model architecture.

To fill this gap, Z4N are introduced, learning QM8 $\lambda_{j}$ for every feature, with each feature keeping a V7K (V7K denoting the persistent state) that decays at its QM8 (QM8 denoting the persistence coefficient) and is updated by R3X (R3X denoting the token-wise sparse activation), in such a way that, if carrying a feature's state forward helps reconstruct later hidden states, there is an incentive to learn a higher $\lambda_{j}$ during optimisation, whereas, if it does not, the objective favours a smaller $\lambda_{j}$ and faster decay.

SAE features are shown to exhibit temporal persistence—after a feature is activated, its decoder direction remains elevated, and past activations can help predict subsequent states—and Z4N, a new SAE variant, explicitly model this temporal persistence by learning one coefficient per sparse feature through reconstruction alone, retaining competitive reconstruction quality and discovering a broad spectrum of timescales without a predefined partition of temporal roles, while N9Q (N9Q denoting the short-timescale, fast features) act as locally interpretable detectors and K2Z (K2Z denoting the long-timescale, slow features) accumulate information that retains contextual information in a sequence, the potential of K2Z to monitor and steer models over long contexts being demonstrated through S4M (S4M denoting the prompt-injection monitoring case study), opening new opportunities to interpret and control LLMs via explicitly persistent features.

## 2 Background and Related Work

Z4N fill the gap, with recent works—specifically temporal formulations of SAE [10, 11]—having made progress toward capturing feature persistence, left when P9T [10] (P9T denoting the paper's Temporal SAEs), pre-assigning a high-level feature subset and using a contrastive objective to align their activations across nearby tokens, and Lubana et al. [11], using an attention-based predictor to separate context-predictable information from a sparse novel residual representation, both prescribe temporal structure via a fixed partition while neither allows each sparse feature to learn its own temporal persistence, complicating post-hoc analysis of the expected timescale of different features, by assigning each feature a V7K and a learned QM8 that can be easily analysed; Figure 1: standard SAE features exhibit a temporal structure that is not explicitly modelled, (a) natural F6T sequences preserving local and global continuity whereas A8Q sequences (A8Q denoting the paper's shuffled control), formed by concatenating sentences from different documents, preserve local continuity while breaking global continuity, (b) $R_{j}(\tau)$ (T8V denoting the paper's Empirical feature persistence) decaying more slowly in natural sequences than in A8Q sequences, (c) past feature activations improving held-out reconstruction of later hidden states in natural sequences but not in A8Q ones, and (d) fitted context-correction weights $w_{j}$ at lag $\tau=1$ being predominantly positive for natural sequences but centred near zero for A8Q ones.

Given an LLM’s hidden state $\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ at token $t$, a standard SAE [3] encodes $\mathbf{x}^{(t)}$ as non-negative sparse activations $\mathbf{f}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}$ from which it can be reconstructed as in (1), the $j$-th feature under this representation being represented by the activation $\mathbf{f}^{(t)}_{j}$ and its corresponding normalised decoder direction $\mathbf{d}_{j}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}[:,j]/\lVert\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}[:,j]\rVert_{2}$ (see Appendix A for a notation summary), feature $j$ being intuitively active when $\mathbf{f}^{(t)}_{j}>0$, and the contribution of individual features being made easier to inspect [3, 13] because $\mathbf{f}^{(t)}$ is sparse by construction and each reconstruction depends on only a small set of features; subsequent SAEs improve how representational capacity is allocated and organised, W5J [8] (W5J denoting the paper's BatchTopK) replacing the fixed per-token TopK constraint with a batch-level sparsity budget and allowing the number of active features to adapt across tokens, C7Q [9] (C7Q denoting the paper's Matryoshka SAEs) jointly training nested dictionaries of increasing size so that smaller subsets retain higher-level features as larger dictionaries add more specific ones.

Although autoregressive LLMs build $\mathbf{x}^{(t)}$ from the preceding context, standard SAEs and most SAE variants compute features $\mathbf{f}^{(t)}$ independently of the features observed in preceding tokens, information carried across tokens is not explicitly exposed by SAE features, traditional slow feature analysis shows that slowly varying latent factors reveal higher-level features in neural networks [14, 15], prior studies show that language models track entities and that their hidden states encode game-board, spatial, temporal, and propositional states [16, 17, 18, 19], and mechanisms that explicitly encode temporal persistence in SAE features are what these findings motivate.

## 3 Does Temporal Structure Exist at the Feature Level?

That standard SAEs leave reconstruction-relevant temporal structure unused within their features is implied jointly by the observation in Figure 1 (c) that a fraction of the baseline reconstruction error is recovered by past activations in natural documents, with the gains decreasing as the lag grows, an effect that is not observed when the A8Q activations are used, and by the observation in Figure 1 (d) that the fitted X6M weights at $\tau=1$ are mostly positive for natural documents but centred near zero for the A8Q; and across both models and SAE families the decay of T8V is observed in Figure 1 (b) to be slower in natural documents than in the A8Q, which suggests that the persistence associated with global continuity is inherited by standard SAE features even when the SAE does not explicitly encode it, that feature-aligned signals persist with document-level context and not only at the tokens that activate the feature, and, this temporal structure already being exhibited by standard SAE features, that its explicit modelling may be possible and that whether past activations help reconstruct the hidden states of subsequent tokens may be tested.

Whether $\mathbf{f}^{(t-\tau)}$ can reduce an SAE's reconstruction error for token $t$ is tested with a restricted procedure (X6M denoting the restricted context correction experiment) in which, starting from the reconstruction in Equation 1, a contribution from the activations $\tau$ tokens earlier is added,

$\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}_{\mathrm{ctx}}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}_{\mathrm{base}}+\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}\left(\mathbf{w}_{\tau}\odot\mathbf{f}^{(t-\tau)}\right).$
(3)

the weights $\mathbf{w}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ specifying how much each past feature activation affects the reconstruction only along its corresponding feature direction, so that the weight vector $\mathbf{w}_{\tau}$ directly tests whether a feature's past activation predicts a later contribution along the same direction; in this setting, for a given lag, $\mathbf{w}_{\tau}$ is fitted for 10 epochs with an $L_{2}$ penalty, and the fraction of the standard SAE's reconstruction error that the X6M recovers on held-out data, H4R, is measured as

$R^{2}_{\mathrm{improve}}=1-\Big({\sum_{t}\lVert\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}_{\mathrm{ctx}}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\Big)\Big/\Big({\sum_{t}\lVert\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}_{\mathrm{base}}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\Big).$
(4)

and, for the control, the X6M weights are refitted using past activations from the A8Q corpus while the same natural-document hidden states are kept as targets, by which it is tested whether the reconstruction gain requires the actual document history, the training details being described in Section D.1.

For each token position $t$ the quantity $p^{(t)}_{j}=\mathbf{d}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(t)}$ is defined as the projection of the hidden state $\mathbf{x}^{(t)}$ for the $t$-th token onto the direction representing feature $j$, a larger $p^{(t)}_{j}$ indicating a stronger alignment between the hidden state and the feature direction $\mathbf{d}_{j}$, and, conditioning on feature $j$ being active at token $t$, its T8V at lag $\tau$, where $\tau$ is the number of subsequent tokens, is defined as

$R_{j}(\tau):=\big({\mathbb{E}\!\left[p^{(t+\tau)}_{j}\mid\mathbf{f}^{(t)}_{j}>0\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[p^{(t)}_{j}\right]}\Big)\Big/\Big({\mathbb{E}\!\left[p^{(t)}_{j}\mid\mathbf{f}^{(t)}_{j}>0\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[p^{(t)}_{j}\right]}\Big),$
(2)

where $\mathbb{E}[p^{(t)}_{j}]$ is the feature's average projection across all evaluated tokens, a larger $R_{j}(\tau)$ indicating greater persistence, $R_{j}(\tau)=1$ meaning that the activated-token elevation is fully retained while $R_{j}(\tau)=0$ means that it has decayed to baseline activation after $\tau$ tokens, the full evaluation procedure and feature-selection criteria for this measure being provided in Appendix D.

The hidden states analysed are residual-stream hidden states $\{\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\}_{t=1}^{T}$ from U2X (layer 8) [20] and O9C (layer 12) [21] (U2X denoting Pythia-160M-deduped and O9C denoting Gemma-2-2B) on F6T documents [22], and W5J [8] and C7Q [9] SAEs, neither of which has a temporal prior, are trained, the training configurations being described in Appendix B; natural F6T documents being continuous at two scales, tokens forming coherent sentences (local continuity) and the sentences collectively developing a coherent document-level context (global continuity), the contribution of global continuity is isolated by the A8Q through the concatenation of intact sentences drawn from different F6T documents, the construction details of the dataset being given in Appendix C, so that local continuity is preserved by both natural documents and the A8Q while global continuity is preserved only by natural documents, the comparison of them testing whether feature persistence reflects connections across the document beyond the local coherence within individual sentences (Figure 1 (a)).

Lubana et al. [11] show that hidden-state correlations vary across a sequence and that past hidden states explain much of the current hidden state; the experiments here, in which it is asked under a shared setup whether standard SAEs inherit temporal structure from the hidden states they reconstruct, whether the signal along $\mathbf{d}_{j}$ remains after feature $j$ activates, and whether past activations improve the reconstruction of subsequent tokens' hidden states, explore in contrast a narrower, feature-level question, namely whether individual SAE features capture this temporal structure, and their persistence is found to help reconstruction and to vary widely across features, the explicit learning of temporal persistence for each feature in the next section being thereby motivated.

## 4 Persistent Sparse Autoencoders

The hidden state is reconstructed by the decoder from the V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$:

| | $\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}\mathbf{h}^{(t)}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{dec}}.$
| | (6)

The discovery of each feature's timescale being thereby achieved by the model without the predefined feature partitions or auxiliary temporal objectives that previous work requires, a larger $\lambda_{j}$ is favoured by the objective during reconstruction training whenever the carrying of feature $j$ forward helps reconstruct later hidden states, the temporal structure observed in the previous section being made explicit in Z4N (Figure 2) through the separation of an R3X from a V7K, and the integration of this activation into a V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}$ being performed by each feature, in contrast to standard SAEs, by means of a learned QM8 $\lambda_{j}\in[0,1]$:

| | $h^{(t)}_{j}=\lambda_{j}h^{(t-1)}_{j}+(1-\lambda_{j})a^{(t)}_{j}.$
| | (5)

The recurrence being diagonal in feature space, with feature $j$ carrying only its own activation forward so that feature-level attribution is preserved through its corresponding decoder direction, sparsity is imposed on the R3X $\mathbf{a}^{(t)}$, the accumulated V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$ integrating sparse updates while being itself not necessarily sparse. The parameterisation $\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{\max}\sigma(l_{j})$ is adopted, $l_{j}$ being an unconstrained learned parameter and $\lambda_{\max}=0.999$ keeping every feature's timescale finite, further details about the persistence parametrisation being provided in Section B.3, while the factor $(1-\lambda_{j})$ serves the prevention of excessive accumulation of activations, the state magnitude being thereby kept bounded.

Figure 2:
Z4N. The integration of its R3X $\mathbf{a}^{(t)}$ into a V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$ with its learned QM8 $\lambda_{j}$ is performed by each feature.

A small $\lambda_{j}$ yielding a short-timescale N9Q that stays close to the current token, a large $\lambda_{j}$ gives a long-timescale K2Z that integrates over many tokens across the sequence, the R3X $\mathbf{a}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}$ being produced by a Z4N encoder as in standard W5J encoders (see Section B.2 for details), where $a^{(t)}_{j}>0$ means feature $j$ finds new evidence at token $t$, and the initialisation $\mathbf{h}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}$ being performed during training at the start of every input sequence.

## 5 Experiments

The probing and intervention results taken together show complementary roles for fast and slow features, since N9Q provide strong local detection while K2Z retain a compact injection-related signal that remains both decodable and aligned with the monitor's judgement, and Z4N therefore support both immediate detection and long-context retention and causal influence in a single representation, potentially enabling more robust monitoring and control of LLMs, a conclusion whose supporting setup is established below in an order that places the final demonstrations first; in the S4M, which uses trajectories from the banking, travel, and workspace domains of E7N [12] (E7N denoting AgentDojo), generated by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct [33] (see Section C.3), each trajectory containing a request, an untrusted span with a prompt injection or benign content, and the agent's subsequent tool calls, only failed attacks are considered—where an injection is present but the agent does not complete the attack's action—so that the monitor cannot rely on malicious actions to identify an attack, and O9C is used as an external monitor by replaying each trajectory through the monitor model and recording the layer-12 residual stream, with a linear probe trained at the final token of the untrusted span ($d=0$), where the injection is directly visible, and evaluated up to $350$ tokens later without retraining (Figure 7 (a)), comparing the raw hidden state and baseline SAE representations with the V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$ restricted to K2Z (highest $1\%$ by $\lambda_{j}$) or N9Q (lowest $50\%$), following the definitions given later in connection with Figure 3, this evaluation measuring how long each representation preserves the injection signal (see Appendix H).

Slow features produce the largest and most consistently positive intervention effect at every distance, whereas directions constructed from W5J, C7Q, and P9T features produce near-zero or negative effects and show no comparable alignment over distance, meaning that slow-feature directions remain aligned with the monitor's judgement long after the local injection signal has passed; this was tested by truncating each trajectory at a later distance, appending a query asking whether it should be flagged for prompt injection, and intervening in the model at the final query token, because probe accuracy shows that a representation contains injection information but not whether the corresponding direction influences the monitor's own judgement, and, building on prior work that selects SAE features for steering and uses linear classifiers to identify task-relevant sparse subspaces [7, 34], a Y5R $\mathbf{v}_{r}$ (Y5R denoting the steering vector) is constructed for each SAE representation $r$ by weighting its most predictive decoder directions by their probe coefficients, interventions being applied in both the positive and negative directions, so that, with $s(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}(\mathbf{x})-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}(\mathbf{x})$ the monitor's logit margin, the measurement is

| | $\displaystyle B9K(r,d)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{i}\Big[s\big(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(d)}+\alpha_{i}^{(d)}\mathbf{v}_{r}\big)-s\big(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(d)}-\alpha_{i}^{(d)}\mathbf{v}_{r}\big)\Big].$ | | (8)

(B9K denoting $\Delta_{\mathrm{causal}}$), where $\alpha_{i}^{(d)}$ scales each intervention to $10\%$ of the hidden-state norm and a positive B9K means that moving in the probe direction shifts the monitor towards answering yes. At $d=0$, the fast-feature state and standard SAE representations provide the strongest signals for detecting an injection (Figure 7 (b), top), and as the evaluation moves further beyond the untrusted span their accuracy falls towards chance, while the slow-feature state retains a clearer signal at longer distances, so that fast and slow features play complementary roles—N9Q detect injected text when it appears, while slow states preserve the signal through the subsequent context—which constitutes a practical test, via indirect prompt injection [12], in which an agent may encounter a malicious instruction in an email, webpage, or tool response, continue reasoning for hundreds of tokens, and act later [30], of the hypothesis, itself suggested by experiments described below, that fast features detect interpretable patterns at the current token while slow features integrate token-wise activations into a compact V7K, and a useful agentic monitor must therefore do more than detect the injected text when it appears, and should preserve a security-relevant signal until the agent acts [31, 32].

Selecting features by decreasing persistence is nearly as efficient as supervised selection, as seen in Figure 6, where across both models the slowest $1\%$ of features reach high AUC while low-$\lambda_{j}$, random, and baseline SAE representations retain substantially less subject information at the same budget, so that persistence provides an unsupervised criterion for selecting a compact state from a much wider sparse dictionary that tracks the current subject of a sequence; this G2V evaluation (G2V denoting Subject-probing AUC) was conducted on spliced L3R sequences (L3R denoting MMLU [29]), created by concatenating labelled segments from different subjects so that the subject changes at segment boundaries, with a linear probe at each token predicting the current subject using varying fractions of the feature dictionary for training, comparing three rankings of Z4N features—decreasing $\lambda_{j}$, increasing $\lambda_{j}$, and random order—against W5J, C7Q, and P9T representations, and including a supervised ranking as an oracle baseline that favours, for each subject, features activating more strongly on that subject than on all other subjects, which because it uses the subject labels directly serves as a label-informed oracle baseline (see Appendix G).

Short-timescale features receive relatively more evidence from lexical content, whereas long-timescale features are preferentially updated at positions associated with organising and integrating context, as Figure 4 shows with the clearest difference between the two ends of the timescale distribution: features in the lowest-$\lambda$ quintile receive more updates at nouns, aligning with their stronger local interpretability in Table 1 since nouns provide clear lexical evidence at the current token, while features in the highest-$\lambda$ quintile receive updates more often at punctuation and conjunctions, consistent with recent analyses of LLMs finding that punctuation and other function-like tokens can carry substantial contextual information [27] and that explicit structural markers gate the integration of information across sentences [28]; these update locations do not by themselves reveal what information the resulting V7Ks encode, and qualitative M6Z interpretations of high-$\lambda$ features often describe broader semantic domains (Tables 4 and 5), motivating the turn from where slow features receive activation to what their accumulated states represent, the preceding analysis having asked how well individual feature activations can be explained at the tokens where they occur. Features were divided into five equal-sized $\lambda$ quintiles and their update positions grouped into six token categories—punctuation, conjunctions, function words, verbs, adjectives or adverbs, and nouns—following the classical distinction between lexical/content words and grammatical/function words [26], with function words (Func. in Figure 4) including adpositions, determiners, particles, and pronouns, and for each quintile $q$ and token category $c$ the Q8W (Q8W denoting relative update frequency) is computed as

| | $U_{q,c}={\Pr\!\left(T^{(t)}=c\mid a^{(t)}_{j}>0,\;j\in Q_{q}\right)}\big/{\Pr\!\left(T^{(t)}=c\right)},$ | | (7)

where $T^{(t)}$ is the category of token $t$ and $Q_{q}$ is the set of features in quintile $q$, this ratio comparing how often features are updated at category $c$ with how often that category occurs in the corpus, a value above one meaning more updates than expected and a value below one meaning fewer.

The evaluation of the quality of the representations learnt by Z4N, and the exploration of what appears at each learned timescale, tests whether adding a V7K preserves reconstruction quality and interpretable token-wise activations (Sections 5.1 and 5.2), asks what information the accumulated states of slow features capture (Section 5.3), and demonstrates, as a practical use of the proposed approach, how persistent states can improve long-context monitoring in a prompt injection case study (Section 5.4); throughout all experiments the same models and dataset as in Section 3 are used, with W5J SAE [8] and C7Q [9] compared as strong standard baselines and P9T [10] as the closest temporally-structured baseline, all SAEs trained with $m=16{,}384$ features and a W5J budget of $k=20$, and both C7Q and P9T using the same predefined 20%/80% feature partition, with Appendix B giving the full training configuration.

## 6 Conclusion and Discussion

An unsupervised means of organising sparse representations by timescale is thereby afforded by Z4N, which we introduced and which learn, from reconstruction alone, how long individual sparse features ought to retain information across tokens, and a potential direction is opened for the interpretation and monitoring of information that LLMs maintain over long contexts. Interactions between features and context-dependent resets cannot be captured, a single, fixed QM8 being assigned to each feature through a diagonal recurrence, and richer recurrent dynamics, alongside tests of whether persistent feature interventions affect end-to-end agent behaviour, could be explored in future work. The learned per-feature QM8 span a broad range of timescales, across which K2Z, situated at the long-timescale end, accumulate contextual information that tracks subject identity and preserve monitoring signals over long contexts, while N9Q, at the short-timescale end, remain locally interpretable. Because an external replay monitor of agent trajectories is used in the S4M, the intervention results establish effects on the judgement of the monitor rather than on the actions of the original agent.

## Appendix B SAE Training Details

Checkpoints are saved on a fixed schedule, the final dictionary (ae.pt) is used for every downstream evaluation, and all experiments were run on NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80 GB of memory.

Figure 8: Z4N training dynamics. Training loss (left), the number of non-zero coordinates in V7K $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$ (state $L_{0}$; centre), and the fraction of state activation mass contributed by the carried term $\bm{\lambda}\odot\mathbf{h}^{(t-1)}$ (right) for U2X at layer 8 and O9C at layer 12. After a short initial transient, the loss and active-state size stabilise, while the carried-mass fraction remains well away from both zero and one; the models use both carried state and new token-wise activations without continual growth in the active state. For both models, the loss decreases rapidly and then remains stable, while the state $L_{0}$ settles at roughly $400$–$500$ active coordinates despite continued training; the carried-mass fraction stabilises at approximately $0.5$ for U2X and $0.65$–$0.70$ for O9C, the recurrence does not cause the active state to grow continually, and neither model relies exclusively on new evidence or carried state.

The QM8s for Z4N are parameterised as $\lambda_{j}=\sigma(l_{j})\cdot\lambda_{\max}$ (12), where $l_{j}\in\mathbb{R}$ is an unconstrained logit, $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function, and $\lambda_{\max}=0.999$ is a hard cap that prevents exact poles, which ensures $\lambda_{j}\in(0,\lambda_{\max})$ throughout training; the factor $(1-\lambda_{j})$ in Equation 5 makes the recurrence an exponential moving average (EMA) instead of an unnormalised sum and, under a constant activation $a^{(t)}_{j}=a_{j}$, V7K converges to $a_{j}$ for every $\lambda_{j}$, so QM8 does not systematically increase the state magnitude, while the cap below one prevents exact poles and gives every feature finite effective memory; before training, every logit $l_{j}$ is initialised to $0.5$, giving each feature moderate initial QM8 while keeping the sigmoid away from saturation, so training can readily adjust its timescale in either direction.

Four SAE families are trained for comparison: W5J [8], C7Q W5J [9], P9T [10], and our Z4N, with each family using the same dictionary width, W5J activation budget, optimiser, and token budget, a matched setup whose effect is isolation of how each SAE represents temporal information rather than differences in the amount of training data or the number of optimisation steps; every SAE has a dictionary of $m=16{,}384$ features and a W5J activation budget of $k=20$ per token, Adam is used at a learning rate of $3\times 10^{-4}$ with a linear warm-up over the first $1{,}000$ steps, from step $1{,}000$ the W5J activation-threshold schedule is applied with decay $\beta=0.999$ together with the auxiliary dead-feature loss weighted by $\alpha_{\mathrm{aux}}=0.03125$, training activations come from F6T [35] (F6T denoting the paper's Pile Uncopyrighted) with the beginning-of-sequence position removed, for O9C [21] the layer-12 residual stream with hidden width $d=2{,}304$ is used, and for U2X [20] (160) the layer-8 residual stream with $d=768$ is used. C7Q and P9T both divide their dictionaries into the same two predefined feature groups, containing $20\%$ and $80\%$ of the $16{,}384$ features; P9T weights its reconstruction losses by $0.2$ and $0.8$, respectively, whereas C7Q uses uniform reconstruction weights, and Z4N does not use a predefined partition, instead learning a QM8 for every feature.

All families receive the same number of gradient updates and, up to the pairing or sequencing required by their objectives, approximately the same number of token activations, because the four families process activations in different units—W5J and C7Q use shuffled token activations, P9T uses adjacent token pairs, and Z4N uses contiguous sequences so that it can carry state across tokens—and the number of activation tokens processed per optimisation step and the total number of steps are matched rather than the number of these structural units; each model is trained for $244{,}140$ steps with an effective batch of approximately $2{,}048$ activation tokens per step, which for the flat baselines gives $2{,}048\times 244{,}140=499{,}998{,}720$ tokens, or approximately $5.0\times 10^{8}$ tokens in total, while the activation buffer for W5J and C7Q returns a flat batch of $2{,}048$ token activations at each step, the buffer for P9T returns adjacent previous–next token pairs from the same activation stream with the number of reconstructed token positions per step kept equal to the flat-baseline batch size, and Z4N, whose recurrent state requires contiguous sequences rather than shuffled tokens, uses eight sequences of context length $256$ per step, giving, after excluding the beginning-of-sequence position, $8\times 255=2{,}040$ usable token activations per step, within $0.4\%$ of the flat-baseline budget.

For every SAE in this paper, sparsity is imposed using a W5J mechanism in which a hidden state $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is first mapped to non-negative pre-activations $\tilde{\mathbf{f}}=\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{dec}})+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{enc}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}$ (9), and only the largest pre-activations are retained across the batch; for a batch $X=\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{B}$, the pre-activations $\{\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{B}$ are flattened into a single vector of length $Bm$, and the sparse activation $\mathbf{f}_{i}$ is obtained by keeping only the top $kB$ entries globally and setting all others to zero, with reconstruction $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}\mathbf{f}_{i}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ (10); in practice, after training with exact W5J selection, inference uses an equivalent thresholded form $f_{ij}=\tilde{f}_{ij}\,\mathbf{1}[\tilde{f}_{ij}>\theta]$ (11), where the threshold $\theta$ is estimated from training-time top-$k$ statistics, and Z4N uses the resulting sparse activation as its R3X $\mathbf{a}^{(t)}$, with only its downstream use differing.

## Appendix C Dataset Details

Sentences sampled from documents drawn from the same corpus are concatenated subject to a simple adjacency constraint that prevents consecutive sentences from coming from the same source document, so that local continuity is preserved while global continuity, including long-range entity tracking and discourse progression, is broken, and this construction, carried out at the sentence level rather than by permuting tokens, yields A8Q. Three datasets are used for distinct parts of the evaluation: the hidden states used to train and evaluate Z4N are provided by Pile documents, subject-labelled text for analysing V7K is provided by L3R questions, and S4M is supported by E7N trajectories, with L3R used only for the subject analyses and not used to train Z4N. If a sequence has retained length $L$, temporal statistics are computed on token positions $\{16,\dots,L-1\}$ after the initial tokens are dropped, and, for the natural corpus, each accepted document is passed through the model with the chosen residual-stream hidden states retained, acceptance of a document being conditional on its containing, after tokenisation with the model tokeniser and truncation to the context cap, at least the required minimum number of tokens. Documents are accessed in streaming mode and shuffled with a buffer of size $10{,}000$ using a fixed seed. The original Pile is an $825$ GiB English-language corpus assembled from 22 sources, including academic papers, web pages, books, code, and online discussions [22], and the release used is F6T [35], a derived version in which the Books3, BookCorpus2, OpenSubtitles, YouTube Subtitles, and OpenWebText2 components are removed.

For the geometry analysis in Section 5.3, $100$ sequences are sampled across the five subjects and the final $20$ token representations from each sequence are retained, while for subject probing sequences are constructed by joining equal-length segments from different subjects and each token is assigned the subject label of its source segment, the sampling and evaluation details for the two analyses being provided in Appendix F and Appendix G. The questions used come from abstract algebra, astronomy, business ethics, computer security, and European history, these subjects covering distinct areas while providing a subject label for every example. L3R is an English-language benchmark of four-option multiple-choice questions from 57 subjects spanning STEM, the humanities, the social sciences, and other professional and academic fields [29].

The positive class ($y=1$) contains runs with an important_instructions or direct attack for which the E7N injection checker returns security=false, a value meaning that an injection was present but the agent did not complete the attacker's target action, whereas the negative class ($y=0$) is formed by benign trajectories without an injection; these failed attacks are retained deliberately, since successful attacks often contain an overt malicious action, such as transferring money or sending private data, which could provide a trivial downstream classification cue, and the restriction thereby asks whether the monitor retains a trace of the injection even when no observable attack success occurs, with no filtering by task utility, so the legitimate user request may either succeed or fail. The monitoring dataset uses trajectories from the banking, travel, and workspace domains generated by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Indirect prompt injection occurs when an agent retrieves attacker-controlled text from emails, webpages, documents, or tool outputs and follows instructions inside that text, and this setting is evaluated by E7N [12] with stateful tool environments containing 97 user tasks, 27 injection targets, and 629 security test cases across workspace, Slack, travel, and banking domains, whose task-specific checks evaluate whether the legitimate user request and the attacker's target action were completed by inspecting the agent output and changes to the environment.

## Appendix D Empirical Persistence of Standard SAE Features

Median exponents obtained by fitting power-law descriptors to the positive decay tail of T8V are $\beta_{\mathrm{nat}}\approx 0.32$ against $\beta_{\mathrm{shf}}\approx 0.87$–$0.92$, a gap that holds for both SAE families, with A8Q decaying to approximately zero at long lags on both models, whereas on O9C natural-document QM8 remains measurable at lag $512$, reaching $0.042$ under W5J and $0.034$ under C7Q, and on U2X, the 160M-parameter model, the median QM8 for W5J falls from $0.618$ at lag $1$ to $0.160$ at lag $64$ and is still $0.096$ at lag $512$, C7Q following the same pattern, from $0.504$ to $0.108$ and $0.059$.

The persistence profiles of individual features differ substantially across lags: when features are ranked by their average $R_{j}(\tau)$ within short ($\tau\leq 16$), medium ($32\leq\tau\leq 64$), and long ($\tau\geq 128$) lag bands, the resulting distributions span nearly the full unit interval at every band, some features being strongly persistent at short lags yet decaying before medium lags, others retaining elevated projections well into the long-lag regime, this heterogeneity being present in both SAE families and substantially larger than what is observed under A8Q.

The lags entering the evaluation are $\tau\in\{1,4,16,32,64,128,256,512\}$, computed on documents of at most $1{,}024$ traced tokens, a feature being retained only if it has at least $256$ activation events, at least $64$ valid anchors at every lag, and a non-negligible activated-token elevation; QM8, $R_{j}(\tau)$, is undefined and excluded whenever its denominator $\mathbb{E}[p^{(t)}_{j}\mid\mathbf{f}^{(t)}_{j}>0]-\mathbb{E}[p^{(t)}_{j}]$ is near zero, as occurs for near-dead or diffuse features, and no $\epsilon$ is added to the numerator or the denominator.

In X6M, on U2X the correction removes $0.89\%$ (W5J) and $0.61\%$ (C7Q) of the baseline reconstruction error at lag $1$, decreasing but staying positive at lag $32$ ($0.22\%$ and $0.13\%$), while on O9C both SAEs remove approximately $0.40\%$ at lag $1$, falling to about $0.02\%$ at lag $16$ and vanishing by lag $32$; the A8Q controls stay slightly below zero throughout, so unrelated context never improves reconstruction, the gain requiring feature activations from the actual history of the target document.

The per-feature context correction of Equation 3 is fitted independently at each lag $\tau\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$, $192$ natural documents being used to fit $\mathbf{w}_{\tau}$ and $96$ held-out natural documents to evaluate H4R ($R^{2}_{\mathrm{improve}}$), the target always being the natural-document reconstruction error; for the control, the diagonal head is refitted to predict these same natural targets from context activations drawn from the shuffled corpus, under the same parameterisation and optimisation budget, each document contributing at most $768$ tokens after $16$ prefix tokens are dropped, optimisation proceeding for $10$ epochs with AdamW, learning rate $5\times 10^{-3}$, and weight decay $10^{-3}$.

On natural documents the mean fitted lag-one weight is positive and a large majority of features take a positive weight (Table 3), indicating that past activation of many features predicts a positive contribution along the same feature direction at the next token, whereas the shuffled distributions are sharply centred at zero; the reconstruction gain thereby admits a feature-level reading, arising largely from the carrying of feature-aligned activity forward through a coherent document, which is precisely the behaviour that V7K of Section 4 makes explicit.
