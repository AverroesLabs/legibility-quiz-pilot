Prompt-based steering of LLMs is fragile: outputs shift under lexical perturbation, adversarial suffixes override alignment priors, and prompt steerability is asymmetric and bounded. Activation steering instead injects a steering direction into an intermediate residual stream; Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) provide an interpretable basis, decomposing activations into sparse features whose decoder directions can be amplified to promote or suppress a behavior.

Standard implementations steer every generated token, but stronger or more frequent intervention can degrade generation quality, leaving open whether persistent intervention is necessary. We ask: how much of dense steering's behavioral effect remains when only a fraction of token positions are steered? We replace deterministic every-token intervention with stochastic gating and measure behavioral and fluency effects across intervention ratios.

We introduce two stochastic interventions over SAE feature steering. Stochastic Token Steering (STS) samples an independent gate $m_t \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ per position, applying the signal to a random subset of tokens. Stochastic Block Steering (SBS) draws one $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ gate per sequence and applies it to a leading window, treating early-token influence as an atomic intervention site.

Contributions. First, dense every-token intervention is largely unnecessary: across two model families and two task types—suppression (toxicity reduction) and elicitation (emotion steering)—STS recovers most of the full-steering behavioral effect while steering only a fraction of tokens, with less fluency degradation. Second, the best-performing steering magnitude rises as the intervention ratio falls, a consistent interaction between intervention frequency and per-token strength. The method reduces inference-time control to one interpretable sparsity parameter $p$, requires no reward model or learned gating policy, and adds only an activation vector addition at inference.
