Behavioral control over large language models (LLMs) – reducing toxicity, enforcing persona, adjusting refusal – is a significant deployment concern. The default is the system prompt, but prompt-based steering is fragile: output distributions shift under minor lexical perturbation; adversarial suffixes override alignment priors; steerability is asymmetric and bounded. Activation steering injects a steering direction into an intermediate residual stream during generation. Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) decompose activations into sparse features; decoder directions can be amplified to promote or suppress a target behavior. Standard implementations apply the direction at every token, raising the question whether persistent intervention is necessary, as stronger or more frequent intervention can degrade quality. We investigate this by replacing deterministic every-token intervention with stochastic gating. Stochastic Token Steering (STS) samples an independent gate \(m_t \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\) at each position, applying the signal to a random subset of tokens. Stochastic Block Steering (SBS) draws a single \(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\) per sequence and applies it to a leading window, treating early-token influence as an atomic intervention site. Contributions: (1) Dense every‑token intervention is largely unnecessary: across two model families and two task types (toxicity suppression and emotion elicitation), STS recovers most full‑steering effect while steering only a fraction of tokens and causing less fluency degradation. (2) The optimal steering magnitude rises as the intervention ratio falls, showing an interaction between frequency and per‑token strength. The method reduces inference-time behavioral control to a single sparsity parameter \(p\), without a reward model or learned gating policy, adding only an activation vector addition during inference.
