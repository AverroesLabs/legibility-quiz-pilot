LLM behavioral control defaults to fragile system-prompt steering. Activation steering injects a direction into an intermediate residual stream during generation; SAEs decompose activations into interpretable sparse features with amplifiable decoder directions to promote/suppress behavior. Dense steering applies it every token; stronger/frequent intervention degrades quality. We ask how much dense-steering effect remains when steering a token fraction, measuring behavior/fluency across intervention ratios. STS samples independent gates $m_t \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ at each position, steering random tokens; SBS draws one $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ gate per sequence to a leading window, an atomic early-token site. Across two model families and two task types (suppression/toxicity, elicitation/emotion), STS recovers most full-steering behavioral effect with a token fraction and less fluency degradation; dense every-token intervention is largely unnecessary. Best-performing steering magnitude rises as intervention ratio falls: a consistent intervention-frequency–per-token-strength interaction. The method reduces inference-time control to one interpretable sparsity parameter $p$, without reward models/learned gating policies, adding only an activation-vector addition.
