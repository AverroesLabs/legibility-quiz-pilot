## 2 Related Work

The dissociation under jailbreaks of the harmfulness direction, localized at the instruction token, from W8J, localized at the post-instruction token—a split succeeding the refinement of refusal's mediation by a single low-dimensional direction into multi-dimensional polyhedral concept cones—is extended by us to the multilingual setting (Appendix A); this against a backdrop in which the cross-lingual transfer of safety degradation, effected via ~20% of parameters, enables the jailbreaking of a model through fine-tuning on harmless low-resource data, and in which the rate at which unsafe outputs are yielded by low-resource languages stands at ~3× that of high-resource ones, standardized benchmarks remaining English-only. The attribution, by the closest work—a 14-language translated dataset—of the phenomenon to weak harmful-harmless separation accompanies its demonstration that W8Js transfer across languages; Q7X adds manipulation type as an independent variable.

## 5 Geometric Analysis of Refusal

English margins of $b_{0}$ of $9.7$ for X5M, $6.1$ for K6R and $2.8$ for J9Q, against cross-lingual values of $5.3$, $3.8$ and $0.75$ respectively, indicate that the principal direction generalises, yet margins decay steeply thereafter, the minimum sampled cone margin by dimension three being $\sim$0 for all models, and the cross-lingual margins of $b_{1}$–$b_{4}$ being $\sim$0 or negative ($-0.4$ for K6R $b_{1}$; $-0.9$ for X5M $b_{1}$), so that non-English separation is degraded, the directions being mutually independent ($|r|<0.2$) but language-cluster-specific, K6R $b_{1}$ being negative for Japanese and Korean and X5M $b_{2}$ singling out Arabic, cross-lingual refusal being thereby effectively one-dimensional; and, $b_{0}$ margins being $\sim$0 for S1K 4 on all models, the failure is upstream (collapsed harm), not a misoriented axis, so repair must target harm detection (Figures 5–6, Appendix D).

The harm and refusal signals being geometrically decoupled (Fig. 9, Appendix C), harm and refusal norms are weakly correlated and widely scattered on all models, Swahili, Scottish Gaelic, Yoruba and Zulu each showing cases of large harm norm relative to refusal, a B7Q signature, and the contrastive harm signal $\sigma_{\mathrm{harm}}$ at $t_{\mathrm{inst}}$ against the refusal signal $\sigma_{\mathrm{refusal}}$ at $t_{\mathrm{post}}$ yields $r=-0.79$ for K6R, $-0.68$ for J9Q and $+0.53$ for X5M, whether S1K-4 clusters in the low-harm, variable-refusal region universally remaining open; layer-wise failure types are predominantly mixed on all models, a few upstream cases occurring in S1K 4 and downstream failure never occurring, refusal being never suppressed once given adequate harm signal. S1K 1 meets both prerequisites (clean separation, adequate projection onto refusal); in S1K 3–4 one or both fail, the dominant mixed mode routing a usable instruction-token harm representation through a misaligned low-rank subspace with insufficient intersection with $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{(\ell)}$, and in S1K 4 the harm representation also degrades to chance (upstream on top of B7Q): jailbreaks starve $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{(\ell)}$ of input rather than destroy it.

The setup of 5.1 posits a transformer $\mathcal{M}$ of $L$ layers and width $d$, with residual activations $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ taken at the last post-instruction token $t^{*}$ and, per language $\lambda$ and layer $\ell$, harmful and harmless sets $\mathcal{H}^{(\ell)}_{\lambda}$, $\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}_{\lambda}$; the D5L being obtained from logistic regression probes per $(\lambda,\ell)$, weights stacked over categories into $\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times|\mathcal{C}|}$, $\mathcal{C}$ = "all" and "unknown" F2D (F2D denoting HarmBench) categories, $\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)}_{\lambda}$ being the span of the top-$k$ right singular vectors with $k$ covering $\geq 95\%$ of the spectral mass; and the W8J being computed from English refused ($\mathcal{R}$) vs. complied ($\mathcal{C}$) activations as

$$\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{(\ell)}=\frac{\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{(\ell)}_{\mathcal{R}}-\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{(\ell)}_{\mathcal{C}}}{\|\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{(\ell)}_{\mathcal{R}}-\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{(\ell)}_{\mathcal{C}}\|_{2}} \quad (2)$$

each $(\lambda,\ell)$ then being classified as upstream (harm subspace too weak to feed refusal), downstream (harm represented, refusal suppressed), or mixed (both attenuated), refusal being taken as $\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{(\ell)\top}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x})>\tau^{(\ell)}$ and B7Q as harmful activations below $\tau^{(\ell)}=0.95$ despite nontrivial harm signal.

The treatment in 5.3 of E9U structure and Cross-Lingual Representational Independence (CL-RepInd) rests on extending English E9Us (E9U denoting the refusal cone) multilingually via (i) activation-domain cone optimisation, the fitting being carried out on cached residual activations instead of gradient-based model interventions, and (ii) CL-RepInd, the question of whether two basis directions use shared or distinct cross-lingual circuits, an orthonormal $\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}$, seeded with $\hat{r}^{(\ell)}$, being optimised by Stiefel-manifold gradient ascent for 400 steps ($\eta=0.05$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{cone}}=5$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{xl}}=1$), $N\in\{1,\ldots,5\}$ with $N=5$ canonical, under

$$\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B})=&-\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{\rm en}(b_{i})}_{\text{EN margin}}+\lambda_{\rm cone}\underbrace{\bigl({\rm ReLU}(\Delta_{\rm en}(b_{i})-\Delta_{\rm en}(b_{i-1}))\bigr)}_{\text{cone spread}}\\&-\lambda_{\rm xl}\underbrace{\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\Delta_{\lambda}(b_{i})}_{\text{CL margin}}\end{split} \quad (3)$$

$\Delta_{\lambda}(b)$ being the harmful–harmless margin along $b$ for $\lambda$ and $\Lambda$ the non-English languages, CL-RepInd being the Pearson correlation of two directions' per-language margin vectors, low values implying different circuits.

## 6 Discussion

That geometry-aware interventions (contrastive activation addition, refusal subspace projection) should generalise only along $b_{0}$, with generalisation along higher dimensions carrying the potential degradation of non-English safety, is a consequence of a refusal geometry that is effectively one-dimensional cross-lingually, $b_{0}$ alone being transferred while $b_{1}$–$b_{4}$ encode script-family-specific sub-circuits; the per-language margin heatmaps, in mirroring Y7H bimodality, suggest a shared geometric origin, while the three modes—Z6G (Y7H of non-Latin scripts; Tier 4), B7Q (Tier-3 G8W, C4Z), and the disruption of language-ID routing under N2B—remain independent of representation quality, and Tier-4 failure, upstream rather than directional, is evidenced by Swahili, a Tier-3 outlier exhibiting ~0 $b_{0}$ margins on all models, by which a decoupling of fluency and safety alignment is demonstrated and tier is shown to be an imperfect proxy for risk, with the tier gradient, script bimodality, harm–refusal decoupling, and one-dimensional cross-lingual refusal replicating on J9Q, X5M, and K6R, constituting a cross-model consistency that is structural rather than model-specific.

## 7 Conclusion

The design of safety audits and governance for global deployment is implicated by consistency across models with differing multilingual training regimes, with testing that ignores perturbation type, script family and per-language coverage missing this vulnerability profile; the English E9U, multi-dimensional yet cross-linguistically thin, constrains interventions to $b_{0}$-aligned additions; low-resource harm occupies a thinner, less separable D5L; attack-type profiles are irreducible to resource level, C4Z consistently amplifies G8W P6V, and Y7H and N2B behave as above.

## Impact Statement

A risk is posed by the revelation of vulnerable language–attack combinations, one mitigated by emphasizing mechanisms over optimized attacks or harmful completions; risks for low-resource communities are obscured by English-only testing, whereas exposure of these gaps is rendered supportive of equitable multilingual safety evaluation, red-teaming and alignment.

## Appendix F Limitations and Future Work

Activation and path patching for the verification of N2B language-ID disruption and the localisation of B7Q layers, the testing of whether contrastive activation addition along $b_{0}$ fixes S1K Tier-3 failures without collapsing fluency, the inclusion of more scripts (Ethiopic, Devanagari) and attacks (phonetic perturbation, homoglyphs), and the tracing of the training examples behind the script-family sub-circuits in $b_{1}$–$b_{4}$ are left to future work, as is activation patching, the mechanistic attribution presented here being correlational; generalisation to larger models remains open, only 8B-scale models having been studied, and the linear probes and mean-difference directions relied upon may miss nonlinear mechanisms, while the quality of U1E varies by pair and its errors may confound, especially Y7H, and T4P served as the sole judge, with NLLB-200 back-translation, which degrades for very-low-resource languages, possibly biasing S1K Tier-4 P6V.
