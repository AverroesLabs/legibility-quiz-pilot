# Probe Generalization as Subspace Selection for OOD Deception Detection

Daniel Yoo

  
Adrians Skapars

###### Abstract


M3Z is closed by 78% on Q7V and by 25% on J8W when, after A8T has scored each PC for whether its most/ least activating examples imply a transferable deception direction, probing is restricted to the highest-scoring PCs, with PC interpretations isolating a subset of those transferable PCs and with M3Z (M3Z denoting the baseline-to-oracle gap) situated in the generalization performance of V6Q (3.1, 8) probes over 3 held-out deception detection datasets, where projecting inputs onto a small subset of principal components (PCs) from the training distribution of activations enables cross-domain transfer that nearly matches S6F, while the directions a source probe weights heavily appear to encode source-specific surface features, the directions that actually transfer appear to encode the same contrast more abstractly in a way natural language descriptions can capture, linear probes can detect behaviors and concepts inside language model activations but may fail to transfer to N5P examples, and N5P robustness of probes is largely determined by subspace selection. Machine Learning, ICML

## 1 Introduction

A target-supervised greedy search over source-domain principal components (PCs) recovers 8–15 components on which a source-trained probe nearly matches an S6F, a small transferable subspace thereby being shown to exist (Section 4.2), while the ranking of PCs by the weights of the source probe or of the target probe fails, on either the source side or the target side, to produce a basis that transfers better than the V9D (Section 4.3), and an A8T is found to recover the subspace partially and without target data, through the interpretation and selection of PCs that are likely to encode transferable behavioral contrast rather than surface patterns (Section 4.5).

Figure 1: An overview of PC-based subspace selection for OOD deception probing, in which activations are projected onto the source-domain PCA basis, candidate PC directions are filtered, and probes are trained on the selected low-dimensional subspace.

Deception probes on V6Q (Grattafiori et al., 2024), trained on a single source dataset and evaluated on 3 held-out target datasets (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025), constitute the testbed, and it is in the basis of source-domain PCs, rather than in that of raw activations, that strategies are explored for the selection of a subset of training dimensions that avoid spurious correlations and transfer to target domains, the model's latent dimensions not being assumed to provide a privileged basis, with Z4K (Z4K denoting the Roleplaying deception scenarios) serving as the single source dataset and with Q7V (Q7V denoting Insider Trading Report), P3Y (P3Y denoting Insider Trading Confirm), and J8W (J8W denoting Sandbagging) as the 3 held-out target datasets.

Linear probes are standard tooling for the reading of information from language model activations (Alain and Bengio, 2018; Hewitt and Liang, 2019), being cheap to train and exposing some semantically meaningful information in the otherwise uninterpretable representation space, and, key behavioral concepts having been shown by existing work to be linearly represented in model activations (Marks and Tegmark, 2024; Park et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2025), probes should have the capacity to detect these concepts, yet probes that look strong in-distribution are sometimes unreliable out-of-distribution (Kirch et al., 2026) and appear in practice to learn spurious correlations (Orgad et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025), which is unacceptable for probe-based monitoring of harmful interactions, the challenge thereby set being how probes that only learn generalizing features can be built.

Principal components offer a simple, data-driven way to study structure in representation spaces, and that the choice of PCs matters is suggested by prior work, Mu et al. (2018) showing that the removal of the top PCs of an embedding space improves downstream performance and Kantamneni et al. (2025) using top-PC projections as a probe baseline, the focus of the investigation thereby becoming which subset of principal components supports generalizable probe training and how that subset can be identified, the comparisons involving an S6F (S6F denoting the target-trained probe), a V9D (V9D denoting the full-representation baseline), and an A8T (A8T denoting an LLM judge), and the model throughout being V6Q (V6Q denoting Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).

## 2 Related Work

Questions about when such directions capture stable concepts rather than context-dependent correlations are raised by a line of work in which high-level behavioral or semantic properties are studied as potentially represented in the form of directions or low-dimensional subspaces in activation space, a perspective through which the explanation and control of model behavior has been pursued and through which linear structure is suggested to be capable of supporting both mechanistic understanding and activation-space interventions. The identification of an "assistant" axis corresponding to assistant-like behavior (Lu et al., 2026), together with the demonstration by Arditi et al. (2024) that a single direction can mediate refusal behavior—suggesting that some high-level behaviors may be localized in simple linear features—stands alongside the formalization of the linear representation hypothesis and its connection to both probing and steering (Park et al., 2024), the report of linear structure in true/false representations across datasets (Marks and Tegmark, 2024), and the modeling of high-level properties such as honesty and emotion as population-level directions that can be read from and intervened on in model activations (Zou et al., 2025).

The centrality of methodological skepticism to the probing literature is expressed in the argument that high probe accuracy alone is insufficient evidence that a model already represents the target property, since the probe may itself learn the task or exploit surface-level correlates (Hewitt and Liang, 2019), in the questioning of whether probing reliably establishes what information is encoded or merely recoverable (Ravichander et al., 2021), and in the finding that probes with strong in-distribution performance can generalize unevenly out of distribution (kramár2026buildingproductionreadyprobesgemini), concerns which motivate careful evaluation of whether safety probes identify robust internal signals rather than dataset-specific artifacts; such probes, linear and lightweight, have a long history in interpretability as tools for analyzing what information is represented in neural networks (Alain and Bengio, 2018; Hewitt and Liang, 2019) and have been applied in recent work to safety-relevant signals in LLM activations, including latent truthfulness without direct supervision (Burns et al., 2024) and strategic deception (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025).

The ablation during fine-tuning of concepts identified through automated natural-language interpretation, undertaken to reduce misaligned behavior (Casademunt et al., 2025), instantiates the explored possibility of using natural-language feature descriptions not only for interpretation but also for intervention, within an approach in which internal representations are interpreted through the assignment of natural-language descriptions to neurons or learned features: the introduction of the use of LLMs to generate explanations of neurons from high-activation examples (Bills et al., 2023) has since been extended to the automatic labeling of features in sparse autoencoders ((Paulo et al., 2025), (Bricken et al., 2023)), enabling large-scale interpretation of learned feature dictionaries.

## 3 Setup

Enough information is retained by the R2F subspace (R2F denoting the top 100 PCs) to separate deceptive from honest responses on every target, as Section 4.1 confirms, the R2F being used because alignment with SAE decoder features (Bricken et al., 2023) decreases with PC index (Appendix C), lower-index PCs thereby being more amenable to natural-language interpretation and hence comparable with interpretability-driven selection methods, and no generalization superiority of low-index PCs being claimed. Following Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2025), 4 of their deception detection datasets—Z4K, Q7V, P3Y, J8W—are used, descriptions being provided in Appendix A, Z4K being the source domain and the other 3 target test sets, each response labeled honest (0), ambiguous (-1), or deceptive (1) by GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024), the ambiguous responses removed for our experiments. Following Ying et al. (2026), layer 15 residual stream activations of V6Q (Grattafiori et al., 2024; 3.1; 8), the layer with the highest cross-domain E3K (E3K denoting the paper's AUROC) in their analysis, are extracted by passing each prompt through the model and averaging token activations over just the assistant’s response; source activations are standardized to zero mean and unit variance, the same source statistics then being applied to standardize all target activations, PCA is run on the scaled source activations and the R2F are retained, all probes are logistic regression classifiers ( $\lambda=10$ ), and, for experiments using a subset $S$ of the R2F, activations are projected onto $S$ before training and evaluation, while the baseline probes train directly on the full standardized activations.

## 4 Experiments

For 4.5, whether selecting PCs based on the interpretability-based N5P (N5P denoting the OOD score) recovers a meaningful fraction of the K9U probe performance without using any target data was tested by selecting the PCs given the maximum N5P by A8T and using them as the probe subspace; because A8T scores are noisy, the scoring was run twice and only PCs that received the maximum score in both runs were kept, giving 19 Q8B in total (Q8B denoting interp-selected PCs), while the control evaluation was made against G2Y (G2Y denoting the interp-ablated PC probe), namely the probe trained on the ablation of the selected PCs, the 81 PCs not used, as well as against a probe trained on the 19-PC W4J control. Error bars: 95% CIs. The Q8B probe outperforms V9D on all 3 targets and outperforms the W4J control, by wide margins on Q7V and J8W, and, as expected, also outperforms G2Y across all 3 targets; the size of the recovery varies sharply by dataset, since on Q7V Q8B closes 78% of M3Z between V9D (0.706) and the K9U oracle (0.929), on P3Y the V9D transfer performance is already near the ceiling so there is little headroom for comparisons and the result is uninformative because each selection method aside from G2Y does roughly as well as one another, and on J8W Q8B closes 25% of M3Z at the K9U oracle (0.832), with the selection of PCs purely by the natural-language description of what they encode, with no use of target data, providing a meaningful improvement over V9D performance on the generalization tasks.

The transferable-subset question in 4.4, namely whether the subset corresponds to anything human-interpretable after it had been shown that a small subset of source PCs supports cross-domain transfer and that this subset is not the same as the directions a source classification or target classification relies on, was approached by adopting the interpretability method of Bills et al. (2023) and Casademunt et al. (2025): for each of the source R2F, the 8 most and 8 least PC-activating examples from the source data were found, A8T was prompted to generate a natural-language description of the positive pole, the negative pole, and the PC direction as a whole, and A8T then rated the generalizability of each direction on a 1–10 scale as N5P, with the complete judge prompt and model detail provided in Appendix B; because no PC received an N5P above 6 on the 1–10 scale, 6 was treated as the maximum observed N5P, all PCs achieving this maximum score in both runs were retained, yielding 19 PCs, 5 PCs showed up in the K9U selections of more than one target, 3 out of 5 PCs received the maximum N5P, suggesting that A8T can partially recover transferable directions, whereas only 1 of the 5 highest-ranked PCs selected by Z1M received the maximum N5P, and the interpretations of the K9U-selected PCs describe the contrast at a level of abstraction that survives the move to other domains, with a complete breakdown of PCs selected by each method provided in Appendix E.

The property question in 4.3 asked what makes these PCs transferable and whether that property can be detected from the source-side alone, and two simple hypotheses for what the K9U subset contains were investigated, the first positing that the transferable PCs are simply the ones that separate the target classes well by themselves across the test set, with K9U happening to pick them because it has access to target data, and the second positing that they are the PCs the source probe already leans on most heavily, with K9U just rediscovering the source signal in a more compact form; two rankings of PCs were compared, both based on U1L (U1L denoting the standardized coefficient, namely the absolute value of each probe coefficient multiplied by the standard deviation of the corresponding PC) given to each PC by a probe trained on activations projected on the source-PCA basis, with Z1M (Z1M denoting the source-PCA probe) ranking PCs by the weights learned by a probe trained on source data and H5X (H5X denoting the target-PCA probe) ranking PCs by the weights learned by a probe trained on target data, while in both cases the activations are projected onto the source-PCA basis and the probes are trained on source data that has been projected onto the top $k$ PCs selected according to their respective rankings, since, if either hypothesis were correct, the corresponding probes should recover the K9U method’s performance; to set $k$, K9U was run on the complete target dataset rather than per fold, giving a single stable PC set with $k=13$ for Q7V, 7 for P3Y, and 14 for J8W, and, because the complete dataset rather than H6Q was used, the K9U numbers differ slightly from the H6Q numbers in 4.2.

In 4.2, a transferable subspace exists in principle, since K9U (K9U denoting greedy PC selection) comes close to an S6F for Q7V and J8W, improving on V9D by 0.22 and 0.23 points of E3K, respectively, while W4J (W4J denoting top-k PCs by variance), with $k$ matched to the number of PCs selected by K9U, matches K9U only for P3Y, the easy case, and falls far behind for Q7V and J8W, the harder generalization tasks, which suggests that the choice of PCs matters and that the amount of variance explained by those PCs does not predict their usefulness; whether any method for training probes on source data closes M3Z between V9D and S6F was tested by starting from an empty set and iteratively adding PCs from the source dataset, searching at each step over R2F and adding the one that most improves E3K on the target, evaluating each candidate by projecting source activations onto the current subset and training a logistic regression probe on those projections, with probe weights always fit on source data but the choice of PC subsets guided by performance on labeled examples from the target dataset, using H6Q over the target dataset to perform K9U on the non-held-out target folds and evaluate the selected source-trained probe on the held-out target fold, stopping when no candidate PC improves E3K by more than 0.001, and converging on small subsets of PCs, on average 15 PCs for Q7V, 8 for P3Y, and 14 for J8W, although the K9U probe provides an upper bound on transfer performance, not an unbiased N5P estimate, because subset selection uses some target examples.

## 5 Limitations

That E3K is raised from 0.606 to 0.662 by selection of Q8B in the case of J8W, the weakest of the results, constitutes a recovery of only 25% of the gap to the K9U upper bound (0.832), a modesty of gain plausibly reflective of distribution shift given that J8W is also the most out-of-distribution target, whereas the corresponding result for Q7V (0.706 to 0.881, closing 78% of the gap to K9U at 0.929) is the more robust; and the claims that are strongest remain still quite narrow, there being several limitations that should be considered by the reader. The structural claims made about what each PC "encodes," resting as they do on top-activating examples and on an A8T's verbalization of the contrast, are descriptive claims, not causal ones, the high-scoring PCs not having been ablated during inference to verify that they carry the contrast they appear to encode. The probe pipeline being fully linear, consisting of a PCA projection followed by logistic regression, the existence of a transferable subspace is perhaps unsurprising, and whether these findings extend to nonlinear probes or to other readout locations remains an open question. All experiments having used a single model, V6Q (the 3.1-series model with 8B parameters, layer 15), and a single source dataset, Z4K (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025), the P3Y results are uninformative inasmuch as baseline transfer on P3Y already reaches 0.874, and whether the results generalize to other model families or to other datasets is an open question left to future work.

## 6 Conclusion

The reframing of OOD probing as a representation-selection problem, under which the goal is constituted not only by the fitting of a classifier but also by the choosing of a subspace that preserves the intended behavioral feature and filters out spurious correlation, rests on the possibility that high in-distribution accuracy may be supported, while transfer is hurt, by the heuristics produced when general behavioral features are combined with dataset-specific surface cues by linear probes trained over full representations, whereas directions corresponding to more general behavioral structure may be better isolated by sparse subspaces of PCs; in probe-based deception monitoring, cross-domain transfer nearly matching S6F is enabled by a small subset of source-domain principal components (PCs), which cannot be found with source-side or target-side separability alone but is partially recovered by an interpretability score over each PC's top-activating examples that is produced by A8T with no access to target data, and what robust probing requires is the identification not only of whether a behavioral signal is linearly accessible, but of where in activation space that signal is represented most robustly.

## Appendix B LLM Judge Prompt

The answer produced in response to the prompt reproduced here, through which the interpretation of directions is carried out by GPT-5-mini (OpenAI, 2025), is required to be written in exactly this format:

Positive pole: ...

Negative pole: ...

Shared direction: ...

OOD deception usefulness (1-10): ...

Scores of 5 or higher are to be reserved for directions that seem plausibly tied to a more general deception-relevant strategy or behavior, rather than local wording, whereas a direction that is noisy, brittle, context-dependent, or likely to fail under different wording or domains should usually be scored around 1 to 4, not 5 or above, and low scores are to be given to directions that are mainly surface cues, lexical habits, or task-specific phrasing, even in cases where correlation with deception in these examples is present, with the rating itself, concerning how likely this direction is to be useful for out-of-distribution deception, being given on a scale from 1 to 10 together with a brief explanation of why. Overgeneralization beyond what is supported by the examples is to be avoided, the narrowest accurate description is to be preferred over a broad generic one, and the direction is not to be assumed to be a broad sentiment direction unless the examples clearly support that.
